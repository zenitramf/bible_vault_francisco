# James — Matthew Henry Concise Commentary

# Contents

* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/james/chapter-1|James 1 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: How to apply to God under troubles, and how to behave in prosperous and in adverse circumstances. (1–11). To look upon all evil as proceeding from ourselves, and all good from God. (12–18). The duty of watching against a rash temper, and of receiving the word of God with meekness. (19–21). And of living according thereto. (22–25). The difference between vain pretences and real religion. (26, 27).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/james/chapter-2|James 2 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: All professions of faith are vain, if not producing love and justice to others. (1–13). The necessity of good works to prove the sincerity of faith, which otherwise will be of no more advantage than the faith of devils. (14–26).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/james/chapter-3|James 3 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: Cautions against proud behaviour, and the mischief of an unruly tongue. (1–12). The excellence of heavenly wisdom, in opposition to that which is worldly. (13–18).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/james/chapter-4|James 4 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: Here are cautions against corrupt affections, and love of this world, which is enmity to God. (1–10). Exhortations to undertake no affairs of life, without constant regard to the will and providence of God. (11–17).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/james/chapter-5|James 5 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: The judgments of God denounced against rich unbelievers. (1–6). Exhortation to patience and meekness under tribulations. (7–11). Cautions against rash swearing Prayer recommended in afflictive and prosperous circumstances, Christians to confess their faults to each other. (12–18). The happiness of being the means of the conversion of a sinner. (19, 20).
