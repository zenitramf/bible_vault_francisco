# 1 Corinthians — Matthew Henry Concise Commentary

# Contents

* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-1|1 Corinthians 1 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: A salutation and thanksgiving. (1–9). Exhortation to brotherly love, and reproof for divisions. (10–16). The doctrine of a crucified Saviour, as advancing the glory of God, (17–25). and humbling the creature before him. (26–31).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-10|1 Corinthians 10 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: The great privileges, and yet terrible overthrow of the Israelites in the wilderness. (1–5). Cautions against all idolatrous, and other sinful practices. (6–14). The partaking in idolatry cannot exist with having communion with Christ. (15–22). All we do to be to the glory of God, and without offence to the consciences of others. (23–33).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-11|1 Corinthians 11 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: The apostle, after an exhortation to follow him, (1). corrects some abuses. (2–16). Also contentions, divisions, and disorderly celebrations of the Lord's supper. (17–22). He reminds them of the nature and design of its institution. (23–26). And directs how to attend upon it in a due manner. (27–34).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-12|1 Corinthians 12 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: The variety of use of spiritual gifts are shown. (1–11). In the human body every member has its place and use. (12–26). This is applied to the church of Christ. (27–30). And there is something more excellent than spiritual gifts. (31).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-13|1 Corinthians 13 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: The necessity and advantage of the grace of love. (1–3). Its excellency represented by its properties and effects; (4–7). and by its abiding, and its superiority. (8–13).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-14|1 Corinthians 14 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: Prophecy preferred to the gift of tongues. (1–5). The unprofitableness of speaking in unknown languages. (6–14). Exhortations to worship that can be understood. (15–25). Disorders from vain display of gifts; (26–33). and from women speaking in the church. (34–40).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-15|1 Corinthians 15 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: The apostle proves the resurrection of Christ from the dead. (1–11). Those answered who deny the resurrection of the body. (12–19). The resurrection of believers to eternal life. (20–34). Objections against it answered. (35–50). The mystery of the change that will be made on those living at Christ's second coming. (51–54). The believer's triumph over death and the grave, An exhortation to diligence. (55–58).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-16|1 Corinthians 16 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: A collection for the poor at Jerusalem. (1–9). Timothy and Apollos commended. (10–12). Exhortation to watchfulness in faith and love. (13–18). Christian salutations. (19–24).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-2|1 Corinthians 2 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: The plain manner in which the apostle preached Christ crucified. (1–5). The wisdom contained in this doctrine. (6–9). It cannot be duly known but by the Holy Spirit. (10–16).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-3|1 Corinthians 3 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: The Corinthians reproved for their contentions. (1–4). The true servants of Christ can do nothing without him. (5–9). He is the only foundation, and every one should take heed what he builds thereon. (10–15). The churches of Christ ought to be kept pure, and to be humble. (16, 17). And they should not glory in men, because ministers and all things else are theirs through Christ. (18–23).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-4|1 Corinthians 4 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: The true character of gospel ministers. (1–6). Cautions against despising the apostle. (7–13). He claims their regard as their spiritual father in Christ, and shows his concern for them. (14–21).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-5|1 Corinthians 5 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: The apostle blames the Corinthians for connivance at an incestuous person; (1–8). and directs their behaviour towards those guilty of scandalous crimes. (9–13).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-6|1 Corinthians 6 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: Cautions against going to law in heathen courts. (1–8). Sins which, if lived and died in, shut out from the kingdom of God. (9–11). Our bodies, which are the members of Christ, and temples of the Holy Ghost, must not be defiled. (12–20).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-7|1 Corinthians 7 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: The apostle answers several questions about marriage. (1–9). Married Christians should not seek to part from their unbelieving consorts. (10–16). Persons, in any fixed station, should usually abide in that. (17–24). It was most desirable, on account of the then perilous days, for people to sit loose to this world. (25–35). Great prudence be used in marriage; it should be only in the Lord. (36–40).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-8|1 Corinthians 8 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: The danger of having a high conceit of knowledge. (1–6). The mischief of offending weak brethren. (7–13).
* [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-9|1 Corinthians 9 | Read the Matthew Henry Concise Bible Commentary Online]] - In this chapter: The apostle shows his authority, and asserts his right to be maintained. (1–14). He waved this part of his Christian liberty, for the good of others. (15–23). He did all this, with care and diligence, in view of an unfading crown. (24–27).
