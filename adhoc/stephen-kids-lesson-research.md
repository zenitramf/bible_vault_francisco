> **What this file is.** Findings pulled from the Bible Vault, not a finished
> lesson plan. Scripture is KJV (per the standing rule). Every quote is
> verbatim from a vault source, and every claim is linked to its source path
> so it can be verified before going into a lesson.
>
> **Scope.** Stephen's life and ministry as recorded in Acts 6–7, drawn from
> Matthew Henry Concise and Matthew Henry Complete (the two vault corpora
> that cover this material). All Scripture references are KJV.
>
> **Series context.** This is the bridge lesson from the "Little-Known
> Heroes" series into the new "Missionaries" series (biblical + last ~100
> years). Treat Stephen as the first Christian missionary-martyr — a useful
> pattern for the next lessons without forcing the connection here.

## 2. The Bible Story in Order (KJV text + vault commentary)

### 2.1 Why Stephen was chosen (Acts 6:1–7)

> "And in those days, when the number of the disciples was multiplied, there
> arose a murmuring of the Grecians against the Hebrews, because their widows
> were neglected in the daily ministration." — Ac 6:1

> "Wherefore, brethren, look ye out among you seven men of honest report, full
> of the Holy Ghost and wisdom, whom we may appoint over this business." —
> Ac 6:3

> "And the saying pleased the whole multitude: and they chose **Stephen, a
> man full of faith and of the Holy Ghost**, and Philip, and Prochorus, and
> Nicanor, and Timon, and Parmenas, and Nicolas a proselyte of Antioch: Whom
> they set before the apostles: and when they had prayed, they laid their
> hands on them." — Ac 6:5–6

> "And the word of God increased; and the number of the disciples multiplied
> in Jerusalem greatly; and a great company of the priests were obedient to
> the faith." — Ac 6:7

**Henry on this:** "The persons chosen to serve tables must be duly
qualified. They must be filled with gifts and graces of the Holy Ghost,
necessary to rightly managing this trust; men of truth, and hating
covetousness." [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/acts/chapter-6#Verses 1–7|MH Concise, Acts 6]]

**Kid-friendly takeaway:** Before Stephen preached, he served. He was first
entrusted with small, faithful work (helping widows) and proved trustworthy.

### 2.2 Stephen's public ministry and arrest (Acts 6:8–15)

> "And Stephen, full of faith and power, did great wonders and miracles among
> the people." — Ac 6:8

> "And they were not able to resist the wisdom and the spirit by which he
> spake." — Ac 6:10

> "Then they suborned men, which said, We have heard him speak blasphemous
> words against Moses, and against God." — Ac 6:11

> "And set up false witnesses, which said, This man ceaseth not to speak
> blasphemous words against this holy place, and the law: For we have heard
> him say, that this Jesus of Nazareth shall destroy this place, and shall
> change the customs which Moses delivered us." — Ac 6:13–14

> "And all that sat in the council, looking stedfastly on him, saw his face
> as it had been the face of an angel." — Ac 6:15

**Henry on Stephen's miracles:** "He was full of faith and power … Those
that are full of faith are full of power, because by faith the power of God
is engaged for us." [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/acts/chapter-6#Stephen's Address|MH Complete, Acts 6]]

**Henry on his face shining:** "Such an undisturbed serenity, such an
undaunted courage, and such an unaccountable mixture of mildness and
majesty, there was in his countenance, that every one said he looked like
an angel." [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/acts/chapter-6#Stephen's Address|MH Complete, Acts 6]]

**Henry on the false witnesses:** "It is next to a miracle of providence,
that no greater number of religious persons have been murdered in the world,
by the way of perjury and pretence of law, when so many thousands hate them,
who make no conscience of false oaths." [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/acts/chapter-6#Verses 8–15|MH Concise, Acts 6]]

**Kid-friendly takeaway:** People couldn't argue against Stephen, so they
lied about him. The bad guys didn't play fair.

### 2.3 Stephen's speech before the council (Acts 7:1–50)

Stephen retells the Old Testament story — Abraham, Joseph, Moses, the
tabernacle, Solomon's temple. He is making one running point: **God has
never been locked up in one holy place or one set of customs.**

> "Howbeit the most High dwelleth not in temples made with hands; as saith
> the prophet, Heaven is my throne, and earth is my footstool: what house
> will ye build me? saith the Lord: or what is the place of my rest? Hath
> not my hand made all these things?" — Ac 7:48–50

**Henry on the strategy of the speech:** "His great intention … is to show
that it was no blasphemy against God … to say that the temple should be
destroyed and the customs of the ceremonial law changed." [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/acts/chapter-7|MH Complete, Acts 7]]

**Henry on Stephen's Bible-saturated mind:** "In this discourse he appears
to be a man ready and mighty in the scriptures … those that are full of the
Holy Ghost will be full of the scripture, as Stephen was." [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/acts/chapter-7|MH Complete, Acts 7]]

**Henry on the meaning of the burning bush:** "God, manifested in the
flesh, was as the flame of fire manifested in the bush." [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/acts/chapter-7#Verses 30–41|MH Concise, Acts 7]]

**Kid-friendly takeaway:** Stephen knew God's big story backwards and
forwards. He used it to explain who Jesus is.

### 2.4 The rebuke and the accusation (Acts 7:51–53)

> "Ye stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, ye do always resist
> the Holy Ghost: as your fathers did, so do ye. Which of the prophets have
> not your fathers persecuted? and they have slain them which showed before
> of the coming of the Just One; of whom ye have been now the betrayers and
> murderers: Who have received the law by the disposition of angels, and have
> not kept it." — Ac 7:51–53

**Henry on what cut them to the heart:** "Rejecters of the gospel and
opposers of it are really tormentors to themselves. Enmity to God is a
heart-cutting thing; faith and love are heart-healing." [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/acts/chapter-7#Stephen's Address|MH Complete, Acts 7]]

**Kid-friendly takeaway:** Stephen told the truth to the very people who
could hurt him most. That's courage.

### 2.5 Stephen's death and prayer (Acts 7:54–60)

> "When they heard these things, they were cut to the heart, and they gnashed
> on him with their teeth." — Ac 7:54

> "But he, being full of the Holy Ghost, looked up stedfastly into heaven,
> and saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing on the right hand of God, And
> said, Behold, I see the heavens opened, and the Son of man standing on the
> right hand of God." — Ac 7:55–56

> "And they stoned Stephen, calling upon God, and saying, Lord Jesus, receive
> my spirit. And he kneeled down, and cried with a loud voice, Lord, lay not
> this sin to their charge. And when he had said this, he fell asleep." —
> Ac 7:59–60

**Henry on Jesus standing (not sitting):** "He is usually said to sit there;
but Stephen sees him standing there, as one more than ordinarily concerned
at present for his suffering servant; he stood up as a judge to plead his
cause against his persecutors." [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/acts/chapter-7#Stephen's Martyrdom; Stephen's Dying Prayer|MH Complete, Acts 7]]

**Henry on Stephen's first prayer:** "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit … Our
Lord Jesus is God, to whom we are to seek, and in whom we are to confide and
comfort ourselves living and dying." [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/acts/chapter-7#Stephen's Martyrdom; Stephen's Dying Prayer|MH Complete, Acts 7]]

**Henry on Stephen's prayer for his killers:** "He followed the example of
his dying Master, who prayed thus for his persecutors, 'Father, forgive
them'; and set an example to all following sufferers in the cause of Christ
thus to pray for those that persecute them." [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/acts/chapter-7#Stephen's Martyrdom; Stephen's Dying Prayer|MH Complete, Acts 7]]

**Henry on "fell asleep":** "Death is but a sleep to good people … Stephen
died as much in a hurry as ever any man did, and yet, when he died, he fell
asleep." [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/acts/chapter-7#Verses 54–60|MH Concise, Acts 7]]

**Kid-friendly takeaway:** In the worst moment of his life, Stephen saw
Jesus standing for him, and prayed for the very people killing him.

## 5. Themes Useful for a Kids' Lesson

These came straight out of the Henry material — already kid-friendly in
shape.

- **Faithful in the little things first.** Stephen served tables before he
  preached. ([MH Concise, Acts 6])
- **God uses ordinary people who are full of the Spirit.** He wasn't an
  apostle; he was a servant. ([MH Complete, Acts 6])
- **When people can't argue with you, they may lie about you.** False
  witnesses were brought against Stephen. ([MH Concise, Acts 6])
- **Know your Bible.** Stephen could tell the whole Old Testament story
  from memory. ([MH Complete, Acts 7])
- **Courage is doing the right thing when it costs you.** Stephen rebuked
  the very men who could kill him. ([MH Complete, Acts 7])
- **Jesus stands up for His people.** Usually He is said to be seated at
  the Father's right hand; here He is **standing** — for Stephen.
  ([MH Complete, Acts 7])
- **Forgive your enemies.** Stephen prayed for the men stoning him.
  ([MH Complete, Acts 7])
- **Death is a sleep for the believer.** "He fell asleep." ([MH Concise,
  Acts 7])
- **A young man's faith can shape a whole church.** The Saul who held the
  coats at Stephen's death later became Paul, the apostle to the Gentiles.
  ([MH Complete, Acts 7])

## 6. Hooks and Illustrations Already in the Commentary

These are the images Henry gives that translate easily to a kids' class.

- **The burning bush that was not consumed.** A picture God gave to show
  Israel in Egypt — and a picture of Christ taking on human flesh without
  being destroyed by it. ([MH Concise, Acts 7 — "He appeared to Moses in a
  flame of fire, yet the bush was not consumed"])
- **The face like an angel.** The whole council saw a strange brightness
  on Stephen's face when he stood trial. ([MH Complete, Acts 6])
- **The coat-holder.** A young man named Saul was there, "a pleased
  spectator of this tragedy" — and later became the church's great
  missionary. ([MH Complete, Acts 7])
- **Two short prayers in dying moments.** Stephen prayed one prayer for
  himself and one prayer for his enemies — and died in the middle of the
  second one. ([MH Complete, Acts 7])

## 7. What the Vault Does *Not* Yet Have (Honest Gaps)

These are questions the kids may ask that the current vault sources do not
settle. Flag them in the lesson rather than guess.

- **Stephen's family and background.** The text does not say. We know he
  was a Hellenist Jew (Greek-speaking), but not whether he was married,
  had children, or came from a particular town.
- **How old Stephen was.** Not stated. Henry guesses he was younger than
  the apostles ("late converts, in comparison of the apostles"), but the
  Bible does not give his age.
- **What happened to the Gospel-spreading after Stephen's death.** The
  vault has Acts 8 and onward as separate pages, but the synthesis here
  only summarizes the martyrdom itself. The connection "Stephen's death
  → scattering of the church → Philip's ministry in Samaria" is in the
  biblical text (Ac 8:1–4) but is not in the [[wiki/passages/Acts 8|Acts
  8]] synthesis page yet.
- **The "Jesus standing" detail in any other source.** Henry's note is the
  vault's only commentary on this specific phrase. The vault's [[wiki/passages/Acts 7|Acts
  7]] page only references Henry; no Spurgeon or other commentaries on
  this point are present in the current corpus.

If the lesson needs more, the next move would be to add a [[wiki/people/Stephen|Stephen]]
person page and a fuller Acts 6/7 synthesis, but that's a separate
ingest — not required for the kids' class.

## 9. Quick "If I Only Had Five Minutes" Summary

> Stephen was one of seven men chosen to help serve the early church. He
> was full of faith and the Holy Spirit. He did miracles, preached Jesus
> boldly, and when people lied about him and put him on trial, he told
> the truth — even the hard parts. The council dragged him out of the
> city and stoned him. As the stones fell, he saw Jesus standing in
> heaven (not sitting — standing, as if to help him). He prayed two
> prayers: "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit," and "Lord, do not hold this
> sin against them." Then he fell asleep. He was the first Christian to
> die for telling people about Jesus. A young man named Saul watched it
> happen — and that same Saul later became Paul, the great missionary.
