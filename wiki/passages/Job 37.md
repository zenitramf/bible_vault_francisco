# Job 37

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Job 37: Elihu observes the power of God. (1–13). Job required to explain the works of nature. (14–20). God is great, and is to be feared. (21–24).

## Core claims

- The changes of the weather are the subject of a great deal of our thoughts and common talk; but how seldom do we think and speak of these things, as Elihu, with a regard to God, the director of them! We must notice the glory of God, not only in the thunder and lightning, but in the more common and less awful changes… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-37#Verses 1–13|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 37]]
- Due thoughts of the works of God will help to reconcile us to all his providences. As God has a powerful, freezing north wind, so he has a thawing, composing south wind: the Spirit is compared to both, because he both convinces and comforts, So 4:16. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-37#Verses 14–20|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 37]]
- Elihu concludes his discourse with some great sayings concerning the glory of God. Light always is, but is not always to be seen. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-37#Verses 21–24|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 37]]
- Elihu here goes on to extol the wonderful power of God in the meteors and all the changes of the weather: if, in those changes, we submit to the will of God, take the weather as it is and make the best of it, why… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-3/job/chapter-37|Matthew Henry Complete on Job 37]]
