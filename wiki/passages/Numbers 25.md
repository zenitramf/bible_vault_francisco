# Numbers 25

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Numbers 25: The Israelites enticed by the daughters of Moab and Midian. (1–5). Phinehas puts Zimri and Cozbi to death. (6–15). The Midianites to be punished. (16–18).

## Core claims

- The friendship of the wicked is more dangerous than their enmity; for none can prevail against God's people if they are not overcome by their inbred lusts; nor can any enchantment hurt them, but the enticements of worldly interests and pleasures. Here is the sin of Israel, to which they are enticed by the daughters… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/numbers/chapter-25#Verses 1–5|Matthew Henry Concise on Numbers 25]]
- Phinehas, in the courage of zeal and faith, executed vengeance on Zimri and Cozbi. This act can never be an example for private revenge, or religious persecution, or for irregular public vengeance. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/numbers/chapter-25#Verses 6–15|Matthew Henry Concise on Numbers 25]]
- We read not that any Midianites died of the plague; God punished them with the sword of an enemy, not with the rod of a father. We must set ourselves against whatever is an occasion of sin to us, Mt 5:29, 30. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/numbers/chapter-25#Verses 16–18|Matthew Henry Concise on Numbers 25]]
- Israel, having escaped the curse of Balaam, here sustains a great deal of damage and reproach by the counsel of Balaam, who, it seems, before he left Balak, put him into a more effectual way than that which Balak… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-1/numbers/chapter-25|Matthew Henry Complete on Numbers 25]]
