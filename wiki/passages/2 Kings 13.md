# 2 Kings 13

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Kings 13: Reign of Jehoahaz. (1–9). Jehoash, king of Israel, Elisha dying. (10–19). Elisha's death, The victories of Jehoash. (20–25).

## Core claims

- It was the ancient honour of Israel that they were a praying people. Jehoahaz, their king, in his distress, besought the Lord; applied himself for help, but not to the calves; what help could they give him? [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-kings/chapter-13#Verses 1–9|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Kings 13]]
- Jehoash, the king, came to Elisha, to receive his dying counsel and blessing. It may turn much to our spiritual advantage, to attend the sick-beds and death-beds of good men, that we may be encouraged in religion by the living comforts they have from it in a dying hour. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-kings/chapter-13#Verses 10–19|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Kings 13]]
- God has many ways to chastise a provoking people. Trouble comes sometimes from that point whence we least feared it. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-kings/chapter-13#Verses 20–25|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Kings 13]]

## Agreements and tensions
