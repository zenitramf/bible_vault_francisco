# 2 Corinthians 1

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Corinthians 1: The apostle blesses God for comfort in, and deliverance out of troubles. (1–11). He professes his own and his fellow-labourers' integrity. (12–14). Gives reasons for his not coming to them. (15–24). McGee frames the chapter under God’s comfort for life’s plans: comfort as sustaining help (Comforter vocabulary), comfort received to comfort others, and the Spirit who confirms, anoints, seals, and is the earnest.

## Core claims

- We are encouraged to come boldly to the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need. The Lord is able to give peace to the troubled conscience, and to calm the raging passions of the soul. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-corinthians/chapter-1#Verses 1–11|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Corinthians 1]]
- Though, as a sinner, the apostle could only rejoice and glory in Christ Jesus, yet, as a believer, he might rejoice and glory in being really what he professed. Conscience witnesses concerning the steady course and tenor of the life. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-corinthians/chapter-1#Verses 12–14|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Corinthians 1]]
- The apostle clears himself from the charge of levity and inconstancy, in not coming to Corinth. Good men should be careful to keep the reputation of sincerity and constancy; they should not resolve, but on careful thought; and they will not change unless for weighty reasons. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-corinthians/chapter-1#Verses 15–24|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Corinthians 1]]
- After the introduction (2Co 1:1,2) the apostle begins with the narrative of his troubles and God's goodness, which he had met with in Asia, by way of thanksgiving to God (2Co 1:3-6), and for the edification of the… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/2-corinthians/chapter-1|Matthew Henry Complete on 2 Corinthians 1]]
- McGee: “Comfort” and “consolation” nine times in five verses—not sentimental but sustaining help, the same word used for the Holy Spirit the Comforter. Paul experienced comfort through a crisis in Ephesus (probably sickness unto death, v. 9) so he could comfort others. Plan to come to Corinth changed (vv. 15–20). Secondary theme: Spirit confirms, anoints, seals, and is the earnest/pledge (vv. 21–22); Father true, Son absolute, Spirit dwelling within. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on 2 Corinthians]] [[wiki/source-notes/McGee Notes and Outlines on 2 Corinthians|McGee source-note]]

## Agreements and tensions

Complete commentary deepens Concise; McGee adds Comforter word-link, comfort-to-comfort-others principle, and Spirit seal/earnest tetrad. “Probably sickness unto death” is McGee’s inference from v. 9—hold beside Henry’s broader affliction language.
