# Job 21

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Job 21: Job entreats attention. (1–6). The prosperity of the wicked. (7–16). The dealings of God's providence. (17–26). The judgement of the wicked is in the world to come. (27–34).

## Core claims

- Job comes closer to the question in dispute. This was, Whether outward prosperity is a mark of the true church, and the true members of it, so that ruin of a man's prosperity proves him a hypocrite? [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-21#Verses 1–6|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 21]]
- Job had described the prosperity of wicked people; in these verses he opposes this to what his friends had maintained about their certain ruin in this life. He reconciles this to the holiness and justice of God. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-21#Verses 17–26|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 21]]
- Job opposes the opinion of his friends, That the wicked are sure to fall into visible and remarkable ruin, and none but the wicked; upon which principle they condemned Job as wicked. Turn to whom you will, you will find that the punishment of sinners is designed more for the other world than for this, Jude 1:14, 15. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-21#Verses 27–34|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 21]]
- This is Job's reply to Zophar's discourse, in which he complains less of his own miseries than he had done in his former discourses (finding that his friends were not moved by his complaints to pity him in the least),… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-3/job/chapter-21|Matthew Henry Complete on Job 21]]
