# Amos 3

## Summary

God’s charge against the whole house of Israel: chosen privilege heightens responsibility. Henry stresses that distinguishing favors do not exempt from punishment and that communion requires peace with God. McGee makes 3:3 the book’s final test—“Can two walk together, except they be agreed?”—and titles the chapter “privilege creates responsibility; the higher the blessing, the greater the punishment.”

## Core claims

- The distinguishing favours of God to us, if they do not restrain from sin, shall not exempt from punishment. They could not expect communion with God, unless they first sought peace with him. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/amos/chapter-3#Verses 1–8|Matthew Henry Concise on Amos 3]]
- That power which is an instrument of unrighteousness, will justly be brought down and broken. What is got and kept wrongfully, will not be kept long. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/amos/chapter-3#Verses 9–15|Matthew Henry Concise on Amos 3]]
- A stupid, senseless, heedless people, are, in this chapter, called upon to take notice, I. Of the judgments of God denounced against them and the warnings he gave them of those judgments, and to be hereby awakened out… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-4/amos/chapter-3|Matthew Henry Complete on Amos 3]]
- McGee: Israel occupied a unique relationship to God and was chosen for a definite purpose; privilege created greater responsibility than any other nation had (3:1–2). Verse 3 is a great principle by which God must judge all nations and individuals—Israel knew God’s way, disagreed and departed, and by this will be judged. Judgment on the entire nation will be severe; the northern kingdom first (vv. 14–15). [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/Amos-Obadiah.pdf|McGee Notes & Outlines on Amos 3]]

## Agreements and tensions

Complete deepens Concise (Phase 4.1). McGee and Henry agree that election heightens accountability; McGee’s “walk together… agreed” as universal judgment principle is the outline’s thematic center for the whole book.
