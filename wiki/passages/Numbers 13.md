# Numbers 13

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Numbers 13: Twelve men sent to search the land of Canaan, Their instructions. (1–20). Their proceedings. (21–25). Their account of the land. (26–33).

## Core claims

- A memorable and melancholy history is related in this and the following chapter, of the turning back of Israel from the borders of Canaan, and the sentencing them to wander and perish in the wilderness, for their unbelief and murmuring. It appears, De 1:22, that the motion to search out the land came from the people. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/numbers/chapter-13#Verses 1–20|Matthew Henry Concise on Numbers 13]]
- The searchers of the land brought a bunch of grapes with them, and other fruits, as proofs of the goodness of the country; which was to Israel both the earnest and the specimen of all the fruits of Canaan. Such are the present comforts we have in communion with God, foretastes of the fulness of joy we expect in the… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/numbers/chapter-13#Verses 21–25|Matthew Henry Concise on Numbers 13]]
- We may wonder that the people of Israel staid forty days for the return of their spies, when they were ready to enter Canaan, under all the assurances of success they could have from the Divine power, and the miracles that had hitherto attended them. But they distrusted God's power and promise. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/numbers/chapter-13#Verses 26–33|Matthew Henry Concise on Numbers 13]]
