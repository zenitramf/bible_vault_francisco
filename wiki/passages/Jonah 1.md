# Jonah 1

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Jonah 1: Jonah, sent to Nineveh, flees to Tarshish. (1–3). He is stayed by a tempest. (4–7). His discourse with the mariners. (8–12). He is cast into the sea, and miraculously preserved. (13–17).

## Core claims

- It is sad to think how much sin is committed in great cities. Their wickedness, as that of Nineveh, is a bold and open affront to God. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/jonah/chapter-1#Verses 1–3|Matthew Henry Concise on Jonah 1]]
- Jonah gave an account of his religion, for that was his business. We may hope that he told with sorrow and shame, justifying God, condemning himself, and explaining to the mariners what a great God Jehovah is. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/jonah/chapter-1#Verses 8–12|Matthew Henry Concise on Jonah 1]]
- The mariners rowed against wind and tide, the wind of God's displeasure, the tide of his counsel; but it is in vain to think of saving ourselves any other way than by destroying our sins. Even natural conscience cannot but dread blood-guiltiness. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/jonah/chapter-1#Verses 13–17|Matthew Henry Concise on Jonah 1]]
