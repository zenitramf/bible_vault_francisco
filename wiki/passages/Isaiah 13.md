# Isaiah 13

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 13: The armies of God's wrath. (1-5). The conquest of Babylon. (6-18). Its final desolation. (19-22).

## Core claims

- The threatenings of God's word press heavily upon the wicked, and are a sore burden, too heavy for them to bear. The persons brought together to lay Babylon waste, are called God's sanctified or appointed ones; designed for this service, and made able to do it. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-13#Verses 1-5|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 13]]
- We have here the terrible desolation of Babylon by the Medes and Persians. Those who in the day of their peace were proud, and haughty, and terrible, are quite dispirited when trouble comes. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-13#Verses 6-18|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 13]]
- Babylon was a noble city; yet it should be wholly destroyed. None shall dwell there. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-13#Verses 19-22|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 13]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
