# Romans 12

## Summary

Romans 12 turns from doctrine to duty: by the mercies of God, believers present their bodies a living sacrifice, refuse conformity to the world, and are transformed by renewing of the mind. They serve as one body with diverse gifts, practice sincere love, and overcome evil with good.

## Core claims

- By the mercies of God, believers present their bodies a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable; they must not be conformed to this world but transformed by renewing of the mind. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-12#Verses 1, 2|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 12]]
- Pride is natural and must be armed against; all saints make up one body in Christ, the Head and common center of unity, with differing gifts to be used faithfully. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-12#Verses 3–8|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 12]]
- Christian love must be sincere; believers depend on divine grace to abhor evil and cleave to what is good. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-12#Verses 9–16|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 12]]
- Those who embrace religion must expect enemies; they are not to be overcome by evil but to overcome evil with good. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-12#Verses 17–21|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 12]]
- The apostle, having at large cleared and confirmed the prime fundamental doctrines of Christianity, comes in the next place to press the principal duties.
