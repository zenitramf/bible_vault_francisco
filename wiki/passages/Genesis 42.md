# Genesis 42

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 42: Jacob sends ten sons to buy corn. (1–6). Joseph's treatment of his brethren. (7–20). Their remorse, Simeon detained. (21–24). The rest return with corn. (25–28). Jacob refuses to send Benjamin to Egypt. (29–38).

## Core claims

- Jacob saw the corn his neighbours had bought in Egypt, and brought home. It is a spur to exertion to see others supplied. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/genesis/chapter-42#Verses 1–6|Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 42]]
- The office of conscience is to bring to mind things long since said and done. When the guilt of this sin of Joseph's brethren was fresh, they made light of it, and sat down to eat bread; but now, long afterward, their consciences accused them of it. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/genesis/chapter-42#Verses 21–24|Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 42]]
- Here is the report Jacob's sons made to their father. It troubled the good man. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/genesis/chapter-42#Verses 29–38|Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 42]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
