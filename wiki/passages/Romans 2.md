# Romans 2

## Summary

Henry’s concise reading of Romans 2 dismantles Jewish (and any professing) confidence in privilege. God’s judgment is according to real character; repentance is a change of mind that hates former sin; and outward forms—including circumcision as type of mere external religion—profit nothing without regenerating grace that seeks God’s righteousness by faith.

## Core claims

- God’s judgment is according to real character; in every willful sin there is contempt of God’s goodness, and impenitent hearts treasure up wrath. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-2#Verses 1–16|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 2]]
- True repentance includes hatred of former sinfulness from a changed mind that chooses good and refuses evil; conversion is needed by every human being. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-2#Verses 1–16|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 2]]
- Even Gentiles without the written law have conscience as a witness that acquits or condemns as they keep or break natural dictates. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-2#Verses 1–16|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 2]]
- Proud boasting in God and outward profession is the root of hypocrisy; unholy lives of professors dishonor God and cause his name to be blasphemed. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-2#Verses 17–24|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 2]]
- No forms, ordinances, or notions profit without regenerating grace that leads to seeking an interest in the righteousness of God by faith; outward-only religion is not true belonging. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-2#Verses 25–29|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 2]]
