# 1 Peter 3

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Peter 3: The duties of wives and husbands. (1–7). Christians exhorted to agree. (8–13). And encouraged to patience under persecutions for righteousness' sake, considering that Christ suffered patiently. (14–22). McGee places the chapter under suffering and Christ’s suffering producing **Christian conduct**—home (voluntary wifely subjection that may win an unbelieving husband; husbands honoring wives so prayers are not hindered), church (sympathy, no retaliation, reason for hope), and the difficult 3:18–22 unit (Christ’s once-for-all suffering; spirits in prison tied to Noah’s day preaching; baptism as identification with the risen Christ).

## Core claims

- The wife must discharge her duty to her own husband, though he obey not the word. We daily see how narrowly evil men watch the ways and lives of professors of religion. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-peter/chapter-3#Verses 1–7|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Peter 3]]
- Though Christians cannot always be exactly of the same mind, yet they should have compassion one of another, and love as brethren. If any man desires to live comfortably on earth, or to possess eternal life in heaven, he must bridle his tongue from wicked, abusive, or deceitful words. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-peter/chapter-3#Verses 8–13|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Peter 3]]
- We sanctify God before others, when our conduct invites and encourages them to glorify and honour him. What was the ground and reason of their hope? [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-peter/chapter-3#Verses 14–22|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Peter 3]]
- Wherein the apostle describes the duties of husbands and wives one to another, beginning with the duty of the wife, 1Pe 3:1-7. He exhorts Christians to unity, love, compassion, peace, and patience under sufferings; to… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/1-peter/chapter-3|Matthew Henry Complete on 1 Peter 3]]
- McGee: subjection is voluntary, not a bare command—aimed at winning an unsaved husband by behavior, not outward Roman-style adornment or sex appeal; husbands love and honor with intelligent understanding—family prayers fail when marriage is wrong; church conduct is like-minded, tender, humble, non-retaliatory; be ready to explain hope; suffer for good not evil; Christ suffered for sins once; “when” in v. 20 places the preaching in Noah’s day—rejecters’ spirits were later in prison; baptism is identification with Christ by resurrection; believers seated with him in heaven. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Peter.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on 1 Peter]] [[wiki/source-notes/McGee Notes and Outlines on 1 Peter|McGee source-note]]

## Agreements and tensions

Complete commentary on this chapter deepens the Concise atlas with fuller pastoral and doctrinal exposition (Phase 4.1). McGee agrees with Henry on household witness, unity under persecution, and Christ’s suffering as the ground of hope; his Noah-day reading of spirits in prison and baptism-as-identification are distinctive outline positions and should be held beside other interpretations, not as the vault’s sole word. Concise notes and McGee outlines are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
