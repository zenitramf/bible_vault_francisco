# Ezekiel 24

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Ezekiel 24: The fate of Jerusalem. (1-14). The extent of the sufferings of the Jews. (15-27).

## Core claims

- The pot on the fire represented Jerusalem besieged by the Chaldeans: all orders and ranks were within the walls, prepared as a prey for the enemy. They ought to have put away their transgressions, as the scum, which rises by the heat of the fire, is taken from the top of the pot. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ezekiel/chapter-24#Verses 1-14|Matthew Henry Concise on Ezekiel 24]]
- Though mourning for the dead is a duty, yet it must be kept under by religion and right reason: we must not sorrow as men that have no hope. Believers must not copy the language and expressions of those who know not God. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ezekiel/chapter-24#Verses 15-27|Matthew Henry Concise on Ezekiel 24]]
- Here are two sermons in this chapter, preached on a particular occasion, and they are both from Mount Sinai, the mount of terror, both from Mount Ebal, the mount of curses; both speak the approaching fate of Jerusalem.… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-4/ezekiel/chapter-24|Matthew Henry Complete on Ezekiel 24]]
