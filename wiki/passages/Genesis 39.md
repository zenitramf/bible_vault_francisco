# Genesis 39

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 39: Joseph preferred by Potiphar. (1–6). Joseph resists temptation. (7–12). Joseph is falsely accused by his mistress. (13–18). He is cast into prison, God is with him there. (19–23).

## Core claims

- Our enemies may strip us of outward distinctions and ornaments; but wisdom and grace cannot be taken from us. They may separate us from friends, relatives, and country; but they cannot take from us the presence of the Lord. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/genesis/chapter-39#Verses 1–6|Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 39]]
- Joseph's mistress, having tried in vain to make him a guilty man, endeavoured to be avenged on him. Those that have broken the bonds of modesty, will never be held by the bonds of truth. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/genesis/chapter-39#Verses 13–18|Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 39]]
- Joseph's master believed the accusation. Potiphar, it is likely, chose that prison, because it was the worst; but God designed to open the way to Joseph's honour. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/genesis/chapter-39#Verses 19–23|Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 39]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
