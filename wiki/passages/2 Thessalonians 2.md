# 2 Thessalonians 2

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise and Complete caution against the error that Christ’s coming was immediately at hand, describe the man of sin and what hindered him, and comfort the elect remnant to stand fast. McGee’s program for the world: **Rapture first** (“our gathering together unto him”); day of the Lord follows total apostasy and the man of sin; mystery of lawlessness already works, restrained by the Holy Spirit; lawless one appears in the Tribulation and is destroyed by Christ’s brightness; believers established in the Word with the full spectrum of salvation (chosen, Spirit sanctification, belief of truth, glory).

## Core claims

- If errors arise among Christians, we should set them right; and good men will be careful to suppress errors which rise from mistaking their words and actions. We have a cunning adversary, who watches to do mischief, and will promote errors, even by the words of Scripture. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-thessalonians/chapter-2#Verses 1–4|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Thessalonians 2]]
- Something hindered or withheld the man of sin. It is supposed to be the power of the Roman empire, which the apostle did not mention more plainly at that time. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-thessalonians/chapter-2#Verses 5–12|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Thessalonians 2]]
- When we hear of the apostacy of many, it is a great comfort and joy, that there is a remnant according to the election of grace, which does and shall persevere; especially we should rejoice, if we have reason to hope that we are of that number. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-thessalonians/chapter-2#Verses 13–15|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Thessalonians 2]]
- We may and should direct our prayers, not only to God the Father, through our Lord Jesus Christ, but also to our Lord Jesus Christ himself. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-thessalonians/chapter-2#Verses 16, 17|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Thessalonians 2]]
- The apostle is very careful to hinder the spreading of an error into which some among them had fallen concerning the coming of Christ, as being very near, 2Th 2:1-3. Then he proceeds to confute the error he cautioned… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/2-thessalonians/chapter-2|Matthew Henry Complete on 2 Thessalonians 2]]
- McGee: false report claimed the day of the Lord had come and they had missed the gathering; KJV “day of Christ” at 2:2 should be “day of the Lord”—day of Christ relates to the church; day of the Lord begins after the Rapture (Tribulation through Millennium). Two signs: falling away (apostasy) and revelation of the man of sin (lawless one; Daniel 7 little horn), who assumes God’s place. Restrainer is the Holy Spirit; when removed, the lawless one is revealed and later destroyed (Rev 19:20). Power, signs, lying wonders; strong delusion for those who refused truth. Salvation spectrum in 2:13–14; Word establishes, consoles, and leads to the Lord’s work. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Thessalonians.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on 1–2 Thessalonians]] [[wiki/source-notes/McGee Notes and Outlines on 1 and 2 Thessalonians|McGee source-note]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Henry and McGee agree the Thessalonians must not think the final day has already arrived, that apostasy and the man of sin figure in the program, and that election and the Word steady believers.
- **Major tension on the restrainer:** Henry (with a common older Protestant view) supposes Roman imperial power; McGee identifies the Holy Spirit whose removal coincides with the church’s gathering. Both are sourced readings—do not collapse them.
- McGee’s day-of-Christ vs day-of-the-Lord lexical correction and pre-trib sequence are dispensational outline claims; hold beside Henry without declaring a vault verdict.
