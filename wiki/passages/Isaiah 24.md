# Isaiah 24

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 24: The desolation of the land. (1-12). A few shall be preserved. (13-15). God's kingdom advanced by his judgments. (16-23).

## Core claims

- All whose treasures and happiness are laid up on earth, will soon be brought to want and misery. It is good to apply to ourselves what the Scripture says of the vanity and vexation of spirit which attend all things here below. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-24#Verses 1-12|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 24]]
- There shall be a remnant preserved from the general ruin, and it shall be a devout and pious remnant. These few are dispersed; like the gleanings of the olive tree, hid under the leaves. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-24#Verses 13-15|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 24]]
- Believers may be driven into the uttermost parts of the earth; but they are singing, not sighing. Here is terror to sinners; the prophet laments the miseries he saw breaking in like a torrent; and the small number of believers. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-24#Verses 16-23|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 24]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
