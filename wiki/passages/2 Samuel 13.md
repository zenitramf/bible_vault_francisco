# 2 Samuel 13

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Samuel 13: Ammon's violence to his sister. (1–20). Absalom murders his brother Ammon. (21–29). David's grief, Absalom flees to Geshur. (30–39).

## Core claims

- From henceforward David was followed with one trouble after another. Adultery and murder were David's sins, the like sins among his children were the beginnings of his punishment: he was too indulgent to his children. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-samuel/chapter-13#Verses 1–20|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Samuel 13]]
- Observe the aggravations of Absalom's sin: he would have Ammon slain, when least fit to go out of the world. He engaged his servants in the guilt. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-samuel/chapter-13#Verses 21–29|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Samuel 13]]
- Jonadab was as guilty of Ammon's death, as of his sin; such false friends do they prove, who counsel us to do wickedly. Instead of loathing Absalom as a murderer, David, after a time, longed to go forth to him. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-samuel/chapter-13#Verses 30–39|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Samuel 13]]
- The righteous God had lately told David, by Nathan the prophet, that, to chastise him for his son in the matter of Uriah, he would "raise up evil against him out of his own house," (2Sa 12:11).
