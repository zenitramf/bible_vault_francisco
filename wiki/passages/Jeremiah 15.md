# Jeremiah 15

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Jeremiah 15: The destruction of the wicked described. (1-9). The prophet laments such messages, and is reproved. (10-14). He supplicates pardon, and is promised protection. (15-21).

## Core claims

- The Lord declares that even Moses and Samuel must have pleaded in vain. The putting of this as a case, though they should stand before him, shows that they do not, and that saints in heaven do not pray for saints on earth. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/jeremiah/chapter-15#Verses 1-9|Matthew Henry Concise on Jeremiah 15]]
- Jeremiah met with much contempt and reproach, when they ought to have blessed him, and God for him. It is a great and sufficient support to the people of God, that however troublesome their way may be, it shall be well with them in their latter end. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/jeremiah/chapter-15#Verses 10-14|Matthew Henry Concise on Jeremiah 15]]
- It is matter of comfort that we have a God, to whose knowledge of all things we may appeal. Jeremiah pleads with God for mercy and relief against his enemies, persecutors, and slanderers. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/jeremiah/chapter-15#Verses 15-21|Matthew Henry Concise on Jeremiah 15]]
- When we left the prophet, in the close of the foregoing chapter, so pathetically poring out his prayers before God, we had reason to hope that in this chapter we should find God reconciled to the land and the prophet… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-4/jeremiah/chapter-15|Matthew Henry Complete on Jeremiah 15]]
