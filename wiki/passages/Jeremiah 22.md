# Jeremiah 22

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Jeremiah 22: Justice is recommended, and destruction threatened in case of disobedience. (1-9). The captivity of Jehoiakim, and the end of Jeconiah. (10-19). The doom of the royal family. (20-30).

## Core claims

- The king of Judah is spoken to, as sitting upon the throne of David, the man after God's own heart. Let him follow his example, that he may have the benefit of the promises made to him. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/jeremiah/chapter-22#Verses 1-9|Matthew Henry Concise on Jeremiah 22]]
- Here is a sentence of death upon two kings, the wicked sons of a very pious father. Josiah was prevented from seeing the evil to come in this world, and removed to see the good to come in the other world; therefore, weep not for him, but for his son Shallum, who is likely to live and die a wretched captive. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/jeremiah/chapter-22#Verses 10-19|Matthew Henry Concise on Jeremiah 22]]
- The Jewish state is described under a threefold character. Very haughty in a day of peace and safety. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/jeremiah/chapter-22#Verses 20-30|Matthew Henry Concise on Jeremiah 22]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
