# Hosea 1

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Hosea 1: Under a figure, is represented the shameful idolatry of the ten tribes. (1-7). The calling of the Gentiles, and the uniting Israel and Judah under the Messiah. (8-11).

## Core claims

- Israel was prosperous, yet then Hosea boldly tells them of their sins, and foretells their destruction. Men are not to be flattered in sinful ways because they prosper in the world; nor will it last long if they go on still in their trespasses. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/hosea/chapter-1#Verses 1-7|Matthew Henry Concise on Hosea 1]]
- The rejection of Israel for a time, is signified by the name of another child: call him Lo-ammi, "not my people." The Lord disowns all relation to them. We love him, because he first loved us; but our being cast out of covenant, is owing to ourselves and our folly. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/hosea/chapter-1#Verses 8-11|Matthew Henry Concise on Hosea 1]]
- The mind of God is revealed to this prophet, and by him to the people, in the first three chapters, by signs and types, but afterwards only by discourse. In this chapter we have, I.
