# 2 Kings 7

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Kings 7: Elisha prophesies plenty. (1, 2). The flight of the Syrian army. (3–11). Samaria plentifully supplied. (12–20).

## Core claims

- Man's extremity is God's opportunity of making his own power to be glorious: his time to appear for his people is when their strength is gone. Unbelief is a sin by which men greatly dishonour and displease God, and deprive themselves of the favours he designed for them. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-kings/chapter-7#Verses 1, 2|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Kings 7]]
- God can, when he pleases, make the stoutest heart to tremble; and as for those who will not fear God, he can make them fear at the shaking of a leaf. Providence ordered it, that the lepers came as soon as the Syrians were fled. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-kings/chapter-7#Verses 3–11|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Kings 7]]
- Here see the wants of Israel supplied in a way they little thought of, which should encourage us to depend upon the power and goodness of God in our greatest straits. God's promise may be safely relied on, for no word of his shall fall to the ground. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-kings/chapter-7#Verses 12–20|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Kings 7]]
- Relief is here brought to Samaria and her king, when the case is, in a manner, desperate, and the king despairing. I.
