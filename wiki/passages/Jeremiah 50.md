# Jeremiah 50

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Jeremiah 50: The ruin of Babylon. (1-3, 8-16, 21-32, 35-46) . The redemption of God's people. (4-7, 17-20, 33, 34).

## Core claims

- The king of Babylon was kind to Jeremiah, yet the prophet must foretell the ruin of that kingdom. If our friends are God's enemies, we dare not speak peace to them. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/jeremiah/chapter-50#Verses 1-7|Matthew Henry Concise on Jeremiah 50]]
- The forces are mustered and empowered to destroy Babylon. Let them do what God demands, and they shall bring to pass what he threatens. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/jeremiah/chapter-50#Verses 21-32|Matthew Henry Concise on Jeremiah 50]]
- It is Israel's comfort in distress, that, though they are weak, their Redeemer is strong. This may be applied to believers, who complain of the dominion of sin and corruption, and of their own weakness and manifold infirmities. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/jeremiah/chapter-50#Verses 33-46|Matthew Henry Concise on Jeremiah 50]]
