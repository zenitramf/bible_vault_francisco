# 2 John 1

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise and Complete salute the elect lady and her children, commend early faith and love, warn against deceivers concerning Christ’s person or office, and prefer face-to-face fellowship. McGee’s Notes & Outlines thicken the same chapter: theme “for truth’s sake”; love expressed only within the boundary of truth; Gnostic (Cerinthian and Docetic) denial of Christ’s flesh as antichrist; warning of lost reward not salvation; refuse house and greeting to teachers who lack the doctrine of Christ, lest supporters become partners in error.

## Core claims

- Religion turns compliments into real expressions of respect and love. An old disciple is honourable; an old apostle and leader of disciples is more so. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-john/chapter-1#Verses 1–3|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 John 1]]
- It is good to be trained to early religion; and children may be beloved for their parents' sake. It gave great joy to the apostle to see children treading in their parents' steps, and likely in their turn to support the gospel. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-john/chapter-1#Verses 4–6|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 John 1]]
- The deceiver and his deceit are described: he brings some error concerning the person or office of the Lord Jesus. Such a one is a deceiver and an antichrist; he deludes souls, and undermines the glory and kingdom of the Lord Christ. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-john/chapter-1#Verses 7–11|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 John 1]]
- The apostle refers many things to a personal meeting. Pen and ink were means of strengthening and comforting others; but to see each other is more so. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-john/chapter-1#Verses 12, 13|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 John 1]]
- The apostle here salutes an honourable matron and her children, 2Jo 1:1-3. Recommends to them faith and love, 2Jo 1:5,6. Warns them of deceivers (2Jo 1:7), and to take heed to themselves, 2Jo 1:8. Teaches how to treat… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/2-john/chapter-1|Matthew Henry Complete on 2 John 1]]
- McGee: writer John the Apostle (A.D. 90–100) as “the elder”; addressee “elect lady” may be a prominent lady or (his lean) a local church; theme is truth worth contending for—love within the boundary of truth (vv. 1–6), life as expression of the doctrine of Christ (vv. 7–11). [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee Notes & Outlines on 2–3 John]] [[wiki/source-notes/McGee Notes and Outlines on 2 and 3 John|McGee 2–3 John source-note]]
- McGee: Gnosticism denied Christ come in the flesh (Cerinthian: Spirit came at baptism and left at the cross; Docetic: phantom manhood); “an antichrist” should be the antichrist (v. 7). Warning is of missing reward, not salvation (v. 8); rejecting the doctrine of Christ marks non-believers—doctrine proves life (v. 9). Do not receive or greet such teachers; supporters are partners in error (vv. 10–11). [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee on 2 John 7–11]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Henry and McGee agree that error about Christ’s person is antichristian deceit and that hospitality/support must not underwrite false teachers.
- McGee adds the Gnostic taxonomy (Cerinthian/Docetic), truth-and-love polarity with Eph 4:15, reward-not-salvation on v. 8, and a preferred local-church reading of “elect lady” that he does not insist on—hold beside Henry’s honourable-matron reading.
- Complete commentary deepens Concise pastoral summary; McGee supplies radio outline praxis rather than full critical commentary.

## Biblical passages

Primary scope: 2 John 1 (full chapter). Supporting: Ephesians 4:15; 1 John 2:7 (McGee cross-ref).
