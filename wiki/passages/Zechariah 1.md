# Zechariah 1

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Zechariah 1: An exhortation to repentance. (1–6). A vision of the ministry of angels. (7–17). The security of the Jews and the destruction of their enemies. (18–21).

## Core claims

- God's almighty power and sovereign dominion, should engage and encourage sinners to repent and turn to Him. It is very desirable to have the Lord of hosts for our friend, and very dreadful to have him for our enemy. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/zechariah/chapter-1#Verses 1–6|Matthew Henry Concise on Zechariah 1]]
- The prophet saw a dark, shady grove, hidden by hills. This represented the low, melancholy condition of the Jewish church. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/zechariah/chapter-1#Verses 7–17|Matthew Henry Concise on Zechariah 1]]
- The enemies of the church threaten to cut off the name of Israel. They are horns, emblems of power, strength, and violence. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/zechariah/chapter-1#Verses 18–21|Matthew Henry Concise on Zechariah 1]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
