# Deuteronomy 25

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Deuteronomy 25: Extent of punishment. (1–3). The ox that treadeth the corn. (4). Marriage of a brother's wife. (5–12). Of unjust weights. (13–16). War against Amalek. (17–19).

## Core claims

- Every punishment should be with solemnity, that those who see it may be filled with dread, and be warned not to offend in like manner. And though the criminals must be shamed as well as put to pain, for their warning and disgrace, yet care should be taken that they do not appear totally vile. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/deuteronomy/chapter-25#Verses 1–3|Matthew Henry Concise on Deuteronomy 25]]
- The custom here regulated seems to have been in the Jewish law in order to keep inheritances distinct; now it is unlawful. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/deuteronomy/chapter-25#Verses 5–12|Matthew Henry Concise on Deuteronomy 25]]
- Let every persecutor and injurer of God's people take warning from the case of the Amalekites. The longer it is before judgement comes, the more dreadful will it be at last. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/deuteronomy/chapter-25#Verses 17–19|Matthew Henry Concise on Deuteronomy 25]]
- Here is, I. A law to moderate the scourging of malefactors, De 25:1-3. II. A law in favour of the ox that treads out the corn, De 25:4. III.
