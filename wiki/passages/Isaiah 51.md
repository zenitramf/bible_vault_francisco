# Isaiah 51

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 51: Exhortations to trust the Messiah. (1-3). The power of God, and the weakness of man. (4-8). Christ defends his people. (9-16). Their afflictions and deliverances. (17-23).

## Core claims

- It is good for those privileged by the new birth, to consider that they were shapen in sin. This should cause low thoughts of ourselves, and high thoughts of Divine grace. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-51#Verses 1-3|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 51]]
- The people whom Christ has redeemed with his blood, as well as by his power, will obtain joyful deliverance from every enemy. He that designs such joy for us at last, will he not work such deliverance in the mean time, as our cases require? [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-51#Verses 9-16|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 51]]
- God calls upon his people to mind the things that belong to their everlasting peace. Jerusalem had provoked God, and was made to taste the bitter fruits. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-51#Verses 17-23|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 51]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
