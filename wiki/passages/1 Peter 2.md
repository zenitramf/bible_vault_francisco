# 1 Peter 2

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Peter 2: A temper suitable to the Christian character as born again, is recommended. (1–10). Holy conversation among the Gentiles directed. (11, 12). Subjects exhorted to pay all proper obedience to their civil governors. (13–…). McGee frames the chapter under suffering and Christ’s suffering producing **separation**: lay aside fleshly malice; desire pure milk; living Stone and living stones; royal priesthood and people for God’s possession publishing his virtues; civil, social, and labor witness; Christ’s vicarious death and Isa 53 “stripes” as healing from sin.

## Agreements and tensions

Complete commentary on this chapter deepens the Concise atlas with fuller pastoral and doctrinal exposition (Phase 4.1). McGee agrees with Henry on new-birth appetite for the word, priesthood privileges, honest conversation under civil authority, and Christ’s example in unjust suffering; his “peculiar people” as acquisition/possession and Isa 53 sin-healing emphasis sharpen the atonement reading and should sit beside other healing/atonement sources. Concise notes and McGee outlines are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
