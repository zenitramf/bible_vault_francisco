# 1 John 1

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise frames the chapter as apostolic testimonies to Christ for believers’ joy and the necessity of holiness for communion with God. McGee adds the family-epistle frame: “from the beginning” is the incarnation (not John 1:1 eternity or Genesis creation), against Gnostic denial of Jesus’ full humanity; God is light; fellowship (*koinonia*) comes by walking in light and confessing sin.

## Core claims

- That essential Good, that uncreated Excellence, which had been from the beginning, from eternity, as equal with the Father, and which at length appeared in human nature for the salvation of sinners, was the great subject concerning which the apostle wrote to his brethren. - A message from the Lord Jesus, the Word of life, the eternal Word, we should all gladly receive. The great God should be represented to this dark world, as pure and perfect light. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-john/chapter-1#Verses 5–10|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 John 1]]
- Evidence given concerning Christ's person and excellency, 1Jo 1:1,2. The knowledge thereof gives us communion with God and Christ (1Jo 1:3), and joy, 1Jo 1:4. A description of God, 1Jo 1:5. How we are thereupon to… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/1-john/chapter-1|Matthew Henry Complete on 1 John 1]]
- McGee: “From the beginning” is incarnation and earthly life, not John 1:1 or Genesis 1:1; John answers Gnosticism by asserting Jesus heard, seen, gazed upon (*theaomai*), and handled (Luke 24:39). Apostles witness eternal life in him (1:1–2). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_John.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 1 John 1:1–2]]
- McGee: Purposes include fellowship with Father, Son, and believers (1:3) and full joy (1:4); *koinonia* is sharing the things of Christ. God is light—holiness searching darkness; darkness is hostile evil. Walking in the Word’s light exposes sin; the blood cleanses (1:5–7). Confess (*homologomen*) means say what God says about specific sins; he is faithful and just to forgive and cleanse (1:9); claiming sinlessness deceives self and makes God a liar (1:8, 10). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_John.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 1 John 1:3–10]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Henry and McGee agree on incarnate Word, light/holiness, and confession/cleansing for fellowship.
- McGee’s Gnostic polemic and family-vs-church frame thicken the pastoral atlas; Henry stresses communion and joy more devotionally.
- Complete commentary deepens Concise; McGee is radio outline notes, not full critical commentary.
