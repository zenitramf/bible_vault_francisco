# Zechariah 4

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Zechariah 4: A vision of a candlestick, with two olive trees. (1–7). Further encouragement. (8–10). An explanation respecting the olive trees. (11–14).

## Core claims

- The prophet's spirit was willing to attend, but the flesh was weak. We should beg of God that, whenever he speaks to us, he would awaken us, and we should then stir up ourselves. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/zechariah/chapter-4#Verses 1–7|Matthew Henry Concise on Zechariah 4]]
- The exact fulfilment of Scripture prophecies is a convincing proof of their Divine original. Though the instruments be weak and unlikely, yet God often chooses such, to bring about great things by them. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/zechariah/chapter-4#Verses 8–10|Matthew Henry Concise on Zechariah 4]]
- Zechariah desires to know what are the two olive trees. Zerubbabel and Joshua, this prince and this priest, were endued with the gifts and graces of God's Spirit. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/zechariah/chapter-4#Verses 11–14|Matthew Henry Concise on Zechariah 4]]
- In this chapter we have another comfortable vision, which, as it was explained to the prophet, had much in it for the encouragement of the people of God in their present straits, which were so great that they thought… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-4/zechariah/chapter-4|Matthew Henry Complete on Zechariah 4]]
