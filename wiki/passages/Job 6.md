# Job 6

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Job 6: Job justifies his complaints. (1–7). He wishes for death. (8–13). Job reproves his friends as unkind. (14–30).

## Core claims

- Job still justifies himself in his complaints. In addition to outward troubles, the inward sense of God's wrath took away all his courage and resolution. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-6#Verses 1–7|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 6]]
- Job had desired death as the happy end of his miseries. For this, Eliphaz had reproved him, but he asks for it again with more vehemence than before. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-6#Verses 8–13|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 6]]
- In his prosperity Job formed great expectations from his friends, but now was disappointed. This he compares to the failing of brooks in summer. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-6#Verses 14–30|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 6]]
