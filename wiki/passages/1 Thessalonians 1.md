# 1 Thessalonians 1

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise and Complete treat the chapter as thanksgiving for saving benefits and evidences of gospel success—faith, love, and patient hope as tokens of election, and a turn from idols to wait for Christ’s second coming. McGee’s Notes & Outlines frame the chapter as the coming of Christ as an **inspiring hope**: gospel received in assurance and affliction; results are work of faith (turn to God from idols), labor of love (serve the living God), and patience of hope (wait for His Son from heaven), with deliverance from the wrath to come.

## Core claims

- As all good comes from God, so no good can be hoped for by sinners, but from God in Christ. And the best good may be expected from God, as our Father, for the sake of Christ. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-thessalonians/chapter-1#Verses 1–5|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Thessalonians 1]]
- When careless, ignorant, and immoral persons are turned from their carnal pursuits and connexions, to believe in and obey the Lord Jesus, to live soberly, righteously, and godly, the matter speaks for itself. The believers under the Old Testament waited for the coming of the Messiah, and believers now wait for his second coming. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-thessalonians/chapter-1#Verses 6–10|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Thessalonians 1]]
- After the introduction (1Th 1:1) the apostle begins with a thanksgiving to God for the saving benefits bestowed on them, 1Th 1:2-5. And then mentions the sure evidences of the good success of the gospel among them. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/1-thessalonians/chapter-1|Matthew Henry Complete on 1 Thessalonians 1]]
- McGee: grace (Greek greeting) and peace (Hebrew *shalom*)—grace known before peace experienced; faith, love, and hope produce work, labor, and patience (cf. 1 Cor 13); election is God’s side while “whosoever will” remains open; gospel came not in word only but in power and the Holy Spirit; affliction and joy can be joined; turning to Christ automatically turned them from idols (repentance); waiting for the Son delivers from “the wrath to come” (Great Tribulation). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Thessalonians.pdf|McGee Notes & Outlines on 1–2 Thessalonians]] [[wiki/source-notes/McGee Notes and Outlines on 1 and 2 Thessalonians|McGee source-note]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Henry and McGee agree that genuine conversion shows in turned lives and expectant waiting for Christ.
- McGee’s identification of “wrath to come” with the Great Tribulation and his election/whosoever framing are dispensational/pastoral outline readings; hold beside Henry’s broader pastoral summary.
- Complete commentary deepens Concise; McGee adds the inspiring-hope outline and three-fold faith/love/hope results.
