# Genesis 31

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 31: Jacob departs secretly. (1–21). Laban pursues Jacob. (23–35). Jacob's complaint of Laban's conduct. (36–42). Their covenant at Galeed. (43–55).

## Core claims

- The affairs of these families are related very minutely, while (what are called) the great events of states and kingdoms at that period, are not mentioned. - If Jacob were willingly consumed with heat in the day, and frost by night, to become the son-in-law of Laban, what should we refuse to endure, to become the sons of God? Jacob speaks of God as the God of his father; he thought himself unworthy to be regarded, but was beloved for his father's sake. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/genesis/chapter-31#Verses 36–42|Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 31]]
- Laban could neither justify himself nor condemn Jacob, therefore desires to hear no more of that matter. He is not willing to own himself in fault, as he ought to have done. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/genesis/chapter-31#Verses 43–55|Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 31]]
- Jacob was a very honest good man, a man of great devotion and integrity, yet he had more trouble and vexation than any of the patriarchs.
