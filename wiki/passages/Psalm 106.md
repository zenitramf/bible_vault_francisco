# Psalm 106

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 106: The happiness of God's people. (1–5). Israel's sins. (6–12). Their provocations. (13–33). Their rebellions in Canaan. (34–46). Prayer for more complete deliverance. (47, 48).

## Core claims

- None of our sins or sufferings should prevent our ascribing glory and praise to the Lord. The more unworthy we are, the more is his kindness to be admired. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/psalms/psalm-106#Verses 1–5|Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 106]]
- Those that will not wait for God's counsel, shall justly be given up to their own hearts' lusts, to walk in their own counsels. An undue desire, even for lawful things, becomes sinful. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/psalms/psalm-106#Verses 13–33|Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 106]]
- The conduct of the Israelites in Canaan, and God's dealings with them, show that the way of sin is down-hill; omissions make way for commissions: when they neglected to destroy the heathen, they learned their works. One sin led to many more, and brought the judgments of God on them. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/psalms/psalm-106#Verses 34–48|Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 106]]
- We must give glory to God by making confession, not only of his goodness but our own badness, which serve as foils to each other.
