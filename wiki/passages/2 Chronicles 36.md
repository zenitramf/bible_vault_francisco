# 2 Chronicles 36

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Chronicles 36: The destruction of Jerusalem. (1–21). The proclamation of Cyrus. (22, 23). McGee highlights Jeremiah’s sabbath-land explanation of the seventy years (v. 21), Cyrus’s decree to rebuild the temple, and the captivity years passed over as “time out” when Israel is out of the land and will of God.

## Core claims

- The ruin of Judah and Jerusalem came on by degrees. The methods God takes to call back sinners by his word, by ministers, by conscience, by providences, are all instances of his compassion toward them, and his unwillingness that any should perish. - We have here, I. A short but sad account of the utter ruin of Judah and Jerusalem within a few years after Josiah's death. 1. The history of it in the unhappy reigns of Jehoahaz for three months (2Ch 36:1-4), Jehoiakim… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-2/2-chronicles/chapter-36|Matthew Henry Complete on 2 Chronicles 36]]
- McGee: two closing incidents—(1) Jeremiah’s explanation that seventy years let the land enjoy her sabbaths (v. 21); (2) Cyrus’s decree permitting return and temple rebuilding; the seventy years themselves are passed over as people out of God’s will—“time out” in God’s program; God’s clock is spelled I-S-R-A-E-L and runs only while Israel is in the land. ## Agreements and tensions

Complete commentary on this chapter deepens the Concise atlas with fuller pastoral and doctrinal exposition (Phase 4.1).

Henry and McGee agree on gradual ruin, Jeremiah’s seventy years, and Cyrus’s restoration hope. McGee’s “Israel clock / time out” frame is dispensational outline language—hold beside Henry’s pastoral church-not-cast-off comfort without forcing consensus.

Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
