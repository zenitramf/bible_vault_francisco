# Joshua 17

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Joshua 17: The lot of Manasseh. (1–6). The boundaries of Manasseh, The Canaanites not driven out. (7–13). Joseph desires a larger portion. (14–18).

## Core claims

- Manasseh was but half of the tribe of Joseph, yet it was divided into two parts. The daughters of Zelophehad now reaped the benefit of their pious zeal and prudent forecast. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/joshua/chapter-17#Verses 1–6|Matthew Henry Concise on Joshua 17]]
- There was great communication between Manasseh and Ephraim. Though each tribe had its inheritance, yet they should intermix one with another, to do good offices one to another, as became those, who, though of different tribes, were all one Israel, and were bound to love as brethren. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/joshua/chapter-17#Verses 7–13|Matthew Henry Concise on Joshua 17]]
- Joshua, as a public person, had no more regard to his own tribe than to any other, but would govern without favour or affection; wherein he has left a good example to all in public trusts. Joshua tells them, that what was fallen to their share would be a sufficient lot for them, if they would but work and fight. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/joshua/chapter-17#Verses 14–18|Matthew Henry Concise on Joshua 17]]
- The half tribe of Manasseh comes next to be provided for; and here we have, I. The families of that tribe that were to be portioned, Jos 17:1-6. II. The country that fell to their lot, Jos 17:7-13.
