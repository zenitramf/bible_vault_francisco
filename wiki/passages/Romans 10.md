# Romans 10

## Summary

Romans 10 in Henry Concise contrasts law-righteousness with faith-righteousness that is near in the preached word. The same Lord is rich to all who call upon him; faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God; Israel’s unbelief was foretold, yet the invitation remains free. Paul Chappell’s Lancaster Baptist sermon on 10:14–17 presses the same hearing-chain pastorally: provision in Christ’s blood is not yet universal *offer*, churches must send preachers, and faith comes by hearing though not all obey.

## Core claims

- Many build on a false foundation and refuse free salvation by faith in Christ; the law’s strictness shows the need of salvation by grace through faith. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-10#Verses 1–4|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 10]]
- The righteousness of faith does not require climbing to heaven or descending into the deep; Christ is offered in the word of the gospel near to us. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-10#Verses 5–11|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 10]]
- There is not one kinder God for Jews and a lesser for Gentiles; the promise is the same to all who call on the name of the Lord Jesus. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-10#Verses 12–17|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 10]]
- Faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God; the gospel must be sent if people are to believe. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-10#Verses 12–17|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 10]]
- The dissolving of the peculiar church-state of the Jews, and the rejection of that polity by the repealing of their ceremonial law, the vacating of all the institutions of it, the abolishing of their priesthood, the… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/romans/chapter-10|Matthew Henry Complete on Romans 10]]
- From Romans 10:14–17, “whosoever shall call” implies that hearers need a preacher and that churches must send laborers; salvation *provided* is not the same as salvation *offered* where the gospel has never been heard. [[sources/transcripts/How Shall They Hear - Paul Chappell|Paul Chappell, How Shall They Hear]] [[wiki/source-notes/How Shall They Hear - Paul Chappell|How Shall They Hear — Paul Chappell]]
- Faith comes by hearing the word of God, yet not all obey the gospel; people are saved by a believing heart that hears, not by automatic response to provision alone. [[sources/transcripts/How Shall They Hear - Paul Chappell|Paul Chappell, How Shall They Hear]] [[wiki/source-notes/How Shall They Hear - Paul Chappell|How Shall They Hear — Paul Chappell]]

## Agreements and tensions

Complete commentary on this chapter deepens the Concise atlas with fuller pastoral and doctrinal exposition (Phase 4.1).

Henry Concise/Complete supply the commentary spine; Chappell’s sermon thickens 10:14–17 with Independent Baptist missional application (soulwinning, sending, Bible college). Chappell’s denial of a God who “selects some for heaven and damns others to hell,” while affirming divine knowledge of destinies, is a free-will framing that tensions with stronger particular-election readings elsewhere—keep attributed, not as atlas consensus.

## Biblical passages

Primary scope: Romans 10 (full chapter). Focal sermon pericope: Romans 10:14–17 (with v. 13 as invitation hinge).
