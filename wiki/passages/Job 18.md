# Job 18

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Job 18: Bildad reproves Job. (1–4). Ruin attends the wicked. (5–10). The ruin of the wicked. (11–21).

## Core claims

- Bildad had before given Job good advice and encouragement; here he used nothing but rebukes, and declared his ruin. And he concluded that Job shut out the providence of God from the management of human affairs, because he would not admit himself to be wicked. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-18#Verses 1–4|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 18]]
- Bildad describes the miserable condition of a wicked man; in which there is much certain truth, if we consider that a sinful condition is a sad condition, and that sin will be men's ruin, if they do not repent. Though Bildad thought the application of it to Job was easy, yet it was not safe nor just. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-18#Verses 5–10|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 18]]
- Bildad describes the destruction wicked people are kept for, in the other world, and which in some degree, often seizes them in this world. The way of sin is the way of fear, and leads to everlasting confusion, of which the present terrors of an impure conscience are earnests, as in Cain and Judas. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-18#Verses 11–21|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 18]]
- In this chapter Bildad makes a second assault upon Job. In his first discourse (Job 8:1-22) he had given him encouragement to hope that all should yet be well with him.
