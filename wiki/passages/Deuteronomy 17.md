# Deuteronomy 17

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Deuteronomy 17: All sacrifices to be perfect, Idolaters must be slain. (1–7). Difficult controversies. (8–13). The choice of a king, His duties. (14–20).

## Core claims

- No creature which had any blemish was to be offered in sacrifice to God. We are thus called to remember the perfect, pure, and spotless sacrifice of Christ, and reminded to serve God with the best of our abilities, time, and possession, or our pretended obedience will be hateful to him. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/deuteronomy/chapter-17#Verses 1–7|Matthew Henry Concise on Deuteronomy 17]]
- Courts of judgment were to be set up in every city. Though their judgment had not the Divine authority of an oracle, it was the judgment of wise, prudent, experienced men, and had the advantage of a Divine promise. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/deuteronomy/chapter-17#Verses 8–13|Matthew Henry Concise on Deuteronomy 17]]
- God himself was in a particular manner Israel's King; and if they set another over them, it was necessary that he should choose the person. Accordingly, when the people desired a king, they applied to Samuel, a prophet of the Lord. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/deuteronomy/chapter-17#Verses 14–20|Matthew Henry Concise on Deuteronomy 17]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
