# Psalm 32

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 32: The happiness of a pardoned sinner. (1, 2). The misery that went before, and the comfort that followed the confession of sins. (3–7). Sinners instructed, believers encouraged. (8–11).

## Core claims

- Sin is the cause of our misery; but the true believer's transgressions of the Divine law are all forgiven, being covered with the atonement. Christ bare his sins, therefore they are not imputed to him. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/psalms/psalm-32#Verses 1, 2|Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 32]]
- It is very difficult to bring sinful man humbly to accept free mercy, with a full confession of his sins and self-condemnation. But the true and only way to peace of conscience, is, to confess our sins, that they may be forgiven; to declare them that we may be justified. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/psalms/psalm-32#Verses 3–7|Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 32]]
- God teaches by his word, and guides with the secret intimations of his will. David gives a word of caution to sinners. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/psalms/psalm-32#Verses 8–11|Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 32]]
- This psalm, though it speaks not of Christ, as many of the psalms we have hitherto met with have done, has yet a great deal of gospel in it.
