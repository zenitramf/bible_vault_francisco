# Acts 22

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Acts 22: Paul's account of his conversion. (1–11). Paul directed to preach to the Gentiles. (12–21). The rage of the Jews Paul pleads that he is a Roman citizen. (22–30).

## Core claims

- The apostle addressed the enraged multitude, in the customary style of respect and good-will. Paul relates the history of his early life very particularly; he notices that his conversion was wholly the act of God. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/acts/chapter-22#Verses 1–11|Matthew Henry Concise on Acts 22]]
- The apostle goes on to relate how he was confirmed in the change he had made. The Lord having chosen the sinner, that he should know his will, he is humbled, enlightened, and brought to the knowledge of Christ and his blessed gospel. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/acts/chapter-22#Verses 12–21|Matthew Henry Concise on Acts 22]]
- The Jews listened to Paul's account of his conversion, but the mention of his being sent to the Gentiles, was so contrary to all their national prejudices, that they would hear no more. Their frantic conduct astonished the Roman officer, who supposed that Paul must have committed some great crime. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/acts/chapter-22#Verses 22–30|Matthew Henry Concise on Acts 22]]
