# Judges 11

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Judges 11: Jephtah and the Gileadites. (1–11). He attempts to make peace. (12–28). Jephthah's vow. He vanquishes the Ammonites. (29–40).

## Core claims

- Men ought not to be blamed for their parentage, so long as they by their personal merits roll away any reproach. God had forgiven Israel, therefore Jephthah will forgive. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/judges/chapter-11#Verses 1–11|Matthew Henry Concise on Judges 11]]
- One instance of the honour and respect we owe to God, as our God, is, rightly to employ what he gives us to possess. Receive it from him, use it for him, and part with it when he calls for it. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/judges/chapter-11#Verses 12–28|Matthew Henry Concise on Judges 11]]
- Several important lessons are to be learned from Jephthah's vow. 1. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/judges/chapter-11#Verses 29–40|Matthew Henry Concise on Judges 11]]
- This chapter gives as the history of Jephthah, another of Israel's judges, and numbered among the worthies of the Old Testament, that by faith did great things (Heb 11:32), though he had not such an extraordinary call… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-2/judges/chapter-11|Matthew Henry Complete on Judges 11]]
