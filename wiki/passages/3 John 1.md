# 3 John 1

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise and Complete commend Gaius’s soul prosperity and hospitality, expose Diotrephes’ proud turbulent spirit, and praise Demetrius’ good report. McGee’s Notes & Outlines cast the letter as truth worth working for (complement to 2 John’s stand against false teachers): Gaius receives true itinerant teachers; Diotrephes the preeminence-lover refuses apostles and missionaries and expels hosts; Demetrius adorns the doctrine—two genuine men and one phony in shoe-leather Christianity.

## Core claims

- Those who are beloved of Christ, will love the brethren for his sake. Soul prosperity is the greatest blessing on this side heaven. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/3-john/chapter-1#Verses 1–8|Matthew Henry Concise on 3 John 1]]
- Both the heart and mouth must be watched. The temper and spirit of Diotrephes was full of pride and ambition. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/3-john/chapter-1#Verses 9–12|Matthew Henry Concise on 3 John 1]]
- Here is the character of Demetrius. A name in the gospel, or a good report in the churches, is better than worldly honour. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/3-john/chapter-1#Verses 13, 14|Matthew Henry Concise on 3 John 1]]
- In this epistle the apostle congratulates Gaius upon the prosperity of his soul (3Jo 1:1,2), upon the fame he had among good Christians (3Jo 1:3,4), and upon his charity and hospitality to the servants of Christ, 3Jo… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/3-john/chapter-1|Matthew Henry Complete on 3 John 1]]
- McGee: same elder-author and date as 2 John; truth is worth working for. Gaius “the beloved” (four times); first “the truth” is apostolic doctrine, second “truth” is conduct; walk in truth means conduct conforms to doctrine; John prays for physical and material welfare and commends hospitality to true teachers (vv. 1–8)—the positive counterpart of 2 John’s ban on false teachers. [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee Notes & Outlines on 2–3 John]] [[wiki/source-notes/McGee Notes and Outlines on 2 and 3 John|McGee 2–3 John source-note]]
- McGee: Diotrephes loved preeminence—five charges: must lead; refused John; malicious talk against apostles; refused missionaries (wanted to teach himself); excommunicated those who hosted them—“Diotrephes, the dictator”; evil deeds express false doctrine (vv. 9–11). Demetrius has good report of all and of the truth; pagan-name convert who adorned Christ’s doctrine (v. 12). John plans a visit (vv. 13–14). [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee on 3 John 9–14]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Henry and McGee agree on Gaius’s commendable hospitality, Diotrephes’ prideful ambition, and Demetrius’ excellent character.
- McGee sharpens the 2 John / 3 John pair (stand for truth vs work for truth), the doctrine/conduct dual use of “truth,” the five-charge Diotrephes catalog, and the itinerant-missionary housing setting—useful pastoral color, not a sole polity rule.
- Complete deepens Concise; McGee is radio outline praxis.

## Biblical passages

Primary scope: 3 John 1 (full chapter). Pair with 2 John 1 on hospitality and truth.
