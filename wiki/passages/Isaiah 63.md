# Isaiah 63

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 63: Christ's victory over his enemies. (1-6). His mercy toward his church. (7-14). The prayer of the church. (15-19).

## Core claims

- The prophet, in vision, beholds the Messiah returning in triumph from the conquest of his enemies, of whom Edom was a type. Travelling, not as wearied by the combat, but, in the greatness of his strength, prepared to overcome every opposing power. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-63#Verses 1-6|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 63]]
- The latter part of this chapter, and the whole of the next, seem to express the prayers of the Jews on their conversation. They acknowledge God's great mercies and favours to their nation. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-63#Verses 7-14|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 63]]
- They beseech him to look down on the abject condition of their once-favoured nation. Would it not be glorious to his name to remove the veil from their hearts, to return to the tribes of his inheritance? [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-63#Verses 15-19|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 63]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
