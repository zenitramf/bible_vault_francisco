# Micah 7

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Micah 7: The general prevalence of wickedness. (1–7). Reliance on God, and triumph over enemies. (8–13). Promises and encouragements for Israel. (14–20).

## Core claims

- The prophet bemoans himself that he lived among a people ripening apace for ruin, in which many good persons would suffer. Men had no comfort, no satisfaction in their own families or in their nearest relations. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/micah/chapter-7#Verses 1–7|Matthew Henry Concise on Micah 7]]
- Those truly penitent for sin, will see great reason to be patient under affliction. When we complain to the Lord of the badness of the times, we ought to complain against ourselves for the badness of our hearts. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/micah/chapter-7#Verses 8–13|Matthew Henry Concise on Micah 7]]
- When God is about to deliver his people, he stirs up their friends to pray for them. Apply spiritually the prophet's prayer to Christ, to take care of his church, as the great Shepherd of the sheep, and to go before them, while they are here in this world as in a wood, in this world but not of it. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/micah/chapter-7#Verses 14–20|Matthew Henry Concise on Micah 7]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
