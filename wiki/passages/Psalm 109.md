# Psalm 109

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 109: David complains of his enemies. (1–5). He prophesies their destruction. (6–20). Prayers and praises. (21–31).

## Core claims

- It is the unspeakable comfort of all believers, that whoever is against them, God is for them; and to him they may apply as to one pleased to concern himself for them. David's enemies laughed at him for his devotion, but they could not laugh him out of it. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/psalms/psalm-109#Verses 1–5|Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 109]]
- The Lord Jesus may speak here as a Judge, denouncing sentence on some of his enemies, to warn others. When men reject the salvation of Christ, even their prayers are numbered among their sins. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/psalms/psalm-109#Verses 6–20|Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 109]]
- The psalmist takes God's comforts to himself, but in a very humble manner. He was troubled in mind. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/psalms/psalm-109#Verses 21–31|Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 109]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
