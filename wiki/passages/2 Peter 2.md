# 2 Peter 2

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Peter 2 cautions believers against false teachers, shows the certainty of their punishment from examples (1–9), describes seducers as exceedingly wicked (10–16), and exposes empty liberty pretences that end in worse entanglement (17–22). McGee’s Notes & Outlines thicken the chapter as **apostasy brought in by false teachers** (not false prophets—those belonged to Israel): marks include denying Christ’s redemption and covetousness; three past types preview last-days rebellion; Lot illustrates God delivering the godly and reserving the unjust; the “prodigal pig” pictures head-knowledge apostates who return to the mire.

## Core claims

- Though the way of error is a hurtful way, many are always ready to walk therein. Let us take care we give no occasion to the enemy to blaspheme the holy name whereby we are called, or to speak evil of the way of salvation by Jesus Christ, who is the Way, the Truth, and the Life. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-peter/chapter-2#Verses 1–9|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Peter 2]]
- Impure seducers and their abandoned followers, give themselves up to their own fleshly minds. Refusing to bring every thought to the obedience of Christ, they act against God's righteous precepts. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-peter/chapter-2#Verses 10–16|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Peter 2]]
- The word of truth is the water of life, which refreshes the souls that receive it; but deceivers spread and promote error, and are set forth as empty, because there is no truth in them. As clouds hinder the light of the sun, so do these darken counsel by words wherein there is no truth. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-peter/chapter-2#Verses 17–22|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Peter 2]]
- The apostle, having in the foregoing chapter exhorted them to proceed and advance in the Christian race, now comes to remove, as much as in him lay, what he could not but apprehend would hinder their complying with his… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/2-peter/chapter-2|Matthew Henry Complete on 2 Peter 2]]
- McGee: false prophets brought heresy to Israel; false teachers are the bearers of heresy and apostasy into the church—one mark is denying Christ’s work of redemption for them. False followers go after false teachers; God’s elect cannot be permanently deceived; 1 Corinthians 11:19 explains why God permits cults. Covetousness is another mark. Three past types of apostates: angels that sinned (rebellion, cf. Psalm 2:1–3); ungodly of Noah’s day who left God out of eating, drinking, and marriage (Matthew 24:37–39); gross immorality of Sodom and Gomorrah (Romans 1:24–32). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Peter.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on 2 Peter]]
- McGee: Genesis does not make clear that Lot was righteous—it implies the opposite—yet Peter calls him righteous; Lot illustrates that God delivers the godly out of temptations and keeps the unjust for judgment. Apostates are total and hopeless, presumptuous beyond angels, like animals. Way of Balaam is covetous willingness to sell the gift for money. They have head knowledge of the truth without receiving a love of the truth. Peter’s parable of the prodigal pig—the sow that was washed returned to the mire (see Jude for fuller apostate description). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Peter.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 2 Peter 2:7–22]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Complete commentary deepens the Concise atlas with fuller pastoral and doctrinal exposition (Phase 4.1).
- McGee agrees with Henry on judgment certainty from OT examples, fleshly seducers, and empty/darkening teachers; he adds the false-teachers-vs-false-prophets distinction, elect-not-permanently-deceived claim, Lot-as-righteous clarification, and prodigal-pig reading.
- Head-knowledge apostasy and washed-sow imagery should not be flattened into loss of true regeneration without multi-source comparison; hold beside Henry and Jude synthesis.
- Concise notes and McGee outlines are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.

## Biblical passages

Primary scope: 2 Peter 2 (full chapter). Supporting refs in McGee: 1 Corinthians 11:19; Psalm 2:1–3; Matthew 24:37–39; Romans 1:24–32; Jude.
