# Ecclesiastes 1

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Ecclesiastes 1: Solomon shows that all human things are vain. (1-3). Man's toil and want of satisfaction. (4-8). There is nothing new. (9-11). The vexation in pursuit of knowledge. (12-18).

## Core claims

- Much is to be learned by comparing one part of Scripture with another. We here behold Solomon returning from the broken and empty cisterns of the world, to the Fountain of living water; recording his own folly and shame, the bitterness of his disappointment, and the lessons he had learned. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ecclesiastes/chapter-1#Verses 1-3|Matthew Henry Concise on Ecclesiastes 1]]
- Men's hearts and their corruptions are the same now as in former times; their desires, and pursuits, and complaints, still the same. This should take us from expecting happiness in the creature, and quicken us to seek eternal blessings. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ecclesiastes/chapter-1#Verses 9-11|Matthew Henry Concise on Ecclesiastes 1]]
- Solomon tried all things, and found them vanity. He found his searches after knowledge weariness, not only to the flesh, but to the mind. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ecclesiastes/chapter-1#Verses 12-18|Matthew Henry Concise on Ecclesiastes 1]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
