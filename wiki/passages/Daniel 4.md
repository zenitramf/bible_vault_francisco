# Daniel 4

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Daniel 4: Nebuchadnezzar acknowledges the power of Jehovah. (1-18). Daniel interprets his dream. (19-27). The fulfilment of it. (28-37).

## Core claims

- The beginning and end of this chapter lead us to hope, that Nebuchadnezzar was a monument of the power of Divine grace, and of the riches of Divine mercy. After he was recovered from his madness, he told to distant places, and wrote down for future ages, how God had justly humbled and graciously restored him. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/daniel/chapter-4#Verses 1-18|Matthew Henry Concise on Daniel 4]]
- Daniel was struck with amazement and terror at so heavy a judgment coming upon so great a prince, and gives advice with tenderness and respect. It is necessary, in repentance, that we not only cease to do evil, but learn to do good. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/daniel/chapter-4#Verses 19-27|Matthew Henry Concise on Daniel 4]]
- Pride and self-conceit are sins that beset great men. They are apt to take that glory to themselves which is due to God only. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/daniel/chapter-4#Verses 28-37|Matthew Henry Concise on Daniel 4]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
