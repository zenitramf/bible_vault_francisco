# Psalm 6

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 6: The psalmist deprecates God's wrath, and begs for the return of his favour. (1–7). He assures himself of an answer of peace. (8–10).

## Core claims

- These verses speak the language of a heart truly humbled, of a broken and contrite spirit under great afflictions, sent to awaken conscience and mortify corruption. Sickness brought sin to his remembrance, and he looked upon it as a token of God's displeasure against him. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/psalms/psalm-6#Verses 1–7|Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 6]]
- What a sudden change is here! Having made his request known to God, the psalmist is confident that his sorrow will be turned into joy. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/psalms/psalm-6#Verses 8–10|Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 6]]
- David was a weeping prophet as well as Jeremiah, and this psalm is one of his lamentations: either it was penned in a time, or at least calculated for a time, of great trouble, both outward and inward.
