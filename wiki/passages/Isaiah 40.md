# Isaiah 40

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 40: The preaching of the gospel, and glad tidings of the coming of Christ. (1-11). The almighty power of God. (12-17). The folly of idolatry. (18-26). Against unbelief. (27-31).

## Core claims

- All human life is a warfare; the Christian life is the most so; but the struggle will not last always. Troubles are removed in love, when sin is pardoned. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-40#Verses 1-11|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 40]]
- Whatever we esteem or love, fear or hope in, more than God, that creature we make equal with God, though we do not make images or worship them. He that is so poor, that he has scarcely a sacrifice to offer, yet will not be without a god of his own. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-40#Verses 18-26|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 40]]
- The people of God are reproved for their unbelief and distrust of God. Let them remember they took the names Jacob and Israel, from one who found God faithful to him in all his straits. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-40#Verses 27-31|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 40]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
