# Ecclesiastes 9

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Ecclesiastes 9: Good and bad men fare alike as to this world. (1-3). All men must die, Their portion as to this life. (4-10). Disappointments common. (11,12) . Benefits of wisdom. (13-18).

## Core claims

- We are not to think our searching into the word or works of God useless, because we cannot explain all difficulties. We may learn many things good for ourselves and useful to others. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ecclesiastes/chapter-9#Verses 1-3|Matthew Henry Concise on Ecclesiastes 9]]
- Men's success seldom equals their expectations. We must use means, but not trust to them: if we succeed, we must give God the praise; if crossed, we must submit to his will. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ecclesiastes/chapter-9#Verses 11,12|Matthew Henry Concise on Ecclesiastes 9]]
- A man may, by his wisdom, bring to pass that which he could never do by his strength. If God be for us, who can be against us, or stand before us? [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ecclesiastes/chapter-9#Verses 13-18|Matthew Henry Concise on Ecclesiastes 9]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
