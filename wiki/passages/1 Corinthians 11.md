# 1 Corinthians 11

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Corinthians 11: public-assembly order; disorders at the Lord’s supper; institution and self-judgment. McGee praises the Corinthians first at v. 2, states three headships for order, ties unveiling in Corinth to prostitution (local application), treats the Lord’s Supper as highest worship, ends disordered agape meals, and stresses self-judgment to avoid chastening.

## Core claims

- Here begin particulars respecting the public assemblies, ch. 1Co 14. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-11#Verses 2–16|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Corinthians 11]]
- The apostle rebukes the disorders in their partaking of the Lord's supper. The ordinances of Christ, if they do not make us better, will be apt to make us worse. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-11#Verses 17–22|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Corinthians 11]]
- The apostle describes the sacred ordinance, of which he had the knowledge by revelation from Christ. As to the visible signs, these are the bread and wine. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-11#Verses 23–34|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Corinthians 11]]
- In this chapter the apostle blames, and endeavours to rectify, some great indecencies and manifest disorders in the church of Corinth; as, I. The misconduct of their women (some of whom seem to have been inspired) in… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/1-corinthians/chapter-11|Matthew Henry Complete on 1 Corinthians 11]]
- McGee: first praise in the letter at v. 2; principle of authority for order—head of every man is Christ, head of woman is man, head of Christ is God (redemptive subordination, Phil 2:5–8); unveiled woman in Corinth signaled prostitution—“your town may be different”; public ministry dress must not use sex appeal. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 1 Corinthians 11:2–16]]
- McGee: Lord’s Supper is highest expression of Christian worship; early agape meal discontinued because of disorders; Paul received the institution directly; simplicity, sublimity, and sanity of the Supper; self-judgment regarding sins prevents the Lord’s judgment and chastening. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 1 Corinthians 11:17–34]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Henry and McGee both correct assembly indecency and Supper abuse and ground the ordinance in Christ’s institution.
- McGee’s head-covering application is explicitly contextualized to Corinthian prostitution signals; do not universalize cultural form without further sources.
- Complete commentary deepens Concise atlas (Phase 4.1).
