# Isaiah 50

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 50: The rejection of the Jews. (1-3). The sufferings and exaltation of the Messiah. (4-9). Consolation to the believer, and warning to the unbeliever. (10,11).

## Core claims

- Those who have professed to be people of God, and seem to be dealt severely with, are apt to complain, as if God had been hard with them. Here is an answer for such murmurings; God never deprived any of their advantages, except for their sins. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-50#Verses 1-3|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 50]]
- As Jesus was God and man in one person, we find him sometimes speaking, or spoken of, as the Lord God; at other times, as man and the servant of Jehovah. He was to declare the truths which comfort the broken, contrite heart, those weary of sin, harassed with afflictions. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-50#Verses 4-9|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 50]]
- A child of God is afraid of incurring his displeasure. This grace usually appears most in believers when in darkness, when other graces appear not. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-50#Verses 10,11|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 50]]
