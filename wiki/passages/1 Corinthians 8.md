# 1 Corinthians 8

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Corinthians 8: The danger of having a high conceit of knowledge. (1–6). The mischief of offending weak brethren. (7–13).

## Core claims

- There is no proof of ignorance more common than conceit of knowledge. Much may be known, when nothing is known to good purpose. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-8#Verses 1–6|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Corinthians 8]]
- Eating one kind of food, and abstaining from another, have nothing in them to recommend a person to God. But the apostle cautions against putting a stumbling-block in the way of the weak; lest they be made bold to eat what was offered to the idol, not as common food, but as a sacrifice, and thereby be guilty of idol… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-corinthians/chapter-8#Verses 7–13|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Corinthians 8]]
