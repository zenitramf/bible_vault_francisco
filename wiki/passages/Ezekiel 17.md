# Ezekiel 17

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Ezekiel 17: A parable relative to the Jewish nation. (1-10). to which an explanation is added. (11-21). A direct promise of the Messiah. (22-24).

## Core claims

- Mighty conquerors are aptly likened to birds or beasts of prey, but their destructive passions are overruled to forward God's designs. Those who depart from God, only vary their crimes by changing one carnal confidence for another, and never will prosper. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ezekiel/chapter-17#Verses 1-10|Matthew Henry Concise on Ezekiel 17]]
- The parable is explained, and the particulars of the history of the Jewish nation at that time may be traced. Zedekiah had been ungrateful to his benefactor, which is a sin against God. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ezekiel/chapter-17#Verses 11-21|Matthew Henry Concise on Ezekiel 17]]
- The unbelief of man shall not make the promise of God of none effect. The parable of a tree, used in the threatening, is here presented in the promise. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ezekiel/chapter-17#Verses 22-24|Matthew Henry Concise on Ezekiel 17]]
