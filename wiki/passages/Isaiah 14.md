# Isaiah 14

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 14: The destruction of Babylon, and the death of its proud monarch. (1-23). Assurance of the destruction of Assyria. (24-27). The destruction of the Philistines. (28-32).

## Core claims

- The whole plan of Divine Providence is arranged with a view to the good of the people of God. A settlement in the land of promise is of God's mercy. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-14#Verses 1-23|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 14]]
- Let those that make themselves a yoke and a burden to God's people, see what they are to expect. Let those that are the called according to God's purpose, comfort themselves, that whatever God has purposed, it shall stand. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-14#Verses 24-27|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 14]]
- Assurance is given of the destruction of the Philistines and their power, by famine and war. Hezekiah would be more terrible to them than Uzziah had been. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-14#Verses 28-32|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 14]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
