## Core claims

- Christianity teaches men to be joyful under troubles: such exercises are sent from God's love; and trials in the way of duty will brighten our graces now, and our crown at last. - It is not every man who suffers, that is blessed; but he who with patience and constancy goes through all difficulties in the way of duty. Afflictions cannot make us miserable, if it be not our own fault. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/james/chapter-1#Verses 12–18|Matthew Henry Concise on James 1]]
- Instead of blaming God under our trials, let us open our ears and hearts to learn what he teaches by them. And if men would govern their tongues, they must govern their passions. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/james/chapter-1#Verses 19–21|Matthew Henry Concise on James 1]]
- If we heard a sermon every day of the week, and an angel from heaven were the preacher, yet, if we rested in hearing only, it would never bring us to heaven. Mere hearers are self-deceivers; and self-deceit will be found the worst deceit at last. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/james/chapter-1#Verses 22–25|Matthew Henry Concise on James 1]]
- When men take more pains to seem religious than really to be so, it is a sign their religion is in vain. The not bridling the tongue, readiness to speak of the faults of others, or to lessen their wisdom and piety, are signs of a vain religion.
