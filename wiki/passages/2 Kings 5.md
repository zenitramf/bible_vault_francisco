# 2 Kings 5

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Kings 5: Naaman's leprosy. (1–8). The cure of it. (9–14). Elisha refuses Naaman's gifts. (15–19). Gehazi's covetousness and falsehood. (20–27).

## Core claims

- Though the Syrians were idolaters, and oppressed God's people, yet the deliverance of which Naaman had been the means, is here ascribed to the Lord. Such is the correct language of Scripture, while those who write common history, plainly show that God is not in all their thoughts. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-kings/chapter-5#Verses 1–8|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Kings 5]]
- The mercy of the cure affected Naaman more than the miracle. Those are best able to speak of the power of Divine grace, who themselves experience it. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-kings/chapter-5#Verses 15–19|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Kings 5]]
- Naaman, a Syrian, a courtier, a soldier, had many servants, and we read how wise and good they were. Elisha, a holy prophet, a man of God, has but one servant, and he proves a base liar. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-kings/chapter-5#Verses 20–27|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Kings 5]]
- Two more of Elisha's miracles are recorded in this chapter. I. The cleansing of Naaman, a Syrian, a stranger, from his leprosy, and there, 1. The badness of his case, 2Ki 5:1. 2.
