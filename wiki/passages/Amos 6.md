# Amos 6

## Summary

Israel is admonished in the present: ease in luxury, pride, and sensuality invite sworn ruin. Henry describes pride, security, and sensuality; McGee names three national sins—gluttony, heathen music, drunkenness—done apart from God in a day of affluence, and warns of captivity for both nations.

## Core claims

- Those are looked upon as doing well for themselves, who do well for their bodies; but we are here told what their ease is, and what their woe is. Here is a description of the pride, security, and sensuality, for which God would reckon. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/amos/chapter-6#Verses 1–7|Matthew Henry Concise on Amos 6]]
- How dreadful, how miserable, is the case of those whose eternal ruin the Lord himself has sworn; for he can execute his purpose, and none can alter it! ## Agreements and tensions

Complete deepens Concise (Phase 4.1). McGee’s three-sin catalog and “godless nation” line thicken Henry’s luxury-and-false-security pastoral reading for [[wiki/concepts/Justice|Justice]] and [[wiki/concepts/Holiness|Holiness]].
