# Job 29

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Job 29: Job's former comforts. (1–6). The honour paid to Job, His usefulness. (7–17). His prospect of prosperity. (18–25).

## Core claims

- Job proceeds to contrast his former prosperity with his present misery, through God's withdrawing from him. A gracious soul delights in God's smiles, not in the smiles of this world. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-29#Verses 1–6|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 29]]
- All sorts of people paid respect to Job, not only for the dignity of his rank, but for his personal merit, his prudence, integrity, and good management. Happy the men who are blessed with such gifts as these! [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-29#Verses 7–17|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 29]]
- Being thus honoured and useful, Job had hoped to die in peace and honour, in a good old age. If such an expectation arise from lively faith in the providence and promise of God, it is well; but if from conceit of our own wisdom, and dependence on changeable, earthly things, it is ill grounded, and turns to sin. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-29#Verses 18–25|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 29]]
- After that excellent discourse concerning wisdom in the foregoing chapter Job sat down and paused awhile, not because he had talked himself out of breath, but because he would not, without the leave of the company,… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-3/job/chapter-29|Matthew Henry Complete on Job 29]]
