# 1 Peter 4

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Peter 4: The consideration of Christ's sufferings is urged for purity and holiness. (1–6). And the approaching end of the Jewish state, as a reason for sobriety, watchfulness, and prayer. (7–11). Believers encouraged to rejoice under the fiery trial. McGee frames the chapter as suffering producing **obedience to the will of God**: arming with Christ’s mind restrains from sin; the unbeliever’s old life is left; the Lord’s coming incentives prayer, fervent love, and Spirit-powered gifts; fiery trial is normal Christian experience and fellowship in Christ’s sufferings; suffer as a Christian, not as a sinner; judgment begins at the house of God.

## Agreements and tensions

Complete commentary on this chapter deepens the Concise atlas with fuller pastoral and doctrinal exposition (Phase 4.1). Henry’s Concise ties “the end” strongly to the approaching fall of the Jewish state; McGee emphasizes the Lord’s coming, judgment seat, and gifts under trial—complementary pastoral angles. Both treat fiery trial as expected for Christians and center Christ’s sufferings as the pattern and comfort. Concise notes and McGee outlines are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
