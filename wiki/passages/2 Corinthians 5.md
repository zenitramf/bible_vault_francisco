# 2 Corinthians 5

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Corinthians 5: The apostle's hope and desire of heavenly glory. (1–8). This excited to diligence. The reasons of his being affected with zeal for the Corinthians. (9–15). The necessity of regeneration, and of reconciliation with God…. McGee titles the chapter God’s comfort in the ministry of martyrdom: tent-body, home with the Lord, *bema* for works, and reconciliation as God already reconciled in Christ.

## Core claims

- The believer not only is well assured by faith that there is another and a happy life after this is ended, but he has good hope, through grace, of heaven as a dwelling-place, a resting-place, a hiding-place. In our Father's house there are many mansions, whose Builder and Maker is God. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-corinthians/chapter-5#Verses 1–8|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Corinthians 5]]
- The apostle quickens himself and others to acts of duty. Well-grounded hopes of heaven will not encourage sloth and sinful security. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-corinthians/chapter-5#Verses 9–15|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Corinthians 5]]
- The renewed man acts upon new principles, by new rules, with new ends, and in new company. The believer is created anew; his heart is not merely set right, but a new heart is given him. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-corinthians/chapter-5#Verses 16–21|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Corinthians 5]]
- The apostle proceeds in showing the reasons why they did not faint under their afflictions, namely, their expectation, desire, and assurance of happiness after death (2Co 5:1-5), and deduces an inference for the… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/2-corinthians/chapter-5|Matthew Henry Complete on 2 Corinthians 5]]
- McGee: physical death is departure from the tent-body; temporary suffering bodies; leave fragile bodies to go home with the Lord (vv. 1–8). Believers appear at the *bema* of Christ for reward or not—works judged (vv. 9–13). Secondary (major) theme is reconciliation: believers joined to glorified Christ; God is already reconciled by Christ’s work; gospel calls us to accept God’s message and method of reconciliation, not to reconcile God by our doing (vv. 14–21). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf|McGee Notes & Outlines on 2 Corinthians]] [[wiki/source-notes/McGee Notes and Outlines on 2 Corinthians|McGee source-note]]

## Agreements and tensions

Henry stresses heavenly hope, diligence, and new creation; McGee sharpens *bema* as works-reward and “God already reconciled” gospel logic. Hold *bema* distinct from final judgment of the lost; McGee’s martyrdom title is outline framing for death/home-going, not a claim that all in view are martyrs.
