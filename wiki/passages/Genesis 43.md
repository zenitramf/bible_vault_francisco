# Genesis 43

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 43: Jacob is persuaded to send Benjamin into Egypt. (1–14). Joseph's reception of his brethren, their fears. (15–25). Joseph makes a feast for his brethren. (26–34).

## Core claims

- Jacob urges his sons to go and buy a little food; now, in time of dearth, a little must suffice. Judah urges that Benjamin should go with them. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/genesis/chapter-43#Verses 1–14|Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 43]]
- Jacob's sons went down the second time into Egypt to buy corn. If we should ever know what a famine of the word means, let us not think it much to travel as far for spiritual food, as they did for bodily food. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/genesis/chapter-43#Verses 15–25|Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 43]]
- Observe the great respect Joseph's brethren paid to him. Thus were Joseph's dreams more and more fulfilled. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/genesis/chapter-43#Verses 26–34|Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 43]]
- Here the story of Joseph's brethren is carried on, and very particularly related. I. Their melancholy parting with their father Jacob in Canaan, Ge 43:1-14. II. Their pleasant meeting with Joseph in Egypt, Ge 43:15-34,… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-1/genesis/chapter-43|Matthew Henry Complete on Genesis 43]]
