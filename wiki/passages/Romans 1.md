# Romans 1

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise reads Romans 1 as opening the epistle’s design: all flesh is charged with sinfulness, deliverance is only by faith in God’s mercy through Jesus Christ, and the gospel reveals a righteousness that runs from faith to faith—not from faith to works. Gentile ungodliness and unrighteousness leave humanity without excuse, because creation reveals God’s power and Godhead while people suppress the truth.

## Core claims

- The Christian profession is obedience of faith, not bare notional assent; those effectually called of Jesus Christ are beloved of God and called to be saints. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-1#Verses 1–7|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 1]]
- The gospel’s design charges all flesh with sinfulness and declares the only deliverance from condemnation: faith in the mercy of God through Jesus Christ. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-1#Verses 16, 17|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 1]]
- The righteousness made known in the gospel is Christ’s righteousness, a gracious method of acceptance despite guilt; faith is all in all in the beginning and progress of Christian life, from faith to faith rather than from faith to works. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-1#Verses 16, 17|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 1]]
- None can obtain God’s favor or escape wrath by their own works; sinfulness includes ungodliness against God and unrighteousness against neighbor, rooted in holding the truth in unrighteousness. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-1#Verses 18–25|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 1]]
- Creator’s invisible power and Godhead are clearly shown in the works he has made, so that even idolaters are without excuse when they exchange the Creator’s glory for idols. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-1#Verses 18–25|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 1]]
- Being given up to lusts is a severe slavery; the charges apply more or less to human nature until full submission to the faith of Christ and renewal by divine power. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-1#Verses 26–32|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 1]]
