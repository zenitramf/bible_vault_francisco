# 2 Samuel 5

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Samuel 5: David king over all Israel. (1–5). He takes the strong-hold of Zion. (6–10). David's kingdom established. (11–16). He defeats the Philistines. (17–25).

## Core claims

- David was anointed king a third time. His advances were gradual, that his faith might be tried, and that he might gain experience. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-samuel/chapter-5#Verses 1–5|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Samuel 5]]
- David's house was not the worse, nor the less fit to be dedicated to God, for being built by the sons of the stranger. It is prophesied of the gospel church, The sons of strangers shall build up thy walls, and their kings shall minister unto thee, Isa 60:10. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-samuel/chapter-5#Verses 11–16|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Samuel 5]]
- The Philistines considered not that David had the presence of God with him, which Saul had forfeited and lost. The kingdom of the Messiah, as soon as it was set up in the world, was thus attacked by the powers of darkness. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-samuel/chapter-5#Verses 17–25|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Samuel 5]]
- How far Abner's deserting the house of Saul, his murder, and the murder of Ish-bosheth, might contribute to the perfecting of the revolution, and the establishing of David as king over all Israel, does not appear; but,… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-2/2-samuel/chapter-5|Matthew Henry Complete on 2 Samuel 5]]
