# Isaiah 49

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 49: The unbelief and rejection of the Jews. (1-6). Gracious promise to the Gentiles. (7-12). God's love to the church. (13-17). Its increase. (18-23). And deliverance. (24-26).

## Core claims

- The great Author of redemption shows the authority for his work. The sword of his word slays the lusts of his people, and all at enmity with them. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-49#Verses 1-6|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 49]]
- Let there be universal joy, for God will have mercy upon the afflicted, because of his compassion; upon his afflicted, because of his covenant. We have no more reason to question his promise and grace, than we have to question his providence and justice. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-49#Verses 13-17|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 49]]
- We were lawful captives to the justice of God, yet delivered by a price of unspeakable value. Here is an express promise: Even the prey of the terrible shall be delivered. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-49#Verses 24-26|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 49]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
