# Romans 8:26–27

## Summary

Believers often do not know what to pray for as they ought. The Spirit helps their infirmities and intercedes with groanings that cannot be uttered, according to the will of God. The passage emphasizes divine aid in prayer rather than human eloquence or a fixed petition form. Henry Concise places the help inside the larger Romans 8 security of those who walk after the Spirit; Spurgeon expands the experiential texture of that help.

## Core claims

- Believers often do not know what to pray for as they ought; the Spirit helps their infirmities. - The Spirit intercedes with groanings that cannot be uttered, aligning prayer with God's will. [[sources/commentaries_english/chspurgeon-sermons/volume-26/sermon_1532|Spurgeon, The Holy Spirit's Intercession]] [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/romans/chapter-8#Verses 26, 27|Matthew Henry Concise on Romans 8]]

## Agreements and tensions

Spurgeon's treatment stresses the Spirit's interior aid in weakness more than the form and pattern of petition developed from Matthew 6. Henry Concise is briefer but agrees on help in infirmity and prayer according to God's will within the no-condemnation / adoption chapter. Both strands belong to a full doctrine of Christian prayer.
