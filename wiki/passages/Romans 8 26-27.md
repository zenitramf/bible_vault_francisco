# Romans 8:26–27

## Summary

Believers often do not know what to pray for as they ought. The Spirit helps their infirmities and intercedes with groanings that cannot be uttered, according to the will of God. The passage emphasizes divine aid in prayer rather than human eloquence or a fixed petition form.

## Core claims

- Believers often do not know what to pray for as they ought; the Spirit helps their infirmities. [[sources/commentaries_english/chspurgeon-sermons/volume-26/sermon_1532|Spurgeon, The Holy Spirit's Intercession]]
- The Spirit intercedes with groanings that cannot be uttered, aligning prayer with God's will. [[sources/commentaries_english/chspurgeon-sermons/volume-26/sermon_1532|Spurgeon, The Holy Spirit's Intercession]]

## Agreements and tensions

Spurgeon's treatment stresses the Spirit's interior aid in weakness more than the form and pattern of petition developed from Matthew 6. Both strands belong to a full doctrine of Christian prayer. [[wiki/concepts/Prayer|Prayer]]
