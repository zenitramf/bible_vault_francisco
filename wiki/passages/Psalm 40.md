# Psalm 40

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 40: Confidence for deliverance. (1–5). Christ's work of redemption. (6–10). Prayer for mercy and grace. (11–17).

## Core claims

- Doubts and fears about the eternal state, are a horrible pit and miry clay, and have been so to many a dear child of God. There is power enough in God to help the weakest, and grace enough to help the unworthiest of all that trust in him. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/psalms/psalm-40#Verses 1–5|Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 40]]
- The psalmist foretells that work of wonder, redemption by our Lord Jesus Christ. The Substance must come, which is Christ, who must bring that glory to God, and that grace to man, which it was impossible the sacrifices should ever do. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/psalms/psalm-40#Verses 6–10|Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 40]]
- The best saints see themselves undone, unless continually preserved by the grace of God. But see the frightful view the psalmist had of sin. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/psalms/psalm-40#Verses 11–17|Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 40]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
