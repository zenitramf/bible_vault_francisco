# 2 Kings 1

## Summary

Matthew Henry Complete on 2 Kings 1: Ahaziah, Ahab’s son, dies within two years after a fall through a lattice in Samaria. He sends to Baal-zebub of Ekron to ask whether he will recover; Elijah intercepts the messengers with the rebuke “Is it not because there is not a God in Israel?” and a death sentence. Two captains of fifty sent to seize Elijah are consumed by fire from heaven; the third humbles himself and is spared; Elijah goes down and delivers the sentence in person, which is fulfilled. McGee frames the chapter as Elijah’s last public act: Jezebel-influenced inquiry to Ekron, fire on two detachments, and personal delivery of the fulfilled death message with the third.

## Core claims

- Ahaziah, genuine son and successor of Ahab, reigns under two years; after Moab’s revolt (2 Ki 1:1) he falls through a lattice in his upper chamber in Samaria and is sick. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-2/2-kings/chapter-1|Matthew Henry Complete on 2 Kings 1]]
- In distress he foolishly and wickedly sends messengers to inquire of Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron, whether he will recover—honoring a Philistine dunghill-deity instead of the God of Israel. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-2/2-kings/chapter-1|Henry on Ahaziah’s inquiry]]
- The angel of the LORD sends Elijah to meet the messengers: “Is it not because there is not a God in Israel…?” Practical atheism is the construction put on seeking Ekron; the sentence is sure death—he shall not come down from that bed. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-2/2-kings/chapter-1|Henry on Elijah’s message]]
- Ahaziah, unsoftened by sickness, issues warrants for Elijah. Two captains of fifty command the “man of God” to come down; Elijah calls fire from heaven and both companies are consumed—not for private revenge but to prove mission and reveal wrath. The third captain kneels, begs that life be precious, and is spared; Elijah goes with him and speaks the sentence to the king’s face. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-2/2-kings/chapter-1|Henry on fire and the third captain]]
- The prediction is fulfilled: Ahaziah dies; childless, he is succeeded by his brother Jehoram. Henry notes that external alarms often exasperate secure sinners rather than convert them, and that contending with God is vain—submission is the wise path. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-2/2-kings/chapter-1|Henry on fulfillment]]
- McGee: Ahaziah, son of Ahab and Jezebel, falls through a lattice and is seriously injured; greatly influenced by Jezebel, he sends to Ekron to inquire of Baal whether he would be healed. Elijah’s last public act is the stern rebuke and death sentence; fire destroys two detachments of fifty; Elijah goes with the third and personally delivers the death message—which was fulfilled. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on 1–2 Kings]] [[wiki/source-notes/McGee Notes and Outlines on 1 and 2 Kings|McGee Kings source-note]]

## Agreements and tensions

Henry and McGee agree on the narrative spine: lattice injury, Baal-zebub inquiry, Elijah’s rebuke and death sentence, fire on two fifties, mercy to the third captain, personal delivery, and fulfillment. Henry develops pastoral and theological applications (practical atheism, consulting the devil, OT fire vs Luke 9:54–55 “you know not what spirit you are of,” hardness of Ahaziah’s heart). McGee’s radio outline is leaner and flags this as Elijah’s last public act before the translation in chapter 2—use both without flattening Henry’s fuller moral reading into McGee’s brief notes.
