# Acts 11

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Acts 11: Peter's defence. (1–18). The success of the gospel at Antioch. (19–24). The disciples named Christians, Relief sent to Judea. (25–30).

## Core claims

- The imperfect state of human nature strongly appears, when godly persons are displeased even to hear that the word of God has been received, because their own system has not been attended to. And we are too apt to despair of doing good to those who yet, when tried, prove very teachable. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/acts/chapter-11#Verses 1–18|Matthew Henry Concise on Acts 11]]
- The first preachers of the gospel at Antioch, were dispersed from Jerusalem by persecution; thus what was meant to hurt the church, was made to work for its good. The wrath of man is made to praise God. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/acts/chapter-11#Verses 19–24|Matthew Henry Concise on Acts 11]]
- Hitherto the followers of Christ were called disciples, that is, learners, scholars; but from that time they were called Christians. The proper meaning of this name is, a follower of Christ; it denotes one who, from serious thought, embraces the religion of Christ, believes his promises, and makes it his chief care … [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/acts/chapter-11#Verses 25–30|Matthew Henry Concise on Acts 11]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
