# 2 Timothy 4

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Timothy 4: The apostle solemnly charges Timothy to be diligent, though many will not bear sound doctrine. (1–5). Enforces the charge from his own martyrdom, then at hand. (6–8). Desires him to come speedily. (9–13). He cautions,…. McGee calls “Preach the word” the rallying cry for last-days believers and reads 4:6–8 as Paul’s deathbed epitaph (soldier, athlete, steward) with a crown for those who love Christ’s appearing.

## Core claims

- People will turn away from the truth, they will grow weary of the plain gospel of Christ, they will be greedy of fables, and take pleasure in them. People do so when they will not endure that preaching which is searching, plain, and to the purpose. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-timothy/chapter-4#Verses 1–5|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Timothy 4]]
- The blood of the martyrs, though not a sacrifice of atonement, yet was a sacrifice of acknowledgment to the grace of God and his truth. Death to a good man, is his release from the imprisonment of this world, and his departure to the enjoyments of another world. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-timothy/chapter-4#Verses 6–8|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Timothy 4]]
- The love of this world, is often the cause of turning back from the truths and ways of Jesus Christ. Paul was guided by Divine inspiration, yet he would have his books. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-timothy/chapter-4#Verses 9–13|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Timothy 4]]
- There is as much danger from false brethren, as from open enemies. It is dangerous having to do with those who would be enemies to such a man as Paul. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-timothy/chapter-4#Verses 14–18|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Timothy 4]]
- We need no more to make us happy, than to have the Lord Jesus Christ with our spirits; for in him all spiritual blessings are summed up. It is the best prayer we can offer for our friends, that the Lord Jesus Christ may be with their spirits, to sanctify and save them, and at last to receive them to himself. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-timothy/chapter-4#Verses 19–22|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Timothy 4]]
- In this chapter, I. Paul with great solemnity and earnestness presses Timothy to the diligent and conscientious discharge of his work and office as an evangelist; and the charge given to him all gospel ministers are to… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/2-timothy/chapter-4|Matthew Henry Complete on 2 Timothy 4]]
- “Preach the word” is the rallying cry for believers in the last days; the church is to give out the Word—ready, convicting, rebuking, comforting, with teaching. [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#C. Instructions for the last days, Chapter 4:1–5|McGee on 2 Timothy 4:2]]
- Hearers will heap teachers to themselves with itching ears; the demand creates the supply, and people turn from truth to fables (cults, isms, showmanship). [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#C. Instructions for the last days, Chapter 4:1–5|McGee on 2 Timothy 4:3–4]]
- Paul writes his own epitaph: poured out as a drink offering, departure as a ship leaving harbor; he fought the good fight as a soldier, finished the course as an athlete, kept the faith as a steward; the crown of righteousness is for those who love his appearing (Rapture). [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#A. Deathbed testimony of Paul, vv. 6–8|McGee on 2 Timothy 4:6–8]]
- At Paul’s first defense the Lord stood with him so that all the Gentiles might hear; the Lord will preserve him unto his heavenly kingdom. [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#B. Last words, vv. 9–22 ("The Lord stood with me.")|McGee on 2 Timothy 4:16–18]]

## Agreements and tensions

Complete commentary on this chapter deepens the Concise atlas with fuller pastoral and doctrinal exposition (Phase 4.1). McGee agrees with Henry on the charge to preach and on Paul’s near-martyrdom; he layers a distinct Rapture reading of “appearing” and a three-figure epitaph. Henry Concise, Henry Complete, and McGee Notes & Outlines now share this hub.
