# James 3

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on James 3: Cautions against proud behaviour, and the mischief of an unruly tongue. (1–12). The excellence of heavenly wisdom, in opposition to that which is worldly. (13–18).

## Core claims

- We are taught to dread an unruly tongue, as one of the greatest evils. The affairs of mankind are thrown into confusion by the tongues of men. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/james/chapter-3#Verses 1–12|Matthew Henry Concise on James 3]]
- These verses show the difference between men's pretending to be wise, and their being really so. He who thinks well, or he who talks well, is not wise in the sense of the Scripture, if he does not live and act well. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/james/chapter-3#Verses 13–18|Matthew Henry Concise on James 3]]
- The apostle here reproves ambition, and an arrogant magisterial tongue; and shows the duty and advantage of bridling it because of its power to do mischief.
