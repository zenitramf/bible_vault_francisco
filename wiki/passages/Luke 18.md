# Luke 18

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Luke 18: The parable of the importunate widow. (1–8). The Pharisee and the publican. (9–14). Children brought to Christ. (15–17). The ruler hindered by his riches. (18–30). Christ foreshows his death. (31–34). A blind man rest….

## Core claims

- All God's people are praying people. Here earnest steadiness in prayer for spiritual mercies is taught. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/luke/chapter-18#Verses 1–8|Matthew Henry Concise on Luke 18]]
- This parable was to convince some who trusted in themselves that they were righteous, and despised others. God sees with what disposition and design we come to him in holy ordinances. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/luke/chapter-18#Verses 9–14|Matthew Henry Concise on Luke 18]]
- None are too little, too young, to be brought to Christ, who knows how to show kindness to those not capable of doing service to him. It is the mind of Christ, that little children should be brought to him. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/luke/chapter-18#Verses 15–17|Matthew Henry Concise on Luke 18]]
- Many have a great deal in them very commendable, yet perish for lack of some one thing; so this ruler could not bear Christ's terms, which would part between him and his estate. Many who are loth to leave Christ, yet do leave him. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/luke/chapter-18#Verses 18–30|Matthew Henry Concise on Luke 18]]
- This poor blind man sat by the wayside, begging. He was not only blind, but poor, the fitter emblem of the world of mankind which Christ came to heal and save. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/luke/chapter-18#Verses 35–43|Matthew Henry Concise on Luke 18]]
- In this chapter we have, I. The parable of the importunate widow, designed to teach us fervency in prayer, Lu 18:1-8. II.
