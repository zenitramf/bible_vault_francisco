## Core claims

- Here is a parable or similitude, taken from the customs of the East, in the management of sheep. Men, as creatures depending on their Creator, are called the sheep of his pasture. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/john/chapter-10#Verses 1–5|Matthew Henry Concise on John 10]]
- Many who hear the word of Christ, do not understand it, because they will not. But we shall find one scripture expounding another, and the blessed Spirit making known the blessed Jesus. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/john/chapter-10#Verses 6–9|Matthew Henry Concise on John 10]]
- Christ is a good Shepherd; many who were not thieves, yet were careless in their duty, and by their neglect the flock was much hurt. Bad principles are the root of bad practices. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/john/chapter-10#Verses 10–18|Matthew Henry Concise on John 10]]
- Satan ruins many, by putting them out of conceit with the word and ordinances. Men would not be laughed out of their necessary food, yet suffer themselves thus to be laughed out of what is far more necessary. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/john/chapter-10#Verses 19–21|Matthew Henry Concise on John 10]]
- No weapon formed against our Lord Jesus shall prosper. He escaped, not because he was afraid to suffer, but because his hour was not come. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/john/chapter-10#Verses 39–42|Matthew Henry Concise on John 10]]
- In this chapter we have, I. Christ's parabolical discourse concerning himself as the door of the sheepfold, and the shepherd of the sheep, Joh 10:1-18. II.
