# Psalm 88

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 88: The psalmist pours out his soul to God in lamentation. (1–9). He wrestles by faith, in his prayer to God for comfort. (10–18).

## Core claims

- The first words of the psalmist are the only words of comfort and support in this psalm. Thus greatly may good men be afflicted, and such dismal thoughts may they have about their afflictions, and such dark conclusion may they make about their end, through the power of melancholy and the weakness of faith. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/psalms/psalm-88#Verses 1–9|Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 88]]
- Departed souls may declare God's faithfulness, justice, and lovingkindness; but deceased bodies can neither receive God's favours in comfort, nor return them in praise. The psalmist resolved to continue in prayer, and the more so, because deliverance did not come speedily. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/psalms/psalm-88#Verses 10–18|Matthew Henry Concise on Psalm 88]]
- This psalm is a lamentation, one of the most melancholy of all the psalms; and it does not conclude, as usually the melancholy psalms do, with the least intimation of comfort or joy, but, from first to last, it is… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-3/psalms/psalm-88|Matthew Henry Complete on Psalm 88]]
