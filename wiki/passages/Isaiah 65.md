# Isaiah 65

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 65: The calling of the Gentiles, and the rejection of the Jews. (1-7). The Lord would preserve a remnant. (8-10). Judgments upon the wicked. (11-16). The future happy and flourishing state of the church. (17-25).

## Core claims

- The Gentiles came to seek God, and find him, because they were first sought and found of him. Often he meets some thoughtless trifler or profligate opposer, and says to him, Behold me; and a speedy change takes place. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-65#Verses 1-7|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 65]]
- Here the different states of the godly and wicked, of the Jews who believed, and of those who persisted in unbelief, are set against one another. They prepared a table for that troop of deities which the heathen worship, and poured out drink-offerings to that countless number. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-65#Verses 11-16|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 65]]
- In the grace and comfort believers have in and from Christ, we are to look for this new heaven and new earth. The former confusions, sins and miseries of the human race, shall be no more remembered or renewed. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-65#Verses 17-25|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 65]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
