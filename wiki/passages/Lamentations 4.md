# Lamentations 4

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Lamentations 4: The deplorable state of the nation is contrasted with its ancient prosperity. (1-22).

## Core claims

- What a change is here! Sin tarnishes the beauty of the most exalted powers and the most excellent gifts; but that gold, tried in the fire, which Christ bestows, never will be taken from us; its outward appearance may be dimmed, but its real value can never be changed. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/lamentations/chapter-4#Verses 1-12|Matthew Henry Concise on Lamentations 4]]
- Nothing ripens a people more for ruin, nor fills the measure faster, than the sins of priests and prophets. The king himself cannot escape, for Divine vengeance pursues him. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/lamentations/chapter-4#Verses 13-20|Matthew Henry Concise on Lamentations 4]]
- Here it is foretold that an end should be put to Zion's troubles. Not the fulness of punishment deserved, but of what God has determined to inflict. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/lamentations/chapter-4#Verses 21,22|Matthew Henry Concise on Lamentations 4]]
- This chapter is another single alphabet of Lamentations for the destruction of Jerusalem, like those in the first two chapters. I.
