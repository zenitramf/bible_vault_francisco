# 2 Timothy 3

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Timothy 3: The apostle foretells the rise of dangerous enemies to the gospel. (1–9). Proposes his own example to Timothy. (10–13). And exhorts him to continue in the doctrine he had learned from the Holy Scriptures. (14–17). McGee lists nineteen marks of the last days, ties godliness to persecution, and insists the God-breathed Scriptures are the believer’s only recourse in apostasy.

## Core claims

- Even in gospel times there would be perilous times; on account of persecution from without, still more on account of corruptions within. Men love to gratify their own lusts, more than to please God and do their duty. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-timothy/chapter-3#Verses 1–9|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Timothy 3]]
- The more fully we know the doctrine of Christ, as taught by the apostles, the more closely we shall cleave to it. When we know the afflictions of believers only in part, they tempt us to decline the cause for which they suffer. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-timothy/chapter-3#Verses 10–13|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Timothy 3]]
- Those who would learn the things of God, and be assured of them, must know the Holy Scriptures, for they are the Divine revelation. The age of children is the age to learn; and those who would get true learning, must get it out of the Scriptures. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-timothy/chapter-3#Verses 14–17|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Timothy 3]]
- I\. The apostle forewarns Timothy what the last days would be, with the reasons thereof, 2Ti 3:1-9. II. Prescribes various remedies against them (2Ti 3:10-17), particularly his own example ("But thou hast fully known… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/2-timothy/chapter-3|Matthew Henry Complete on 2 Timothy 3]]
- “Last days” means the last days of the church; perilous times are grievous or hard; nineteen descriptors climax in having a form of godliness but denying its power—from such turn away. [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#A. Conditions in the last days, Chapter 3:1–9|McGee on 2 Timothy 3:1–5]]
- Godliness and persecution go together as an axiom of Christian life; there is no escaping. [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#B. Authority of Scriptures in the last days, Chapter 3:10–17|McGee on 2 Timothy 3:12]]
- Against this dark background the believer’s only help is the Scriptures; “unto salvation” includes the life that issues from being saved—the *modus operandi* and *modus vivendi* of Christian living. [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#B. Authority of Scriptures in the last days, Chapter 3:10–17|McGee on 2 Timothy 3:14–15]]
- Every passage of Scripture is God-breathed and profitable for teaching, conviction, setting right, and discipline, so that the man of God may be complete and fitted out for every good work. [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#B. Authority of Scriptures in the last days, Chapter 3:10–17|McGee on 2 Timothy 3:16–17]]

## Agreements and tensions

Complete commentary on this chapter deepens the Concise atlas with fuller pastoral and doctrinal exposition (Phase 4.1). McGee agrees with Henry that Scriptures are the divine remedy in corrupt times; he adds a sharper apostasy definition and a nineteen-point last-days catalogue. Henry Concise, Henry Complete, and McGee Notes & Outlines now share this hub.
