# Ezekiel 21

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Ezekiel 21: The ruin of Judah under the emblem of a sharp sword. (1-17). The approach of the king of Babylon described. (18-27). The destruction of the Ammonites. (28-32).

## Core claims

- Here is an explanation of the parable in the last chapter. It is declared that the Lord was about to cut off Jerusalem and the whole land, that all might know it was his decree against a wicked and rebellious people. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ezekiel/chapter-21#Verses 1-17|Matthew Henry Concise on Ezekiel 21]]
- By the Spirit of prophecy Ezekiel foresaw Nebuchadnezzar's march from Babylon, which he would determine by divination. The Lord would overturn the government of Judah, till the coming of Him whose right it is. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ezekiel/chapter-21#Verses 18-27|Matthew Henry Concise on Ezekiel 21]]
- The diviners of the Ammonites made false prophecies of victory. They would never recover their power, but in time would be wholly forgotten. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ezekiel/chapter-21#Verses 28-32|Matthew Henry Concise on Ezekiel 21]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
