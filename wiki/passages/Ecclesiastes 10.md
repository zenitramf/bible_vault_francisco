# Ecclesiastes 10

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Ecclesiastes 10: To preserve a character for wisdom. (1-3). Respecting subjects and rulers. (4-10). Of foolish talk. (11-15). Duties of rulers and subjects. (16-20).

## Core claims

- Those especially who make a profession of religion, should keep from all appearances of evil. A wise man has great advantage over a fool, who is always at a loss when he has anything to do. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ecclesiastes/chapter-10#Verses 1-3|Matthew Henry Concise on Ecclesiastes 10]]
- There is a practice in the East, of charming serpents by music. The babbler's tongue is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison; and contradiction only makes it the more violent. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ecclesiastes/chapter-10#Verses 11-15|Matthew Henry Concise on Ecclesiastes 10]]
- The happiness of a land depends on the character of its rulers. The people cannot be happy when their princes are childish, and lovers of pleasure. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ecclesiastes/chapter-10#Verses 16-20|Matthew Henry Concise on Ecclesiastes 10]]
- This chapter seems to be like Solomon's proverbs, a collection of wise sayings and observations, rather than a part of his sermon; but the preacher studied to be sententious, and "set in order many proverbs," to be… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-3/ecclesiastes/chapter-10|Matthew Henry Complete on Ecclesiastes 10]]
