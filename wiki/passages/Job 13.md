# Job 13

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Job 13: Job reproves his friends. (1–12). He professes his confidence in God. (13–22). Job entreats to know his sins. (23–28).

## Core claims

- With self-preference, Job declared that he needed not to be taught by them. Those who dispute are tempted to magnify themselves, and lower their brethren, more than is fit. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-13#Verses 1–12|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 13]]
- Job resolved to cleave to the testimony his own conscience gave of his uprightness. He depended upon God for justification and salvation, the two great things we hope for through Christ. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-13#Verses 13–22|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 13]]
- Job begs to have his sins discovered to him. A true penitent is willing to know the worst of himself; and we should all desire to know what our transgressions are, that we may confess them, and guard against them for the future. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-13#Verses 23–28|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 13]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
