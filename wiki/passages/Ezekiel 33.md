# Ezekiel 33

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Ezekiel 33: Ezekiel's duty as a watchman. (1-9). He is to vindicate the Divine government. (10-20). The desolation of Judea. (21-29). Judgments on the mockers of the prophets. (30-33).

## Core claims

- The prophet is a watchman to the house of Israel. His business is to warn sinners of their misery and danger. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ezekiel/chapter-33#Verses 1-9|Matthew Henry Concise on Ezekiel 33]]
- Those are unteachable indeed, who do not learn their dependence upon God, when all creature-comforts fail. Many claim an interest in the peculiar blessings to true believers, while their conduct proves them enemies of God. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ezekiel/chapter-33#Verses 21-29|Matthew Henry Concise on Ezekiel 33]]
- Unworthy and corrupt motives often lead men to the places where the word of God is faithfully preached. Many come to find somewhat to oppose: far more come of curiosity or mere habit. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/ezekiel/chapter-33#Verses 30-33|Matthew Henry Concise on Ezekiel 33]]
- The prophet has now come off his circuit, which he went as judge, in God's name, to try and pass sentence upon the neighbouring nations, and, having finished with them, and read them all their doom, in the eight… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-4/ezekiel/chapter-33|Matthew Henry Complete on Ezekiel 33]]
