# Jeremiah 44

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Jeremiah 44: The Jews in Egypt persist in idolatry. (1-14). They refuse to reform. (15-19). Jeremiah then denounces destruction upon them. (20-30).

## Core claims

- God reminds the Jews of the sins that brought desolations upon Judah. It becomes us to warn men of the danger of sin with all seriousness: Oh, do not do it! [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/jeremiah/chapter-44#Verses 1-14|Matthew Henry Concise on Jeremiah 44]]
- These daring sinners do not attempt excuses, but declare they will do that which is forbidden. Those who disobey God, commonly grow worse and worse, and the heart is more hardened by the deceitfulness of sin. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/jeremiah/chapter-44#Verses 15-19|Matthew Henry Concise on Jeremiah 44]]
- Whatever evil comes upon us, it is because we have sinned against the Lord; we should therefore stand in awe, and sin not. Since they were determined to persist in their idolatry, God would go on to punish them. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/jeremiah/chapter-44#Verses 20-30|Matthew Henry Concise on Jeremiah 44]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
