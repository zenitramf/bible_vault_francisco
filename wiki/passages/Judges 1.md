# Judges 1

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Judges 1: Proceedings of the tribes of Judah and Simeon. (1–8). Hebron and other cities taken. (9–20). The proceedings of other tribes. (21–36).

## Core claims

- The Israelites were convinced that the war against the Canaanites was to be continued; but they were in doubt as to the manner in which it was to be carried on after the death of Joshua. In these respects they inquired of the Lord. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/judges/chapter-1#Verses 1–8|Matthew Henry Concise on Judges 1]]
- The Canaanites had iron chariots; but Israel had God on their side, whose chariots are thousands of angels, Ps 68:17. Yet they suffered their fears to prevail against their faith. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/judges/chapter-1#Verses 9–20|Matthew Henry Concise on Judges 1]]
- The people of Israel were very careless of their duty and interest. Owing to slothfulness and cowardice, they would not be at the pains to complete their conquests. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/judges/chapter-1#Verses 21–36|Matthew Henry Concise on Judges 1]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
