# Habakkuk 3

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Habakkuk 3: The prophet beseeches God for his people. (1, 2). He calls to mind former deliverances. (3–15). His firm trust in the Divine mercy. (16–19).

## Core claims

- The word prayer seems used here for an act of devotion. The Lord would revive his work among the people in the midst of the years of adversity. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/habakkuk/chapter-3#Verses 1, 2|Matthew Henry Concise on Habakkuk 3]]
- God's people, when in distress, and ready to despair, seek help by considering the days of old, and the years of ancient times, and by pleading them with God in prayer. The resemblance between the Babylonish and Egyptian captivities, naturally presents itself to the mind, as well as the possibility of a like… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/habakkuk/chapter-3#Verses 3–15|Matthew Henry Concise on Habakkuk 3]]
- When we see a day of trouble approach, it concerns us to prepare. A good hope through grace is founded in holy fear. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/habakkuk/chapter-3#Verses 16–19|Matthew Henry Concise on Habakkuk 3]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
