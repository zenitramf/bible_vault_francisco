# Leviticus 4

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Leviticus 4: The sin-offering of ignorance for the priest. (1–12). For the whole congregation. (13–21). For a ruler. (22–26). For any of the people. (27–35).

## Core claims

- Burnt-offerings, meat-offerings, and peace-offerings, had been offered before the giving of the law upon mount Sinai; and in these the patriarchs had respect to sin, to make atonement for it. But the Jews were now put into a way of making atonement for sin, more particularly by sacrifice, as a shadow of good things… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/leviticus/chapter-4#Verses 1–12|Matthew Henry Concise on Leviticus 4]]
- Those who have power to call others to account, are themselves accountable to the Ruler of rulers. The sin of the ruler, committed through ignorance, must come to his knowledge, either by the check of his own conscience, or by the reproof of his friends; both which even the best and greatest, not only should submit… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/leviticus/chapter-4#Verses 22–26|Matthew Henry Concise on Leviticus 4]]
- Here is the law of the sin-offering for a common person. To be able to plead, when charged with sin, that we did it ignorantly, and through the surprise of temptation, will not bring us off, if we have no interest in that great plea, Christ hath died. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/leviticus/chapter-4#Verses 27–35|Matthew Henry Concise on Leviticus 4]]
- This chapter is concerning the sin-offering, which was properly intended to make atonement for a sin committed through ignorance, I. By the priest himself, Le 4:1-12. Or, II. By the whole congregation, Le 4:13-21. Or,… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-1/leviticus/chapter-4|Matthew Henry Complete on Leviticus 4]]
