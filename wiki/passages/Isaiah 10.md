# Isaiah 10

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 10: Woes against proud oppressors. (1-4). The Assyrian but an instrument in the hand of God for the punishment of his people. (5-19). The deliverance from him. (20-34).

## Core claims

- These verses are to be joined with the foregoing chapter. Woe to the superior powers that devise and decree unrighteous decrees! [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-10#Verses 1-4|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 10]]
- See what a change sin made. The king of Assyria, in his pride, thought to act by his own will. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-10#Verses 5-19|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 10]]
- By our afflictions we may learn not to make creatures our confidence. Those only can with comfort stay upon God, who return to him in truth, not in pretence and profession only. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/isaiah/chapter-10#Verses 20-34|Matthew Henry Concise on Isaiah 10]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
