# Nehemiah 8

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Nehemiah 8: The reading and expounding the law. (1–8). The people called upon to be joyful. (9–12). The feast of tabernacles, The joy of the people. (13–18).

## Core claims

- Sacrifices were to be offered only at the door of the temple; but praying and preaching were, and are, services of religion, as acceptably performed in one place as in another. Masters of families should bring their families with them to the public worship of God. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/nehemiah/chapter-8#Verses 1–8|Matthew Henry Concise on Nehemiah 8]]
- It was a good sign that their hearts were tender, when they heard the words of the law. The people were to send portions to those for whom nothing was prepared. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/nehemiah/chapter-8#Verses 9–12|Matthew Henry Concise on Nehemiah 8]]
- They found written in the law about the feast of tabernacles. Those who diligently search the Scriptures, find things written there which they have forgotten. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/nehemiah/chapter-8#Verses 13–18|Matthew Henry Concise on Nehemiah 8]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
