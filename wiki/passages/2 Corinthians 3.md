# 2 Corinthians 3

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Corinthians 3: The preference of the gospel to the law given by Moses. (1–11). The preaching of the apostle was suitable to the excellency and evidence of the gospel, through the power of the Holy Ghost. (12–18). McGee stresses converts as living gospel, Spirit-led freedom from Law, and *metamorphosis* character change toward the Transfiguration goal.

## Core claims

- Even the appearance of self-praise and courting human applause, is painful to the humble and spiritual mind. Nothing is more delightful to faithful ministers, or more to their praise, than the success of their ministry, as shown in the spirits and lives of those among whom they labour. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-corinthians/chapter-3#Verses 1–11|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Corinthians 3]]
- It is the duty of the ministers of the gospel to use great plainness, or clearness, of speech. The Old Testament believers had only cloudy and passing glimpses of that glorious Saviour, and unbelievers looked no further than to the outward institution. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/2-corinthians/chapter-3#Verses 12–18|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Corinthians 3]]
- The apostle makes an apology for his seeming to commend himself, and is careful not to assume too much to himself, but to ascribe all praise unto God, 2Co 3:1-5. He then draws a comparison between the Old Testament and… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/2-corinthians/chapter-3|Matthew Henry Complete on 2 Corinthians 3]]
- McGee: those who accept the gospel become the gospel to the unsaved (vv. 1–3). Gospel ministry more glorious than Moses’; veil over hearts that follow the Law; “it” (v. 16) means heart; those led by the Spirit are not under the Law (v. 17). V. 18 great theme: only the Spirit develops Christian character; “changed” is *metamorphosis* (same word as Christ’s transfiguration)—ultimate goal of humanity seen in the Transfiguration. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf|McGee Notes & Outlines on 2 Corinthians]] [[wiki/source-notes/McGee Notes and Outlines on 2 Corinthians|McGee source-note]]

## Agreements and tensions

Henry and McGee agree gospel glory surpasses Moses and the Spirit opens unveiled sight. McGee’s *metamorphosis*/Transfiguration goal and living-gospel poem are distinctive outline emphases.
