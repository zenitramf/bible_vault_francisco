# Mark 13

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Mark 13: The destruction of the temple foretold. (1–4). Christ's prophetic declaration. (5–13). Christ's prophecy. (14–23). His prophetic declarations. (24–27). Watchfulness urged. (28–37).

## Core claims

- See how little Christ values outward pomp, where there is not real purity of heart. He looks with pity upon the ruin of precious souls, and weeps over them, but we do not find him look with pity upon the ruin of a fine house. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/mark/chapter-13#Verses 1–4|Matthew Henry Concise on Mark 13]]
- Our Lord Jesus, in reply to the disciples' question, does not so much satisfy their curiosity as direct their consciences. When many are deceived, we should thereby be awakened to look to ourselves. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/mark/chapter-13#Verses 5–13|Matthew Henry Concise on Mark 13]]
- The Jews in rebelling against the Romans, and in persecuting the Christians, hastened their own ruin apace. Here we have a prediction of that ruin which came upon them within less than forty years after this. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/mark/chapter-13#Verses 14–23|Matthew Henry Concise on Mark 13]]
- The disciples had confounded the destruction of Jerusalem and the end of the world. This mistake Christ set right, and showed that the day of Christ's coming, and the day of judgment, shall be after that tribulation. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/mark/chapter-13#Verses 24–27|Matthew Henry Concise on Mark 13]]
- We have the application of this prophetic sermon. As to the destruction of Jerusalem, expect it to come very shortly. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/mark/chapter-13#Verses 28–37|Matthew Henry Concise on Mark 13]]
