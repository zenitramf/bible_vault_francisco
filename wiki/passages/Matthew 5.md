# Matthew 5

## Summary

The Sermon on the Mount begins with Beatitudes, salt and light, and a deeper righteousness that fulfills the law from the heart—anger, lust, truth, non-retaliation, and enemy-love.

## Core claims

- True happiness is sought from Christ by the rule of his word; the Beatitudes describe Christian graces. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/matthew/chapter-5#Verses 3–12|Matthew Henry Concise on Matthew 5]]
- Disciples are salt and light for a corrupt world; good works are to glorify the Father. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/matthew/chapter-5#Verses 13–16|Matthew Henry Concise on Matthew 5]]
- Christ came not to destroy but to fulfill the law; none share his justifying righteousness without repenting of evil deeds. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/matthew/chapter-5#Verses 17–20|Matthew Henry Concise on Matthew 5]]
- The sixth commandment reaches the heart’s anger; the seventh requires victory over lustful desire; speech must be true. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/matthew/chapter-5#Verses 21–26|Matthew Henry Concise on Matthew 5]]
- Christians suffer injury for peace’s sake and love even enemies; the Father’s perfection is the pattern. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/matthew/chapter-5#Verses 38–42|Matthew Henry Concise on Matthew 5]]
- This chapter, and the two that follow it, are a sermon; a famous sermon; the sermon upon the mount. It is the longest and fullest continued discourse of our Saviour that we have upon record in all the gospels.
