# Genesis 47

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 47: Joseph presents his brethren to Pharaoh. (1–6). Jacob blesses Pharaoh. (7–12). Joseph's dealings with the Egyptians during the famine. (13–26). Jacob's age. His desire to be buried in Canaan. (27–31).

## Core claims

- Though Joseph was a great man, especially in Egypt, yet he owned his brethren. Let the rich and great in the world not overlook or despise poor relations. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/genesis/chapter-47#Verses 1–6|Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 47]]
- Care being taken of Jacob and his family, which mercy was especially designed by Providence in Joseph's advancement, an account is given of the saving the kingdom of Egypt from ruin. There was no bread, and the people were ready to die. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/genesis/chapter-47#Verses 13–26|Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 47]]
- At last the time drew nigh that Israel must die. Israel, a prince with God, had power over the Angel, and prevailed, yet must die. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/genesis/chapter-47#Verses 27–31|Matthew Henry Concise on Genesis 47]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
