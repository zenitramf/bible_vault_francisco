# Mark 11

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Mark 11: Christ's triumphant entry into Jerusalem. (1–11). The barren fig-tree cursed, The temple cleansed. (12–18). Prayer in faith. (19–26). The priests and elders questioned concerning John the Baptist. (27–33).

## Core claims

- Christ's coming into Jerusalem thus remarkably, shows that he was not afraid of the power and malice of his enemies. This would encourage his disciples who were full of fear. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/mark/chapter-11#Verses 1–11|Matthew Henry Concise on Mark 11]]
- Christ looked to find some fruit, for the time of gathering figs, though it was near, was not yet come; but he found none. He made this fig-tree an example, not to the trees, but to the men of that generation. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/mark/chapter-11#Verses 12–18|Matthew Henry Concise on Mark 11]]
- The disciples could not think why that fig-tree should so soon wither away; but all wither who reject Christ; it represented the state of the Jewish church. We should rest in no religion that does not make us fruitful in good works. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/mark/chapter-11#Verses 19–26|Matthew Henry Concise on Mark 11]]
- Our Saviour shows how near akin his doctrine and baptism were to those of John; they had the same design and tendency, to bring in the gospel kingdom.
