# 1 John 3

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on 1 John 3: The apostle admires the love of God in making believers his children. (1, 2). The purifying influence of the hope of seeing Christ, and the danger of pretending to this, and living in sin. (3–10). Love to the brethren….

## Core claims

- Little does the world know of the happiness of the real followers of Christ. Little does the world think that these poor, humble, despised ones, are favourites of God, and will dwell in heaven. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-john/chapter-3#Verses 1, 2|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 John 3]]
- The sons of God know that their Lord is of purer eyes than to allow any thing unholy and impure to dwell with him. It is the hope of hypocrites, not of the sons of God, that makes allowance for gratifying impure desires and lusts. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-john/chapter-3#Verses 3–10|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 John 3]]
- We should love the Lord Jesus, value his love, and therefore love all our brethren in Christ. This love is the special fruit of our faith, and a certain sign of our being born again. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-john/chapter-3#Verses 11–15|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 John 3]]
- Here is the condescension, the miracle, the mystery of Divine love, that God would redeem the church with his own blood. Surely we should love those whom God has loved, and so loved. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-john/chapter-3#Verses 16–21|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 John 3]]
- When believers had confidence towards God, through the Spirit of adoption, and by faith in the great High Priest, they might ask what they would of their reconciled Father. They would receive it, if good for them. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/1-john/chapter-3#Verses 22–24|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 John 3]]
