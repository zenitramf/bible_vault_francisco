# John 17

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on John 17: Christ's prayer for himself. (1–5). His prayer for his disciples. (6–10). His prayer. (11–26).

## Core claims

- Our Lord prayed as a man, and as the Mediator of his people; yet he spoke with majesty and authority, as one with and equal to the Father. Eternal life could not be given to believers, unless Christ, their Surety, both glorified the Father, and was glorified of him. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/john/chapter-17#Verses 1–5|Matthew Henry Concise on John 17]]
- Christ prays for those that are his. Thou gavest them me, as sheep to the shepherd, to be kept; as a patient to the physician, to be cured; as children to a tutor, to be taught: thus he will deliver up his charge. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/john/chapter-17#Verses 6–10|Matthew Henry Concise on John 17]]
- Christ does not pray that they might be rich and great in the world, but that they might be kept from sin, strengthened for their duty, and brought safe to heaven. The prosperity of the soul is the best prosperity. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/john/chapter-17#Verses 11–16|Matthew Henry Concise on John 17]]
- Christ next prayed for the disciples, that they might not only be kept from evil, but made good. It is the prayer of Jesus for all that are his, that they may be made holy. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/john/chapter-17#Verses 17–19|Matthew Henry Concise on John 17]]
- Christ, as one with the Father, claimed on behalf of all that had been given to him, and should in due time believe on him, that they should be brought to heaven; and that there the whole company of the redeemed might behold his glory as their beloved Friend and Brother, and therein find happiness.
