## Core claims

- See here the character of an honest man. He sets God before him, and lives as in his sight. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/acts/chapter-23#Verses 1–5|Matthew Henry Concise on Acts 23]]
- The Pharisees were correct in the faith of the Jewish church. The Sadducees were no friends to the Scripture or Divine revelation; they denied a future state; they had neither hope of eternal happiness, nor dread of eternal misery. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/acts/chapter-23#Verses 6–11|Matthew Henry Concise on Acts 23]]
- False religious principles, adopted by carnal men, urge on to such wickedness, as human nature would hardly be supposed capable of. Yet the Lord readily disappoints the best concerted schemes of iniquity. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/acts/chapter-23#Verses 12–24|Matthew Henry Concise on Acts 23]]
- God has instruments for every work. The natural abilities and moral virtues of the heathens often have been employed to protect his persecuted servants. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/acts/chapter-23#Verses 25–35|Matthew Henry Concise on Acts 23]]
