# Matthew 6:5–15

## Summary

Jesus warns against prayer performed for public reputation and teaches a pattern of address to the Father that joins petition, daily dependence, and mutual forgiveness. The passage grounds ordinary discipleship prayer rather than mediatorial intercession unique to Christ.

## Core claims

- Prayer must not be practiced to be seen by people, but offered to the Father who sees in secret. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/matthew/chapter-6#Verses 5–8|Matthew Henry Concise on Matthew 6]]
- Christ teaches how to pray, centering address on the Father and joining petition with forgiveness of others. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/matthew/chapter-6#Verses 9–15|Matthew Henry Concise on Matthew 6]]
- The Lord's Prayer pattern belongs to disciples; it is related to but not identical with Christ's unique mediatorial prayer. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-5/matthew/chapter-6|Matthew Henry Complete on Matthew 6]]

## Agreements and tensions

Henry's concise and complete treatments agree on sincerity and the Father-centered pattern. Distinctions between this taught form and John 17 are developed more fully on the Prayer concept page.
