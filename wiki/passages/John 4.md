# John 4

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on John 4: Christ's departure into Galilee. (1–3). His discourse with the Samaritan woman. (4–26). The effects of Christ's conversation with the woman of Samaria. (27–42). Christ heals the nobleman's son. (43–54).

## Core claims

- Jesus applied himself more to preaching, which was the more excellent, 1Co 1:17, than to baptism. He would put honour upon his disciples, by employing them to baptize. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/john/chapter-4#Verses 1–3|Matthew Henry Concise on John 4]]
- There was great hatred between the Samaritans and the Jews. Christ's road from Judea to Galilee lay through Samaria. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/john/chapter-4#Verses 4–26|Matthew Henry Concise on John 4]]
- The disciples wondered that Christ talked thus with a Samaritan. Yet they knew it was for some good reason, and for some good end. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/john/chapter-4#Verses 27–42|Matthew Henry Concise on John 4]]
- The father was a nobleman, yet the son was sick. Honours and titles are no security from sickness and death. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/john/chapter-4#Verses 43–54|Matthew Henry Concise on John 4]]

## Agreements and tensions

This chapter synthesis is drawn from Matthew Henry Concise only. Later work may add Matthew Henry Complete and Spurgeon on the same pericopes. Concise notes are pastoral summaries, not full critical commentary.
