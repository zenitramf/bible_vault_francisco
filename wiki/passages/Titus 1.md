# Titus 1

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Titus 1: The apostle salutes Titus. (1–4). The qualifications of a faithful pastor. (5–9). The evil temper and practices of false teachers. (10–16). McGee measures the ideal NT church as an orderly organization with ordained elders who meet prescribed requirements; the elder is God’s steward, not self-willed, holding the trustworthy word to exhort and convict heretics amid Cretan reputations for lying.

## Core claims

- All are the servants of God who are not slaves of sin and Satan. All gospel truth is according to godliness, teaching the fear of God. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/titus/chapter-1#Verses 1–4|Matthew Henry Concise on Titus 1]]
- The character and qualification of pastors, here called elders and bishops, agree with what the apostle wrote to Timothy. Being such bishops and overseers of the flock, to be examples to them, and God's stewards to take care of the affairs of his household, there is great reason that they should be blameless. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/titus/chapter-1#Verses 5–9|Matthew Henry Concise on Titus 1]]
- False teachers are described. Faithful ministers must oppose such in good time, that their folly being made manifest, they may go no further They had a base end in what they did; serving a worldly interest under pretence of religion: for the love of money is the root of all evil. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/titus/chapter-1#Verses 10–16|Matthew Henry Concise on Titus 1]]
- In this chapter we have, I. The preface or introduction to the epistle, showing from and to whom it was written, with the apostle's salutation and prayer for Titus, wishing all blessings to him, Tit 1:1-4. II. Entrance… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-6/titus/chapter-1|Matthew Henry Complete on Titus 1]]

- Titus was left in Crete to organize local churches with elders as spiritual leaders (more than one), with oversight and teaching responsibility. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Timothy-Titus-Philemon.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on Pastoral Epistles]]
- Elder and bishop are synonymous; the elder must not be self-willed, for he is a steward of God as well as a representative of the people—holding fast the trustworthy word to exhort in sound teaching and convict gainsayers. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Timothy-Titus-Philemon.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on Pastoral Epistles]]
- Cretan reputation (Epimenides: liars, evil beasts) requires severe dealing so they will not turn from truth to fables; ceremonies cannot change the heart—only the Word; faith without works is dead. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Timothy-Titus-Philemon.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on Pastoral Epistles]]

## Agreements and tensions

- McGee agrees with Henry on love, order, and Scripture’s authority; he stresses local-vs-invisible church contrast, elder/bishop identity, and last-days apostasy more programmatically.
Complete commentary on this chapter deepens the Concise atlas with fuller pastoral and doctrinal exposition (Phase 4.1). McGee and Henry agree that elder/bishop qualifications match 1 Timothy 3 and that false teachers must be opposed; McGee stresses plural eldership and Crete’s cultural severity. Henry Concise, Henry Complete, and McGee Notes & Outlines now share this hub.
