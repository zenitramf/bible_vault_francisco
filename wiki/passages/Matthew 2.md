# Matthew 2

## Summary

Matthew 2 traces Gentile seekers who worship the Child, providential flight to Egypt, Herod’s cruelty, and return to Nazareth under divine guidance.

## Core claims

- Those farthest from ordinary means of grace may seek Christ most diligently; mere human learning cannot of itself direct men to him. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/matthew/chapter-2#Verses 1–8|Matthew Henry Concise on Matthew 2]]
- The wise men worship the child Jesus; joy in finding him is like light after a long night of bondage. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/matthew/chapter-2#Verses 9–12|Matthew Henry Concise on Matthew 2]]
- God can make even Egypt a refuge for the holy Child; providence overrules tyrants. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/matthew/chapter-2#Verses 13–15|Matthew Henry Concise on Matthew 2]]
- Unbridled wrath with unlawful power produces cruel absurdities, as in Herod’s massacre. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/matthew/chapter-2#Verses 16–18|Matthew Henry Concise on Matthew 2]]
- Christ must return to the lost sheep of Israel; temporary shelter is not permanent abode. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/matthew/chapter-2#Verses 19–23|Matthew Henry Concise on Matthew 2]]
- In this chapter, we have the history of our Saviour's infancy, where we find how early he began to suffer, and that in him the word of righteousness was fulfilled, before he himself began to fulfil all righteousness.… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-5/matthew/chapter-2|Matthew Henry Complete on Matthew 2]]
