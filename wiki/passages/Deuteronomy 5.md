# Deuteronomy 5

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Deuteronomy 5: The covenant in Horeb. (1–5). The ten commandments repeated. (6–22). The request of the people that the law might be delivered through Moses. (23–33).

## Core claims

- Moses demands attention. When we hear the word of God we must learn it; and what we have learned we must put in practice, for that is the end of hearing and learning; not to fill our heads with notions, or our mouths with talk, but to direct our affections and conduct. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/deuteronomy/chapter-5#Verses 1–5|Matthew Henry Concise on Deuteronomy 5]]
- There is some variation here from Ex 20 as between the Lord's prayer in Mt 6 and Lu 11. It is more necessary that we tie ourselves to the things, than to the words unalterably. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/deuteronomy/chapter-5#Verses 6–22|Matthew Henry Concise on Deuteronomy 5]]
- Moses refers to the consternation caused by the terror with which the law was given. God's appearances have always been terrible to man, ever since the fall; but Christ, having taken away sin, invites us to come boldly to the throne of grace. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/deuteronomy/chapter-5#Verses 23–33|Matthew Henry Concise on Deuteronomy 5]]
