# Job 31

## Summary

Matthew Henry Concise on Job 31: Job declares his uprightness. (1–8). His integrity. (9–15). Job merciful. (16–23). Job not guilty of covetousness or idolatry. (24–32). Job not guilty of hypocrisy and violence. (33–40).

## Core claims

- Job did not speak the things here recorded by way of boasting, but in answer to the charge of hypocrisy. He understood the spiritual nature of God's commandments, as reaching to the thoughts and intents of the heart. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-31#Verses 1–8|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 31]]
- Job's conscience gave testimony concerning his just and charitable behaviour toward the poor. He is most large upon this head, because in this matter he was particularly accused. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-31#Verses 16–23|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 31]]
- Job clears himself from the charge of hypocrisy. We are loth to confess our faults, willing to excuse them, and to lay the blame upon others. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-31#Verses 33–40|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 31]]
- Job had often protested his integrity in general; here he does it in particular instances, not in a way of commendation (for he does not here proclaim his good deeds), but in his own just and necessary vindication, to… [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-3/job/chapter-31|Matthew Henry Complete on Job 31]]
