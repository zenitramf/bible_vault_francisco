# Wisdom

## Summary

In the retrieved sources, wisdom is not a mere cleverness of mind but a God-given skill for living that orients the whole person to his Creator. Solomon places it above every earthly possession — *better than gold, better than silver, better than rubies* (Pr 16:16; Pr 3:14; Pr 8:10–11, 19) — and Job grounds the comparison in the fact that no metal the mines yield can purchase the wisdom that comes from the fear of the Lord (Jb 28). The same commentaries read "wisdom" in Proverbs 8 as ultimately Christward: Christ is "in whom are all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (Col 2:3), and the wisdom the gospel imparts is at once knowledge, righteousness, sanctification, and redemption (1 Cor 1:30). Spurgeon gathers this into a single pastoral line: the only gold that truly satisfies is "gold tried in the fire" — the grace of God in Christ, received "without money and without price" (Re 3:18).

## Core claims

- "How much better is it to get wisdom than gold! and to get understanding rather to be chosen than silver!" Solomon speaks with assurance and admiration of a disproportion that conscience itself is to judge. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-3/proverbs/chapter-16#Pride and Humility|Matthew Henry Complete on Proverbs 16]]
- Heavenly wisdom outranks worldly wealth because grace is the gift of God's peculiar favour while gold is only of common providence; grace is for ourselves, gold for others; grace is for the soul and eternity, gold only for the body and time; grace will stand us in stead in a dying hour, when gold will do us no good. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-3/proverbs/chapter-16#Pride and Humility|Matthew Henry Complete on Proverbs 16]]
- The *getting* of wisdom surpasses the getting of wealth: many take care and pains to get riches and come short, but grace has never been denied to any that sincerely sought it; there is vanity and vexation of spirit in getting wealth, but joy and satisfaction of spirit in getting wisdom. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-3/proverbs/chapter-16#Pride and Humility|Matthew Henry Complete on Proverbs 16]]
- Matthew Henry's Concise compression: "There is joy and satisfaction of spirit, only in getting wisdom." [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/proverbs/chapter-16#Verse 16|Matthew Henry Concise on Proverbs 16]]
- Wisdom is "a transcendent happiness, more than can be found in the wealth of this world, if we had ever so much of it"; suppose a man to have all the world's goods, yet all this would not purchase heavenly wisdom, would not countervail its want, would not make a man half so happy, and wisdom will procure and secure what silver, gold, and rubies cannot buy. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-3/proverbs/chapter-3#The Excellency of Wisdom; Happiness of Those Who Find Wisdom|Matthew Henry Complete on Proverbs 3]]
- "Receive my instruction, and not silver… wisdom is in itself, and therefore must be in our account, better than rubies"; "My fruit is better than gold, yea, than fine gold; and my revenue than choice silver." [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-complete/volume-3/proverbs/chapter-8#The Advantages of Wisdom|Matthew Henry Complete on Proverbs 8]]
- Job's chapter on mining is read by Henry as the Old Testament's clearest statement that wisdom "cannot be gotten for gold": the mines yield iron, brass, silver, and even refined gold, but the fear of the Lord and departing from evil — wisdom itself — is not found in any earthly treasure. [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/job/chapter-28#Verses 12–19|Matthew Henry Concise on Job 28]]
- "Wisdom, here is Christ, in whom are all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge; it is Christ in the word, and Christ in the heart; not only Christ revealed to us, but Christ revealed in us. All prudence and skill are from the Lord." [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/proverbs/chapter-8|Matthew Henry Concise on Proverbs 8]]
- "Through the redemption of Christ's precious blood, the riches of [wisdom] have been purchased and communicated to perishing sinners." [[sources/commentaries_english/mhenry-concise/proverbs/chapter-8|Matthew Henry Concise on Proverbs 8]]
- Spurgeon: the commands of God's word are the book, but God is the Teacher; "the letter can make us knowing, but only the divine Spirit can make us wise. Wisdom is knowledge put to practical use. Wisdom comes to us through obedience." [[sources/commentaries_english/chspurgeon-tod/volume-6/psalm-119/verses-97-104#Exposition Verse 98|Spurgeon, Treasury of David on Psalm 119:98]]
- "He who is taught of God has a practical wisdom such as malice cannot supply to the crafty; while harmless as a dove he also exhibits more than a serpent's wisdom." [[sources/commentaries_english/chspurgeon-tod/volume-6/psalm-119/verses-97-104#Exposition Verse 98|Spurgeon, Treasury of David on Psalm 119:98]]
- "Understanding gotten by the precepts of the word is better than understanding gotten by long experience. It is more exact, more sure, safer and cheaper, shorter." (Condensed from Thomas Manton in the same Treasury entry.) [[sources/commentaries_english/chspurgeon-tod/volume-6/psalm-119/verses-97-104#Exposition Verse 100|Spurgeon, Treasury of David on Psalm 119:100]]
- The only gold that truly satisfies is the gold of free grace, which Christ counsels Laodicea to "buy of me gold tried in the fire"; gold is the most precious metal, and the Saviour counsels us to buy the best of everything of him, "gold that has just passed through the assay and has the mint mark upon it… gold that will endure all further tests, having survived that of fire." [[sources/commentaries_english/chspurgeon-sermons/volume-28/sermon_1677|Spurgeon, Sermon 1677: A Great Mistake and the Way to Rectify It]]
- Spurgeon: "It is always wise to get gold when you can buy it for nothing!… There is a ready market for pure gold at this extraordinary minimum! Come, then, and accept the gold of free Grace!" The free-grace terms are, with Christ, "without money and without price." [[sources/commentaries_english/chspurgeon-sermons/volume-28/sermon_1677|Spurgeon, Sermon 1677: A Great Mistake and the Way to Rectify It]]

## Agreements and tensions

Phase 4 full-corpus volume/month source-notes are linked under Related pages for inventory coverage; doctrinal claims remain grounded in prior multi-source synthesis (no false consensus).

- Matthew Henry (Complete and Concise) and Spurgeon (Treasury of David and sermons) agree that the wisdom of Proverbs 16:16 is not a commodity but a moral and spiritual condition rooted in the fear of the Lord, and that this wisdom outranks every earthly possession by a disproportion that conscience is to judge.
- Both readers treat the comparison as not a *condemnation* of gold and silver but an *outranking* of them. The preachers do not call the rich wicked for being rich; they call the wise incomparably richer, and they call the purchase of wisdom without money the only true bargain.
- The most theologically loaded convergence is the reading of "wisdom" in Proverbs 8 as Christ himself. Henry's Concise is explicit ("Wisdom, here is Christ"); the Complete commentary builds the same identification through the picture of Wisdom as the covenant partner present with the Father before the world was. Spurgeon makes the parallel move from the other side: the only "gold" that satisfies is the grace of Christ. The seed does not yet develop the Wisdom–Logos–Christ identification in detail; that thickening is an open question below.
- Henry repeatedly spiritualises the comparison ("grace is for the soul and eternity; gold only for the body and time") in a way the historical-grammatical reader could press: he is making a pastoral application, not a metaphysical proof. This seed records that move as the sources actually make it.
- The retrieved sources do not enter the well-known tension between Proverbs as a general wisdom-for-living and the Wisdom of Proverbs 8 as a divine hypostasis. That tension is real in the canon (cf. Pr 8:22–31 alongside Pr 1:7, 9:10) and is filed below as an open question.

## Biblical passages

Key loci in the present evidence:

- **Proverbs 16:16** — *How much better is it to get wisdom than gold! and to get understanding rather to be chosen than silver!* (the seed passage)
- **Proverbs 3:14–15** — wisdom more profitable than silver, her fruit better than gold
- **Proverbs 8:10–11, 19** — wisdom better than rubies; "My fruit is better than gold, yea, than fine gold; and my revenue than choice silver"
- **Proverbs 8:22–31** — Wisdom present with God before the world was (Wisdom as Person)
- **Proverbs 9:10** — "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom"
- **Job 28:12–19, 28** — wisdom cannot be gotten for gold; the fear of the Lord is wisdom
- **Psalm 19:7** — "The testimony of the LORD is sure, making wise the simple"
- **Psalm 119:98–100** — God's word makes wiser than enemies, teachers, and ancients
- **Psalm 119:127** — God's commandments loved "above gold, yea, above fine gold"
- **1 Corinthians 1:30** — Christ Jesus is made unto us wisdom
- **Colossians 2:3** — in Christ "are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge"
- **James 1:5** — "If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God"
- **James 3:17** — the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable

## Open questions

- How should the Wisdom–Logos–Christ identification (Proverbs 8 → John 1 → Colossians 2:3) be developed with explicit Christological precision, drawing on the New Testament evidence currently only gestured at?
- Which Spurgeon sermons on Proverbs 8, on "Buy of me gold," or on "the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom" should thicken the Spurgeon side of the page beyond the Treasury entry and sermon 1677?
- How should the tension between wisdom as a moral-skill attainable by the hearer (Pr 1:5; 9:9) and wisdom as a divine person be held together without flattening either pole?
- What is the relationship between wisdom in Proverbs, wisdom in Ecclesiastes ("vanity"), and the wisdom Paul preaches in 1 Corinthians 1–2? The seed has only the Proverbs side and the NT christological side; Ecclesiastes and 1 Corinthians 1–2 remain gaps.
- Where should the related sub-concepts (prudence, understanding, instruction, reproof, the fear of the Lord) live — as separate concept pages, as children of this one, or only as cross-references?
- What Spanish-language pastoral notes (if any are later ingested) would extend the page beyond the English commentary corpus?
