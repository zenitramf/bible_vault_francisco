# McGee Notes and Outlines on 1 and 2 Samuel

## Summary

Intentional ingest of **J. Vernon McGee, Thru the Bible Notes & Outlines** on 1 & 2 Samuel (immutable PDF under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/`; original raw path `raw/1-2_Samuel.pdf`). McGee treats the two books as originally one in the Jewish canon (first two of four Books of Kings in the Latin Vulgate) and as the origin of the kingdom. Motto verse: Hosea 13:11 — “I gave thee a king in mine anger, and took him away in my wrath.” Theme triad: **prayer** (1 Samuel opens with prayer; 2 Samuel closes with prayer), **kingdom** (theocracy to monarchy; God’s covenant with David), and **prophet** (rise of the prophetic office as God’s messenger in place of the priest). Key verse: 1 Samuel 10:25. First Samuel is transitional from judges to kingdom and foreshadows, in some respects, the coming millennial kingdom; the world needs a king with righteous power, full dependence on God, and full obedience. Second Samuel is given entirely to David’s reign and shows that government of this world in the hands of man is a failure. High-quality firecrawl-parse extract (19 pages) supports book-level synthesis; chapter files under a McGee commentary tree are not yet promoted.

## Core claims

- Writer: Samuel’s name heads the books because his story is first and prominent and he anointed both Saul and David; he is considered author through 1 Samuel 25 (his death); Nathan and Gad completed the writing (1 Chronicles 29:29 ASV). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Samuel.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on 1–2 Samuel]]
- Features include rise of the kingdom; Hannah; little Samuel; David and Goliath; David–Jonathan friendship; Saul at Endor; God’s covenant with David (2 Samuel 7); David’s great sin with Bathsheba; Absalom’s rebellion. Characters in Samuel stand out above the average of Judges—six chief figures in 1 Samuel: Hannah, Eli, Samuel, Saul, Jonathan, David. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Samuel.pdf.extract.md|McGee on Samuel features]]
- Verses worth remembering: 1 Samuel 15:22 (obedience); 1 Samuel 25:29 (Abigail on David’s relationship to God); 2 Samuel 3:33 (Abner’s epitaph); 2 Samuel 7 (covenant; seven “I wills”); 2 Samuel 18:33 (grief over Absalom); 2 Samuel 22 (song = Psalm 18); 2 Samuel 24:24 (rebuke of cheap religion). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Samuel.pdf.extract.md|McGee memorable verses]]
- 1 Samuel outline: I. Samuel — God’s prophet, priest, and judge (chs. 1–8); II. Saul — Satan’s man (chs. 9–15); III. David — God’s man, and Saul — Satan’s man (chs. 16–31). 2 Samuel outline: I. Triumphs of David (chs. 1–10); II. Troubles of David (chs. 11–24). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Samuel.pdf.extract.md|McGee Samuel outline]]
- Hannah’s cry for a child opens the book while the people cry for a king; “God builds the throne on a woman’s cry.” Eli mistakes her prayer for drunkenness; Samuel is vowed and given to the Lord. Hannah’s thanksgiving prayer is prophetic and first mentions the Messiah (2:10). Eli’s evil sons unfit for priesthood; an unnamed prophet announces a faithful priest (2:35); 2:26 spoken only of Samuel and Jesus. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Samuel.pdf.extract.md|McGee on Hannah and Eli]]
- Samuel’s call (ch. 3) is not nursery-only: it marks transition from theocracy to monarchy, priest to king; God will speak to the king through prophets. Josephus places Samuel at about twelve; “child” language parallels Solomon and Jeremiah. Four calls: first two to salvation (v. 7), last two to service (v. 10). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Samuel.pdf.extract.md|McGee on Samuel’s call]]
- Israel takes the ark into battle as a lucky object (superstitious paganism); merit is in God’s presence, not the object. Ark captured; Eli dies. Philistines’ Dagon falls; ark returned via Bethshemesh to Kirjath-jearim. After twenty years Israel puts away Baalim and Ashtaroth; Ebenezer stone — “Hitherto hath the Lord helped us.” Samuel’s extensive ministry as prophet, priest, and judge (7:15–17). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Samuel.pdf.extract.md|McGee on ark and Ebenezer]]
- Samuel fails as father by making unworthy sons judges; Israel demands a king, rejecting God and Samuel under surrounding-nation influence. Hosea 13:11 written over the remainder of 1 Samuel. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Samuel.pdf.extract.md|McGee on demand for a king]]
- Saul looks like a king; people chose him; God granted request but sent leanness. Anointing and Spirit-coming do not prove conversion (Balaam parallel); “another heart” (10:9) is equipment for office, not a new heart. Reception of Saul is rejection of God (10:19); false modesty at hiding among stuff (10:22). Key message verse 10:25. Begins well at Jabesh-gilead. Samuel’s integrity autobiography (12:3); grace still available if people obey (12:14, 22). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Samuel.pdf.extract.md|McGee on Saul received]]
- Saul’s real nature: takes credit for Jonathan’s victories; intrudes into priest’s office and is rebuked/rejected (ch. 13); jealousy would destroy Jonathan (ch. 14); glaring rebellion regarding Agag, covers sin before people (15:30); final rejection with no hope of recovery (15:35). Spiritual principle: to obey is better than sacrifice (15:22–23). God often gives a second opportunity (Jacob, Jonah, Peter, Mark); the individual and we need to know the result (Psalm 51:4; Romans 3:19). Extreme surgery on Amalek anticipates Haman the Agagite (Esther 3:1)—protecting future multitudes. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Samuel.pdf.extract.md|McGee on Saul rejected]]
- God chooses David by heart, not appearance (16:7). Five smooth stones because Goliath had four sons (2 Samuel 21:22), not fear of missing. Jonathan’s covenant love reminds of John the Baptist’s attitude. David spares Saul twice, honoring the office not the man. Abigail prevents rash murder and becomes a good influence (25:29, 32–34). Samuel dies in retirement (ch. 25). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Samuel.pdf.extract.md|McGee on David’s rise]]
- Witch of Endor (ch. 28): Scripture condemns necromancy; McGee maintains Samuel did not appear—either fraud/ventriloquism (G. Campbell Morgan) or a false spirit; even the witch was frightened; false spirit said nothing not previously revealed. Heaven silent, Saul turned to hell. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Samuel.pdf.extract.md|McGee on Endor]]
- Saul’s end: self-destruction; failed in ruling God’s property; spared Amalekites and was killed in connection with an Amalekite (2 Samuel 1). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Samuel.pdf.extract.md|McGee on Saul’s death]]
- 2 Samuel: David’s life important as ancestor of Jesus (Matthew 1:1). Amalekite claims credit for killing Saul and is executed. Civil war with Ish-bosheth; Abner murdered by Joab after leaving city of refuge; David king over all Israel, capital Jerusalem. Ark brought wrong way (cart) then right way (shoulders of Kohathites); Uzzah dies for touching; Michal rebukes David’s devotion. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Samuel.pdf.extract.md|McGee on David’s triumphs]]
- 2 Samuel 7 is one of the great chapters: God will not let bloody David build the temple but builds David a house; promises king and kingdom in David’s line (vv. 12, 13, 16); v. 14 reaches to Christ (“Christ was made sin for us”; Bishop Horsley). Confirmed by oath (Psalm 89:34–37). David understands a coming king more than a man. OT prophets, Matthew 1:1, Gabriel to Mary (Luke 1:32–33), Pentecost (Acts 2), Romans 1:3, and Revelation 22:16 all rest here; about 59 NT references to David. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Samuel.pdf.extract.md|McGee on Davidic covenant]]
- Kindness to Mephibosheth (ch. 9). Two great sins (ch. 11) when David stayed home from war—adultery then murder of Uriah; thought he got by but had not (v. 27). Nathan’s parable; judgment pronounced; David acknowledges sin (12:13); reaps what he sows; Solomon second son of Bathsheba. Family chaos: Amnon rapes Tamar; Absalom kills Amnon; rebellion; David flees without using ark as charm; Absalom slain; deep grief (18:33); David great king but poor father. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Samuel.pdf.extract.md|McGee on David’s troubles]]
- Song of deliverance (ch. 22 = Psalm 18); possible Psalm 23 timing. Last words and hope in everlasting covenant (23:5); mighty men from days of rejection; Uriah among them—one blot. Census sin (ch. 24); chooses to fall into God’s hand; buys Araunah’s threshing floor rather than offer that which costs nothing (v. 24)—site of Solomon’s temple. Closing frame: government in man’s hands fails until he comes whose right it is (Ezekiel 21:27; Isaiah 32:1). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Samuel.pdf.extract.md|McGee on David’s end]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Agrees with Henry Concise/Complete Samuel atlas that Hannah’s prayer, Saul’s rejection, David–Goliath, Davidic covenant, Bathsheba/Nathan, Absalom, and the threshing-floor purchase are central pastoral hubs.
- Pairs with [[wiki/source-notes/McGee Notes and Outlines on 1 and 2 Kings|McGee on 1–2 Kings]] and [[wiki/source-notes/McGee Notes and Outlines on 1 and 2 Chronicles|McGee on 1–2 Chronicles]]: Samuel supplies kingdom origin and Davidic covenant foundation; Kings applies the “as David his father” standard through division and captivity; Chronicles rereads David’s house and temple desire from the altar viewpoint.
- Adds sharp moral dualism (Saul as “Satan’s man,” David as “God’s man”), prayer bookends, prophet-over-priest rise, Endor false-spirit reading, five-stones/four-sons detail, and full NT trajectory of 2 Samuel 7.
- Dispensational and typology notes (kingdom foreshadowing millennium; Horsley Christological renderings of 7:14, 19; ark-as-charm superstition; cheap-religion rebuke at Araunah) are McGee radio-outline readings—hold beside Henry and other sources, not as the vault’s sole word.
- Recommended-books list and chapter-summary worksheet are Thru the Bible study aids, not independent critical apparatus.
- Corpus is a book-level PDF under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/`; optional chapter split remains open.
