# McGee Notes and Outlines on 2 and 3 John

## Summary

Intentional ingest of **J. Vernon McGee, Thru the Bible Notes & Outlines** on 2 John and 3 John (immutable PDF under `sources/personal-notes/ingest/`; original raw path `raw/2-3_John.pdf`). McGee treats both as personal letters by John the Apostle (A.D. 90–100) that put 1 John’s teaching into action around the theme of **truth**: 2 John that truth is worth standing for (do not receive false teachers); 3 John that truth is worth working for (receive and support true teachers). 2 John balances love within the boundary of truth against Gnostic denials of Christ’s flesh; 3 John contrasts Gaius and Demetrius with Diotrephes the preeminence-seeker. High-quality firecrawl-parse extract (8 pages) supports book-level synthesis.

## Core claims

- Writer John the Apostle; date A.D. 90–100 for both epistles. [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee Notes & Outlines on 2–3 John]]
- 2 John is a personal letter like Philemon, addressed to “the elect lady” (*electa kyria*)—possibly a prominent Christian lady, or (McGee’s preferred lean) a local church extending hospitality too freely to claimed Christians including heretics. [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee on 2 John addressee]]
- Theme of 2 John: **for truth’s sake**—truth is worth contending for; it is wrong to receive false teachers. Outline: love expressed in the boundary of truth (vv. 1–6); life as expression of the doctrine of Christ (vv. 7–11); personal greeting (vv. 12–13). [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee on 2 John theme and outline]]
- “The elder” (*presbuteros*) means seniority and/or office; obvious reference to John the apostle, perhaps withholding his name under rising persecution; John in his 90s. [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee on 2 John 1]]
- “Truth” is the epistle’s theme (omit “the” in places): sincerely/truly; “the truth” is the body of apostolic doctrine. In v. 7 the Truth is Jesus Christ; in v. 9 the truth is doctrine. Abiding in the truth means knowledge, fellowship, and constancy (Bengel). [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee on truth in 2 John]]
- Grace, mercy, and peace “in truth and love” name the polarity of Christian life—hard to keep balance between fighting fundamentalists and slobbering liberals (Eph 4:15). Walking in truth is obedience to the Father’s commandments; love and commandment unite (vv. 3–6). [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee on 2 John 3–6]]
- Gnosticism denied Jesus Christ come in the flesh: Cerinthian Gnosticism (Spirit on Jesus at baptism, left at crucifixion); Docetic Gnosticism (Jesus a phantom, not real manhood). “An antichrist” should read **the** antichrist (v. 7). [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee on 2 John 7]]
- Warning in v. 8 is danger of missing a **reward**, not losing salvation. Rejecting the doctrine of Christ marks those who are not real believers; doctrine is also a proof of life (v. 9). [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee on 2 John 8–9]]
- Do not receive into the house or greet the one who does not bring Christ’s teaching—itinerant teachers depended on home hospitality; believers must not support false teachers. One who supports a false teacher is partner in his error (vv. 10–11). [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee on 2 John 10–11]]
- 3 John shares the theme of truth: 2 John says truth is worth standing for; 3 John that truth is worth working for. Personalities structure the letter: Gaius (vv. 1–8), Diotrephes (vv. 9–11), Demetrius (vv. 12–14). [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee on 3 John theme and outline]]
- Gaius is “the beloved” four times; “in truth” means genuinely. John prays for his physical and material welfare. First “the truth” is apostolic doctrine/teaching; second “truth” (omit article) is conduct. “Walk in truth”—conduct conforms to doctrine. Perhaps Gaius was John’s convert (“my children”). [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee on 3 John 1–4]]
- John commends Gaius for receiving true teachers of the Word (vv. 5–8)—complement to 2 John’s ban on false teachers. Early missionaries were itinerants housed by believers because inns were wretched. [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee on 3 John 5–8]]
- Diotrephes “loveth to have the pre-eminence”: opposed missionary hospitality; five charges—(1) must occupy the leading place; (2) refused to receive John; (3) malicious statements against the apostles; (4) refused to entertain missionaries (wanted to do the teaching); (5) excommunicated those who did entertain them. “Diotrephes, the dictator”; evil deeds express false doctrine (vv. 9–11). [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee on 3 John 9–11]]
- Demetrius has good report of all and of the truth itself; name means belonging to Demeter (pagan convert); he adorned the doctrine of Christ. Two genuine men and one phony pass before the reader—gospel in shoe leather in pagan Rome (v. 12). John plans a visit rather than a longer letter (vv. 13–14). [[sources/personal-notes/ingest/2-3_John.pdf|McGee on 3 John 12–14]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Agrees with Henry Concise/Complete on 2–3 John: elect lady and children, joy in walking in truth, caution against deceivers/antichrist, refuse support to false teachers; Gaius’s hospitality and soul prosperity; Diotrephes’ pride; Demetrius’ good report ([[wiki/source-notes/Matthew Henry Concise on 2 John|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 John]], [[wiki/source-notes/Matthew Henry Concise on 3 John|Matthew Henry Concise on 3 John]]).
- Extends McGee’s 1 John anti-Gnostic frame ([[wiki/source-notes/McGee Notes and Outlines on 1 John|McGee on 1 John]]): same Cerinthian/Docetic denial of full humanity; 2–3 John put that doctrine into hospitality and church practice.
- Useful to [[wiki/concepts/Christ|Christ]] (incarnation/doctrine of Christ), [[wiki/concepts/Church|Church]] (hospitality, discipline of false teachers, local order), [[wiki/concepts/Pastoral Ministry|Pastoral Ministry]] (elder, preeminence vs example, receiving true teachers), [[wiki/concepts/Word of God|Word of God]] (truth as apostolic doctrine; walk in truth), and [[wiki/concepts/Holiness|Holiness]] (love bounded by truth; conduct conforming to doctrine).
- Single-author radio outline notes, not full critical commentary. “Elect lady = local church” is McGee’s lean, not insisted. Reward-not-salvation on 2 John 8, “the antichrist” rendering, and Diotrephes-as-would-be-Pope rhetoric should sit beside Henry and other sources. PDF currently staged under personal-notes ingest; optional move beside other McGee PDFs under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/` remains open.
