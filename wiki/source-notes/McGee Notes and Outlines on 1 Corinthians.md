# McGee Notes and Outlines on 1 Corinthians

## Summary

Intentional ingest of **J. Vernon McGee, Thru the Bible Notes & Outlines** on 1 Corinthians (immutable PDF under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/`; original raw path `raw/1_Corinthians.pdf`). McGee presents the letter as Paul’s reply correcting errors and confirming truth in carnal Corinth, with subject **the lordship of Jesus** (1 Corinthians 1:2, 3, 7–10). He dates the epistle about A.D. 55–57 (more likely 57) from Ephesus, outlines divisions, scandals, marriage, liberty, worship order, gifts, gospel/resurrection, and collections, and centers fellowship (*koinonia*) at 1:9 as partnership until the steadfast labor of 15:58. High-quality pdftotext extract supports book-level synthesis; chapter files under a McGee commentary tree are not yet promoted.

## Core claims

- Writer Paul; date A.D. 55–57 (more likely 57); place Ephesus; subject the lordship of Jesus (1 Corinthians 1:2, 3, 7–10). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf|McGee Notes & Outlines on 1 Corinthians]]
- Corinth was the commercial “Vanity Fair” of the Roman Empire (isthmus west of Athens; harbors Lechaeum and Cenchreae; Aphrodite temple with cult prostitution; Isthmian games). Against that background Paul founded the church (Acts 18:1–18) and wrote 1–2 Corinthians. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf|McGee on Corinth background]]
- The letter answers a prior Pauline letter (5:9) and a Corinthian delegation/letter (7:1; 16:17): correction of errors and confirmation of truth. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf|McGee on occasion]]
- Opening (1:1–9) names the Lord Jesus Christ six times; v. 9 is the heart—God is faithful; believers are called into the fellowship/partnership (*koinonia*) of His Son Jesus Christ our Lord (mutual concern, commitment, compassion). The body of the letter is a parenthesis until 15:58. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf|McGee on 1 Corinthians 1:1–9]]
- Divisions (1:10–4:21) arose from party spirit around Paul, Cephas, and Apollos; Christ crucified is God’s wisdom and power; Christ is the believer’s wisdom, righteousness, sanctification, and redemption (1:30)—positional, practical, and permanent sanctification distinguished. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf|McGee on divisions and sanctification]]
- Chapter 2: Paul’s preaching was Jesus Christ and Him crucified, not worldly wisdom; God’s wisdom is revealed by the Spirit; the natural man cannot receive it; the spiritual man discerns. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf|McGee on 1 Corinthians 2]]
- Chapter 3: Carnality shows in envy, strife, and divisions; humanity is natural (Jew/Gentile) or believer (carnal/spiritual); Christ is the only foundation; builders’ works (gold/silver/precious stones vs wood/hay/stubble) are tested by fire for reward or loss while the person may still be saved; the body is the temple of the Holy Spirit. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf|McGee on 1 Corinthians 3]]
- Chapter 4: Faithfulness is the servant’s supreme virtue; believers face three courts (others, conscience, the Master); stewards are judged for faithfulness and Christ will find something to praise in each. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf|McGee on 1 Corinthians 4]]
- Scandals: incest must be disciplined (deliver to Satan; refuse fellowship; cf. 2 Corinthians 2; 7); lawsuits among believers should be settled by saints who will judge the world and angels; the body is the Spirit’s temple (6:19–20). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf|McGee on 1 Corinthians 5–6]]
- Marriage (ch. 7): Paul lifts woman from chattel status to partner in love; he treats himself as unmarried/widower (Sanhedrin background); mixed marriage and departure leave the believer “not under bondage” (legal separation at least); new birth resets standing for those previously divorced if they can live single contentedly. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf|McGee on 1 Corinthians 7]]
- Christian liberty (8:1–11:1): knowledge puffs up, love builds up; meat offered to idols is nothing, yet love restrains liberty; minister’s liberty includes living by the gospel and becoming servant of all; Israel’s wilderness failures warn that liberty is not license; conduct is guided by glorifying God, not offending others, and following Christ (10:31–11:1). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf|McGee on liberty]]
- Woman’s dress (11:2–16): headship order—Christ head of man, man of woman, God of Christ—for order; Corinthian unveiled women signaled prostitution, so application is local; public approach to God must not use sex appeal. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf|McGee on 1 Corinthians 11:2–16]]
- Lord’s Table (11:17–34): highest expression of Christian worship; disordered agape meals discontinued; Paul received the institution by revelation; self-judgment prevents the Lord’s chastening. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf|McGee on Lord’s Supper]]
- Spiritual gifts (12–14): solution to divisions is the Spirit; gifts maintain unity in diversity—Spirit bestows, Christ administers, Father energizes; body metaphor; baptism of the Spirit places believers into Christ’s body; love (ch. 13) is the energy and value of all gifts and a biography of Christ; prophecy preferred to tongues; tongues are real languages unknown to the speaker, not “unknown” tongues; order and women’s silence regarding tongues in assembly (14:34). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf|McGee on 1 Corinthians 12–14]]
- Gospel/resurrection (ch. 15): resurrection is integral to the gospel (vv. 1–4); many witnesses; parade of resurrection (Christ firstfruits, church, OT/Tribulation saints, kingdom, death destroyed, Son subject to Father); resurrection is bodily (*anastasis nekron*); first church heresy denied bodily resurrection against Stoic/Epicurean/Platonic alternatives; Rapture change in a moment (vv. 51–58); labor not in vain (v. 58 ties to 1:9). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf|McGee on 1 Corinthians 15]]
- Collections (16:1–9) on the first day as God prospers; closing personalities, watchfulness, love-motivated action; *Maranatha*—our Lord cometh. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1_Corinthians.pdf|McGee on 1 Corinthians 16]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Agrees with Henry Concise/Complete atlas that 1 Corinthians corrects church disorders, centers Christ crucified, prefers love over showy gifts, and grounds hope in bodily resurrection ([[wiki/source-notes/Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Corinthians|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Corinthians]]).
- Adds sharper lordship/fellowship frame (1:9 → 15:58), natural/carnal/spiritual anthropology, three-court stewardship judgment, and detailed liberty and gifts outlines useful to [[wiki/concepts/Church|Church]], [[wiki/concepts/Holiness|Holiness]], [[wiki/concepts/Holy Spirit|Holy Spirit]], [[wiki/concepts/Resurrection|Resurrection]], and [[wiki/concepts/Hope|Hope]].
- Dispensational parade of resurrection (OT saints, Tribulation saints, kingdom sequence) and cessationist lean on tongues (gift ended early; discourages Corinthian tongues practice) should be held beside other sources, not as the vault’s sole word.
- Marriage remarriage-after-divorce and head-covering applications are McGee’s pastoral readings of Corinthian context; treat as single-author outline notes for radio listeners, not full critical commentary.
- Corpus is a book-level PDF under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/`; optional split into chapter files under the same folder remains open (same pattern as Pastoral Epistles McGee note).
