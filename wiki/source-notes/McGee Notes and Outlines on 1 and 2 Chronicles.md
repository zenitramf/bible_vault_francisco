# McGee Notes and Outlines on 1 and 2 Chronicles

## Summary

Intentional ingest of **J. Vernon McGee, Thru the Bible Notes & Outlines** on 1 & 2 Chronicles (immutable PDF under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/`; original raw path `raw/1-2_Chronicles.pdf`, briefly mis-staged under `sources/personal-notes/ingest/` then promoted). McGee titles Chronicles “the ACTS of the Old Testament” and “Things Omitted” (Greek title), treating them as law-of-recurrence history: same ground as Kings from Saul to Zedekiah, but from the altar rather than the throne—religious history and God’s viewpoint versus political history and man’s viewpoint. David and the house of David, the temple and Jerusalem, and Judah’s line dominate; David’s sin with Bathsheba is omitted (when God forgives, He forgets); five Judah reformations (Asa, Jehoshaphat, Joash, Hezekiah, Josiah) are enlarged as returns to the Word of God. Probable writer Ezra (style affinity with Ezra–Nehemiah; possible compilation of priestly/prophetic journals; Jewish tradition). High-quality pdftotext extract supports book-level synthesis; chapter files under a McGee commentary tree are not yet promoted. Paired throne-side outline: [[wiki/source-notes/McGee Notes and Outlines on 1 and 2 Kings|McGee Notes and Outlines on 1 and 2 Kings]].

## Core claims

- Writer probably Ezra; striking resemblance in style and language to Ezra and Nehemiah; Chronicles written during or around the Babylonian captivity as a possible compilation of priests’ and prophets’ diaries; originally one book with Ezra and Nehemiah in Jewish tradition. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Chronicles.pdf|McGee Notes & Outlines on 1–2 Chronicles]]
- Chronicles is not a mere duplication of Kings: Greek title “Things Omitted”; law of recurrence (as in Genesis 2 and Deuteronomy) adds detail and emphasis God considers important. David is the subject of 1 Chronicles; the house of David is prominent in 2 Chronicles; northern kingdom is practically ignored. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Chronicles.pdf|McGee on Chronicles vs Kings]]
- Viewpoint contrast: Kings gives history from the throne (palace, political, man’s viewpoint); Chronicles from the altar (temple, religious, God’s viewpoint); Chronicles is an interpretation of Kings—hence Kings’ constant reference to Chronicles. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Chronicles.pdf|McGee on viewpoint]]
- David’s sin with Bathsheba is not recorded in Chronicles (noted at 1 Chronicles 20 / Rabbah context); McGee reads this as God’s forgetting forgiven sin. David’s numbering of the people is recorded (1 Chronicles 21) because God permitted him to choose punishment; Satan is named as mastermind of the pride (v. 1). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Chronicles.pdf|McGee on David’s sins]]
- 1 Chronicles outline: genealogies (chs. 1–9) matter to God—sons before work; “Ye must be born again” (John 3:7); they help explain Matthew’s and Luke’s genealogies of Christ (compare 1 Chronicles 3:5 with Luke 3:31); Saul’s reign (ch. 10) is brief from God’s viewpoint with reason for death (v. 13); David’s reign (chs. 11–29) covers mighty men, ark, temple desire and covenant, wars, numbering, and temple preparation/organization. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Chronicles.pdf|McGee 1 Chronicles outline]]
- Genealogical highlights: Adam through David and Levi through the captivity (Ezra 2:62); Reuben lost the birthright to Joseph, yet Judah prevailed and the ruler came from Judah (1 Chronicles 5:1–2); captivity reason for eastern tribes (5:25–26); Aaron’s official occupation (6:49); importance of genealogies for Christ’s humanity (9:1; Matthew 1; Luke 3). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Chronicles.pdf|McGee on genealogies]]
- David and the ark (chs. 13–16): cart attempt fails; God does not dwell in a material house “between the cherubim” as if confined (13:6); ark brought by God’s original instructions (15:2); Michal rejected as mother of the royal line (15:29); sacrifices speaking of Christ emphasized (16:1–3); choir, psalm of praise, priests in courses. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Chronicles.pdf|McGee on David and the ark]]
- David and the temple: desire delights the Lord; Davidic covenant repeated (ch. 17; cf. 2 Samuel 7). Wars expand the kingdom yet Israel occupied only about 30,000 of 300,000 square miles given. Temple should be called David’s temple not Solomon’s—David planned and gathered materials; God forbade him because he had shed much blood (22:8–9); blueprint and encouragement to Solomon (28:11–13, 20–21); final national word concerns building the temple (ch. 29). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Chronicles.pdf|McGee on David and the temple]]
- David’s prayer (1 Chronicles 29:10–19) is one of the great prayers of Scripture—all-comprehensive, majestic, adoration, praise, thanksgiving; repudiates human merit, declares dependence, self-humiliation, confession, dedication; McGee says it was evidently used by our Lord in the so-called Lord’s Prayer. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Chronicles.pdf|McGee on 1 Chronicles 29 prayer]]
- 2 Chronicles outline: Solomon’s reign (chs. 1–9)—building the temple his most important accomplishment; division and Judah only thereafter (chs. 10–36); five reformations given prominence—Asa (14–16), Jehoshaphat (17–20), Joash (23–24), Hezekiah (29–32), Josiah (34–35). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Chronicles.pdf|McGee 2 Chronicles outline]]
- Solomon: prays for wisdom (1:10), probably at David’s suggestion (1 Chronicles 22:12)—divine wisdom to rule yet not ordering personal life; temple on Mt. Moriah where Abraham offered Isaac (3:1; Genesis 22:2); temple proper twice tabernacle size; innovations (larger altar, ten lavers, etc.) take away tabernacle simplicity and plain references to Christ—Hebrews uses the tabernacle, not the temple, as the figure of Christ’s person and work. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Chronicles.pdf|McGee on Solomon’s temple]]
- Dedication: ark to permanent place, staves removed; pot of manna and Aaron’s rod already removed (5:9–10); glory fills the temple as formerly the tabernacle (5:13–14)—God’s approval. Solomon’s dedication prayer (ch. 6): heavens cannot contain God; temple’s future place in God–Israel relationship; Daniel opens toward Jerusalem (Daniel 6:10). God accepts sacrifices by fire (7:1); condition of blessing—2 Chronicles 7:14 has direct reference to Israel. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Chronicles.pdf|McGee on dedication and 7:14]]
- Queen of Sheba (ch. 9): Israel’s witness was nations coming to Jerusalem to worship (Psalm 122:1–2), not going out—contrast with the church’s command to go to the world; temple for all peoples (1 Kings 8:41–43; 2 Chronicles 6:32–33); burnt offering as complete picture of Christ (Romans 3:21–22; Matthew 12:42). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Chronicles.pdf|McGee on witness and Sheba]]
- Division under Rehoboam’s stupidity (ch. 10); mothers’ names of good and bad kings given—mother shares responsibility in God’s sight (noted at Rehoboam, 12:13). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Chronicles.pdf|McGee on Rehoboam and mothers]]
- Five revival periods characterized as return and obedience to the Word of God: Asa 15:8–9; Jehoshaphat 17:3–4; Joash 23:16–21; 24:1–6; Hezekiah 29:3–36 and Passover (Christ) 30:1, 15–16; Josiah 34:18–21. Return to the Word led to repentance of the people and the reformation of the nation. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Chronicles.pdf|McGee on reformations]]
- Closing (ch. 36): Jeremiah’s explanation of seventy years—land enjoyed her sabbaths (v. 21); decree of Cyrus to return and rebuild the temple; seventy years of captivity passed over entirely as “time out” when people are out of the will of God—God’s clock spelled I-S-R-A-E-L, running only while Israel is in the land. Only written prophecy of Elijah appears in 2 Chronicles 21:12–15. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Chronicles.pdf|McGee on captivity close]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Agrees with Henry Concise/Complete Chronicles atlas that temple service, David’s line, dedication glory, and Judah’s fall/Cyrus restoration are central ([[wiki/source-notes/Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Chronicles|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Chronicles]]; [[wiki/source-notes/Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Chronicles|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Chronicles]]).
- Adds sharp Kings-vs-Chronicles viewpoint frame (throne/altar; man/God), intentional omission of Bathsheba as forgiven-and-forgotten, “David’s temple” emphasis, tabernacle-not-temple typology for Hebrews, and reformations-as-return-to-the-Word grid useful to [[wiki/concepts/Worship|Worship]], [[wiki/concepts/Covenant|Covenant]], [[wiki/concepts/Prayer|Prayer]], [[wiki/concepts/Repentance|Repentance]], and [[wiki/concepts/Word of God|Word of God]].
- Paired with [[wiki/source-notes/McGee Notes and Outlines on 1 and 2 Kings|McGee on 1–2 Kings]] for the throne/political narrative (division, Elijah–Elisha, captivity etiology) that Chronicles interprets from the altar.
- Dispensational Israel-clock and “7:14 direct to Israel only” applications, and the claim that the Lord’s Prayer drew on 1 Chronicles 29:10–19, are McGee’s radio-outline readings—hold beside other sources, not as the vault’s sole word.
- Chronological kings table and recommended-books list are study aids for Thru the Bible listeners, not independent critical apparatus.
- Corpus is a book-level PDF under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/` (same home as other McGee Notes & Outlines); optional chapter split remains open.
