# McGee Notes and Outlines on Colossians

## Summary

Intentional ingest of **J. Vernon McGee, Thru the Bible Notes & Outlines** on Colossians (immutable PDF under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/`; original raw path `raw/Colossians.pdf`). McGee treats Colossians as a prison epistle (about A.D. 62) written by Paul to a church he had not visited (Colossians 2:1), founded through Ephesian ministry influence and pastored by Epaphras. The letter answers oriental mysticism and Gnostic intrusion (exclusive spirit, demiurge creation chain that made Christ a creature, asceticism and licentiousness). Doctrinal chapters 1–2 center on Christ as the *pleroma* (fullness) of God in whom believers are made full; practical chapters 3–4 pour that fullness into heavenly-minded, holy, relational living and hearty fellowship. McGee contrasts prison epistles: Ephesians stresses the body (church) with Christ as Head; Colossians stresses the Head (Christ) with the body secondary (1:18); Philippians the *kenosis* (Servant); Colossians the *pleroma* (fullness of God, 2:9). High-quality pdf-inspector extract (19 pages) supports book-level synthesis.

## Core claims

- Writer Paul (Colossians 1:1); date about A.D. 62; Paul had never been to Colosse (2:1) but treated the church as his own after fruitful Ephesian ministry (Acts 19:8–19); Colosse lay 75–100 miles east of Ephesus; Epaphras was minister (1:4–8; 4:12–13); Philemon was apparently among visitors who heard Paul; Paul intended a visit when released (Philemon 22). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Colossians.pdf|McGee Notes & Outlines on Colossians]]
- Problem: oriental mysticism and Gnostic intrusion with Greek pantheistic demiurge philosophy. Three Gnostic marks McGee answers: exclusive aristocratic spirit (1:28); speculative creation chain treating Christ as a creature (1:15–19; 2:18); ethical extremes of asceticism (2:16, 23) and licentiousness (3:5–9). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Colossians.pdf|McGee on Colossian problem]]
- Message: pure Christianity sails between evaporating into philosophy and freezing into form (Scofield cited); do not add to or subtract from Christ. Wesley line: “Thou, O Christ, art all I want; more than all in Thee I find.” Outline: I. Doctrinal chs. 1–2 — in Christ the fullness (*pleroma*) of God we are made full; II. Practical chs. 3–4 — that fullness poured out in life through believers. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Colossians.pdf|McGee on message and outline]]
- Colossians 1:1–8: saints and faithful are the same people; thanksgiving goes directly to God the Father (against Gnostic mediated access); faith–love–hope trinity of graces (past–present–future); gospel already bearing fruit in the Roman *kosmos*; Epaphras may have founded the church; love is fruit of the Spirit. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Colossians.pdf|McGee on Colossians 1:1–8]]
- Colossians 1:9–14: Paul prays for *epignosin* (superknowledge) confined to the will of God in the Word—wisdom and spiritual understanding (wisdom-forms occur forty times in the epistle); walk worthy of the Lord, increasing in knowledge; strength and joy from God by the Spirit; inheritance with saints in light; delivered from Satan’s kingdom into the kingdom of the Son of his love (present kingdom aspect); forgiveness associated with Christ’s blood; redemption (*apolutrosin*) sets free an enslaved people. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Colossians.pdf|McGee on Colossians 1:9–14]]
- Colossians 1:15–19: nine marks answering heresy on Christ’s person (Arianism, Socinus, Unitarianism, Jehovah’s Witnesses, etc.): (1) image (*eikon*) of the invisible God—could not be image unless he was God; (2) firstborn (*prototokos*) of all creation—priority and headship, not first creature; (3) all things created by him (visible and invisible ranks); (4) all things created *for* him; (5) he is before all things; (6) all things consist (*sunesteken*)—he holds creation together; (7) head of the body, the church, and firstborn from the dead; (8) preeminence in all things; (9) fullness (*pleroma*) dwells in him—Jesus 100% God, not 99.44%. Relations: to Father (v. 15), creation (vv. 16–17), church (vv. 18–19), cross (v. 20). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Colossians.pdf|McGee on Colossians 1:15–19]]
- Colossians 1:20–23: peace through the blood of his cross; reconciliation toward man—God reconciled, calling man to be reconciled (2 Corinthians 5:18–20); “all things” limited to things appointed for reconciliation; heaven prepared to receive us (John 14:2); mental as well as moral alienation explains intellectual antagonism; “body of his flesh” answers Docetic Gnosticism; unblamable/unreprovable sacrificial language; “if ye continue” is argumentative *since*, not conditional future uncertainty. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Colossians.pdf|McGee on Colossians 1:20–23]]
- Colossians 1:24–29: Paul fills up what is lacking of Christ’s afflictions for the body—not redemptive sufferings (human, Son-of-God, sin-sacrifice) but shareable sufferings for righteousness and gospel identification; dispensation as stewardship; mystery as sacred secret—Christ in you, looking to likeness (1 John 3:2); goal “perfect” means complete in Christ. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Colossians.pdf|McGee on Colossians 1:24–29]]
- Colossians 2:1–15 (Christ answer to philosophy, for the head): prayer agony for Colosse and Laodicea; mystery of God is Christ in incarnation; all treasures of wisdom and knowledge in him; beware spoil of philosophy, tradition of men, and ABC rudiments; in him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily (clear deity statement); complete/ready for the voyage of life in him; true circumcision is new birth; buried and risen with him in baptism (identification; cf. Romans 6); not improvement of old nature but impartation of new; Law as way of life removed by the cross; spiritual victory over principalities. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Colossians.pdf|McGee on Colossians 2:1–15]]
- Colossians 2:16–23 (Christ answer to ritual, for the heart): do not be judged in food/days—Law rituals were shadow/photograph of Christ; now the reality has come; condemns Gnostic pretense of wisdom and angel intrusion; loose hold on the Head blocks growth; since believers died with Christ, do not return to pre-cross living and ordinances/fads; ascetic “touch not, taste not” is empty pride aping humility, of no honor. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Colossians.pdf|McGee on Colossians 2:16–23]]
- Colossians 3:1–4: raised with Christ—seek and set mind on things above; “ye are dead” is *ye have died* with Christ; when he appears, believers appear with him. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Colossians.pdf|McGee on Colossians 3:1–4]]
- Colossians 3:5–4:6: mortify members (energies of the old man)—specific sins catalogued (fornication, uncleanness, passion, evil desire, covetousness as must-have-more-ness); put off old-man garments (malice, filthy communication, lying); put on new-man wardrobe (compassion, kindness, humility, meekness, longsuffering, forbearing and forgiving as Christ forgave—gospel not legal basis of the so-called Lord’s Prayer alone); love and peace as Spirit fruit; word of Christ dwell at home—Spirit-filled parallel to Ephesians 5:18–20; all done in the name of the Lord Jesus; household codes (wives, husbands, children, fathers, servants) as in Ephesians 5–6; service unto Christ receives reward; masters give just and equal account to the Master in heaven—level up, not level down; prayer (persevere; inhale prayer / exhale thanksgiving; pray for gospel door), walk in wisdom toward outsiders, speech with grace seasoned with salt. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Colossians.pdf|McGee on Colossians 3:5–4:6]]
- Colossians 4:7–18: roster of faithful workers in pagan Rome (like Romans 16 / Hebrews 11)—Tychicus, Onesimus (returned runaway, Philemon), Aristarchus, Mark (restored), Epaphras (prison prayer ministry), Luke the beloved physician; “Remember my bonds” as motto for witnessing under Paul’s imprisonment. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Colossians.pdf|McGee on Colossians 4:7–18]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Agrees with Henry Concise/Complete Colossians atlas on glorious view of Christ, caution against vain philosophy and ritual, heavenly-mindedness, mortification, mutual love/forgiveness, household duties, persevering prayer, and commendation of co-workers ([[wiki/source-notes/Matthew Henry Concise on Colossians|Matthew Henry Concise on Colossians]]).
- Adds sharp anti-Gnostic *pleroma*/nine-mark Christology, prison-epistle Head-vs-body contrast with Ephesians, Docetic answer at 1:22, argumentative “if” at 1:23, shareable vs unshareable sufferings frame, baptism-as-identification with Romans 6, and put-off/put-on garment ethics—useful to [[wiki/concepts/Christ|Christ]], [[wiki/concepts/Church|Church]], [[wiki/concepts/Holiness|Holiness]], and [[wiki/concepts/Prayer|Prayer]].
- “Jesus was 100% God—not 99.44%,” “monkey business” on ascetic taboos, and “must-have-more-ness” for covetousness are McGee radio color; hold as pastoral outline notes for Thru the Bible listeners, not as the vault’s sole exegesis.
- Forgiveness “rather than the legal basis given in the so-called Lord’s Prayer” is McGee’s contrast of gospel forgiveness (as Christ forgave) with reciprocal petition language—pair with Matthew 6 synthesis, not as dismissal of the Lord’s Prayer.
- Corpus is a single Colossians PDF; optional split into chapter files under the same folder remains open (same pattern as other McGee notes).
