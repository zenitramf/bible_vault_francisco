# McGee Notes and Outlines on 1 and 2 Thessalonians

## Summary

Intentional ingest of **J. Vernon McGee, Thru the Bible Notes & Outlines** on 1 and 2 Thessalonians (immutable PDF under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/`; original raw path `raw/1-2_Thessalonians.pdf`). McGee treats 1 Thessalonians as Paul’s earliest epistle (A.D. 52–53, from Athens or more likely Corinth), written after a brief stay in model-church Thessalonica, to confirm young converts, condition them for holy living, and comfort them regarding Christ’s return for believers. He organizes 1 Thessalonians around the coming of Christ as inspiring, working, purifying, comforting, and rousing hope, with a detailed treatment of sleep/death, the Rapture (`harpazo`), and deliverance from the wrath to come. 2 Thessalonians answers a false report that the day of the Lord had already arrived: the Rapture (“our gathering together unto him”) is still future; the day of the Lord follows total apostasy and the man of sin, with the Holy Spirit as restrainer; practical sections establish believers in the Word, walk, and work. High-quality firecrawl-parse extract (30 pages) supports book-level synthesis.

## Core claims

- Writer Paul; date A.D. 52–53; 1 Thessalonians is Paul’s earliest epistle, written from Athens or more likely Corinth on the second missionary journey after Timothy’s report and questions (1 Thessalonians 3:6). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Thessalonians.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on 1–2 Thessalonians]]
- Thessalonica was a Roman colony and chief city of Macedonia (about 100 miles west of Philippi, 200 north of Athens); the church was a model Paul cited to Corinth (1 Thessalonians 1:7; 2 Corinthians 8:1–5). Paul was there less than a month (Acts 17:2) yet taught major doctrines including the second coming. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Thessalonians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on Thessalonica background]]
- Purpose of 1 Thessalonians: (1) confirm young converts in elementary gospel truth; (2) condition them for holy living; (3) comfort them regarding the return of Christ. Against a heathen inscription denying revival after death, Paul emphasizes comfort (1 Thessalonians 4:18). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Thessalonians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on purpose]]
- Theme distinction: Christ’s coming *for* believers is comfort; His catastrophic coming in glory to establish the kingdom (Revelation 19:11–16) is a different phase. Outline frames the coming as inspiring hope (ch. 1), working hope (ch. 2), purifying hope (3:1–4:12), comforting hope (4:13–18), and rousing hope leading to action (ch. 5). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Thessalonians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on theme and outline]]
- 1 Thessalonians 1: faith, love, and hope produce work, labor, and patience; election is God’s side while “whosoever will” remains the human side; gospel came in power and the Holy Spirit, not mere words; Thessalonians turned to God from idols (work of faith), to serve the living God (labor of love), and to wait for His Son from heaven (patience of hope), delivered from “the wrath to come” (Great Tribulation). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Thessalonians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 1 Thessalonians 1]]
- 1 Thessalonians 2: true witness without deceit, uncleanness, or guile; mother side (gentle nursing love), father side (exhort, comfort/persuade, charge; walk worthy of kingdom and glory), brother side (shared suffering); crown of rejoicing is those won to Christ at His coming. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Thessalonians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 1 Thessalonians 2]]
- 1 Thessalonians 3–4:12: believers are appointed to affliction; afflictions promote sanctification and test genuine faith; love seeks another’s welfare and aims at unblamable holiness at Christ’s coming with His saints; walk is by Spirit-enabled commandments (not the Ten Commandments as the Christian standard); chastity, brotherly love, quiet ambition to work with one’s hands, and honest walk before outsiders. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Thessalonians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 1 Thessalonians 3–4:12]]
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18: primary question is the fate of believers who die before the Rapture, not only the Rapture itself. “Sleep” (*koimaomai*) is of the body, never the soul—no soul sleep; body returns to dust, spirit to God; Christian death is absence from the body and presence with the Lord. Jesus *died* (substitutionary); believers *sleep in Jesus*. By the word of the Lord, the living will not precede the dead; the Lord descends with a shout (solo voice of the Son, trumpet-like); dead in Christ rise first; living are “caught up” (*harpazo*—snatched up, raptured); this comforts believers and delivers from Tribulation terror. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Thessalonians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18]]
- 1 Thessalonians 5: the church looks for a Person, not times and seasons; “day of the Lord” begins with the Great Tribulation and runs through the Millennium (relates to Israel); the Lord does not come to the church as a thief; world false peace of Antichrist precedes Tribulation; believers are children of light, not appointed to wrath; twenty-two practical commandments (comfort/edify, esteem leaders, peace, warn/encourage/support/patience, no retaliation, rejoice, pray without ceasing, give thanks, quench not the Spirit, despise not prophesyings, prove all things, abstain from appearance of evil, etc.); man as body, soul, and spirit; God is faithful to sanctify wholly. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Thessalonians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 1 Thessalonians 5]]
- 2 Thessalonians occasion: a false letter/report claimed Christ had already gathered the church and that present persecution was the day of the Lord; Paul states “our gathering together unto him” is future (2:1) and that apostasy and the man of sin must come first. Theme: 1 Thessalonians treats the Rapture; 2 Thessalonians treats Christ’s return to earth in judgment destroying the man of sin (2:8); the interval is the Great Tribulation, identified with Daniel’s 70th week (seven years). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Thessalonians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 2 Thessalonians occasion and theme]]
- Day of the Lord vs day of Christ: KJV “day of Christ” at 2:2 is unfortunate—should be “day of the Lord”; day of Christ relates to the church age/coming for the church; day of the Lord begins after the Rapture with Tribulation through Millennium. Two signs: falling away (apostasy) and revelation of the man of sin (lawless one; little horn of Daniel 7), who assumes God’s place. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Thessalonians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on day of the Lord]]
- Restrainer (2:6–7) is the Holy Spirit, who alone can hinder satanic lawlessness; when He is removed (with the church at the Rapture), the lawless one is revealed in the Tribulation and destroyed by Christ’s coming (Revelation 19:20). God sends strong delusion to those who refused the truth. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Thessalonians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on restrainer and man of sin]]
- 2 Thessalonians 1: persecution produced growing faith, love, and patience; God is righteous to recompense tribulation to troublers and rest to believers at Christ’s revelation; the lost are those who know not God and obey not the gospel. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Thessalonians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 2 Thessalonians 1]]
- Practicality (2:13–3:18): full salvation spectrum—chosen, sanctification of the Spirit, belief of the truth, glory/Rapture; stand in the Word; walk in love and patient waiting, not with the disorderly; work for one’s own bread (“no work—no food”); avoid busybodies; do not grow weary; withdraw from the disobedient yet admonish as a brother; prophecy study brings peace, not fanaticism or laziness. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Thessalonians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 2 Thessalonians practical sections]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Agrees with Henry Concise/Complete Thessalonians atlas on comfort for the bereaved, holy walk, alertness for Christ’s coming, warning about the man of sin, and industrious ordered life ([[wiki/source-notes/Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Thessalonians|Matthew Henry Concise on 1 Thessalonians]]; [[wiki/source-notes/Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Thessalonians|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Thessalonians]]).
- Adds a sharp pre-tribulational, two-phase second-coming frame (Rapture for the church vs day-of-the-Lord/kingdom judgment), body-only “sleep,” *harpazo* Rapture semantics, Holy Spirit as restrainer (vs Henry’s common Roman-empire suggestion), and wrath-as-Great-Tribulation deliverance—useful to [[wiki/concepts/Hope|Hope]], [[wiki/concepts/Resurrection|Resurrection]], [[wiki/concepts/Holiness|Holiness]], and [[wiki/concepts/Suffering|Suffering]], but clearly dispensational outline notes for radio listeners, not the vault’s sole eschatology.
- Tension with Henry Concise on 2 Thessalonians 2:5–12: Henry supposes the restrainer may be Roman imperial power; McGee identifies the Holy Spirit. Hold both as sourced readings.
- Election wording (“no write-in candidates” plus sincere “whosoever will”) and “Ten Commandments are not the standard for Christian conduct” are McGee’s pastoral formulations; pair with wider Pauline and vault holiness synthesis.
- Corpus is a combined 1–2 Thessalonians PDF; optional split into per-book or chapter files under the same folder remains open (same pattern as other McGee notes).
