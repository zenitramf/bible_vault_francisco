# ¿De Quién Es Tu Dinero? — Ringo Ayala

## Summary

Pastor Ringo Ayala (Iglesia Bautista de Santa Ana) opens the series "Por fe damos" (By faith we give) with the message "Por fe reconocemos al dueño" (By faith we recognize the Owner). The sermon establishes three faith-rooted convictions: God owns everything by creation, by preservation, and by provision; therefore the Christian is a steward (mayordomo), not an owner; and giving is the act of returning to God what He first gave. Giving by faith is distinguished from giving by pressure, manipulation, or guilt, and is grounded in gratitude for grace already received. Stewardship covers not only money but time, health, gifts, family, service, opportunities, and resources, all of which will be reviewed at the judgment seat of Christ. The series is preached in the context of an immigrant Spanish-speaking congregation in California, and addresses the church's role as a cause worth investing in.

## Core claims

- God is the owner of everything in heaven and on earth by creation, preservation, and provision; therefore no portion of life or possessions is ultimately the believer's own. [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#3:13|Source]] [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#5:02|Source]] [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#8:02|Source]]
- The believer is a steward (mayordomo) of what God has entrusted, not an absolute proprietor; the proper response to God's ownership is faithful administration, not ownership-talk ("mío, mío, mío"). [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#14:10|Source]] [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#16:23|Source]] [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#33:00|Source]]
- Giving is returning to God what He first gave, not a favor done to a needy God; David's "de lo recibido de tu mano te damos" (1 Chr 29:14) reframes all Christian giving as a grateful echo of prior grace. [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#14:15|Source]] [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#21:08|Source]] [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#26:41|Source]]
- Faithfulness, not equal amounts, is God's requirement: "fiel en lo poco, fiel en lo mucho" (1 Cor 4:2), faithful in abundance and in trial, because God does not require the same amount from every believer. [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#16:23|Source]] [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#16:56|Source]] [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#17:15|Source]]
- Stewardship will be reviewed at the Bema (Rom 14:12): believers give account to God not for sin but for what they did with what was entrusted—time, health, gifts, family, service, opportunities, and resources. [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#19:25|Source]] [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#20:06|Source]]
- Giving is voluntary and joy-rooted, not coerced, manipulated, or guilt-driven; the believer is not saved by giving nor lost for not giving, but gives as a privilege in response to grace already received. [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#22:02|Source]] [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#24:36|Source]] [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#27:01|Source]]
- The church's work—preaching, discipleship, classes, missions, children, youth, families, new believers, mercy, facilities, evangelism, church planting—is the highest cause to which a believer can invest; the gospel cause outranks any temporal charity. [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#27:50|Source]] [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#29:06|Source]] [[sources/transcripts/De Quién Es Tu Dinero - Ringo Ayala#29:37|Source]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Agreeing with the existing Stewardship concept: the sermon reinforces the same root idea that all is entrusted and that giving flows from grace, not from obligation. The Macedonian pattern of self-giving first (2 Cor 8:5) is paralleled by David's "we give you what we received from your hand."
- Tension: the sermon explicitly denies that giving is salvific ("Una persona no tiene que dar para ser salva"), which is consistent with [[wiki/concepts/Grace|Grace]] and [[wiki/concepts/Justification|Justification]] but worth noting for any future work that might slide toward "giving as proof of faith."
- Cultural context: preached to a Spanish-speaking immigrant congregation in California; the framing of "mayordomía cristiana" sits within Latin American evangelical stewardship teaching, distinct from English-language Reformed stewardship vocabulary.
- The parable of the talents (Mt 25) is alluded to (the one-talent servant) but not cited by reference; treated here as an implicit parallel for the "faithful with much / faithful with little" claim.
