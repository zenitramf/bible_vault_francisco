# McGee Notes and Outlines on Pastoral Epistles and Philemon

## Summary

Intentional re-ingest of **J. Vernon McGee, Thru the Bible Notes & Outlines** on 1 Timothy, 2 Timothy, Titus, and Philemon (immutable PDF under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/`; prior cleaned-markdown path under `raw/` was faulty and withdrawn). McGee labels the Timothy/Titus letters **Pastoral Epistles**—Paul to young pastor-converts, yet messages for churches and orderly local assemblies—with particular force for young pastors and the present-day church. **1 Timothy** (about A.D. 64) centers government and order in the **local** church over against Ephesians’ invisible body; key verses 1:3 and 3:15. **2 Timothy** (A.D. 67, swan song) is loyalty in days of apostasy; key verses 2:15 and 4:2. **Titus** measures the ideal NT church: orderly organization, sound doctrine, pure life, ready for every good work; blessed hope still grips Paul (2:13). **Philemon** (about A.D. 62) illustrates substitution/imputation and brotherly love across master–slave classes. High-quality pdftotext extract supports book-level synthesis.

## Core claims

- The Timothy and Titus letters are Pastoral Epistles: written by Paul to young converts established as pastors, yet the message is for churches and for orderly procedure in local and visible assemblies; they speak especially to young pastors and the present-day church. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Timothy-Titus-Philemon.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on Pastoral Epistles]]
- 1 Timothy’s theme is government and order in the local church, in contrast to Ephesians’ focus on the church as Christ’s invisible body; key verses are 1 Timothy 1:3 and 3:15; sound doctrine and correct conduct identify the local church (doctrine, godliness, teach/teacher, good as keyword clusters). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Timothy-Titus-Philemon.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on Pastoral Epistles]]
- McGee dates 1 Timothy about A.D. 64 (interval after release from Rome, probably from Macedonia, Timothy left at Ephesus); Titus in the same window; 2 Timothy about A.D. 67 as Paul’s deathbed swan song before death in Rome. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Timothy-Titus-Philemon.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on Pastoral Epistles]]
- Elder and bishop are the same person: *presbuteros* names the person, *episkopos* the office (some good men disagree); qualifications emphasize character, household management, and aptitude to teach; the elder is God’s steward, not self-willed (1 Timothy 3; Titus 1). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Timothy-Titus-Philemon.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on Pastoral Epistles]]
- Apostasy is total, deliberate desertion of the faith by those who know gospel truth—not ignorance; 2 Timothy forecasts end-time church apostasy while the invisible church goes on to glory; “Preach the word” is the last-days rallying cry (2 Timothy 3–4). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Timothy-Titus-Philemon.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on Pastoral Epistles]]
- In days of apostasy the Scriptures are the believer’s only source of help: every passage is God-breathed and profitable for teaching, conviction, setting right, and discipline so the man of God is complete for every good work (2 Timothy 3:16–17). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Timothy-Titus-Philemon.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on Pastoral Epistles]]
- Titus presents the ideal New Testament church: orderly organization, sound doctrine, pure life, and readiness for every good work; the blessed hope of Christ’s appearing still grips Paul late in life (Titus 2:13). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Timothy-Titus-Philemon.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on Pastoral Epistles]]
- Philemon’s primary purpose is to reveal Christ’s love in substitution and imputation (“put that on mine account”; receive him as myself); its practical purpose is brotherly love across master–slave classes—the only solution McGee names for capital-and-labor hostility. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Timothy-Titus-Philemon.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on Pastoral Epistles]]
- 1 Timothy 1: sound doctrine’s purpose is love in Christian living; the law is morally excellent for conduct but cannot save; Paul was literally the chief of sinners as pattern of mercy; apostates (Hymenaeus, Alexander) had already appeared. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Timothy-Titus-Philemon.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on Pastoral Epistles]]
- 2 Timothy frames the minister with seven figures (son, soldier, athlete, farmer, workman, vessel, servant); “rightly dividing” is handling the Word rightly; last-days description lists nineteen vices including form of godliness without power. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Timothy-Titus-Philemon.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on Pastoral Epistles]]

## Agreements and tensions

- McGee agrees with Henry and the vault’s Pastoral Ministry concept that local-church order, elder character, and feeding under Christ are central; he adds a sharper local-vs-invisible church contrast and a dispensational reading of end-time apostasy, Rapture, and kingdom (e.g. 2 Timothy 4:1, 8; Titus 2:13).
- On women in public prayer and teaching (1 Timothy 2), McGee’s restrictions (order, headship; mystery-religion background; no public tongues) should be held beside other sources rather than treated as the vault’s sole word.
- Elder/bishop identity is McGee’s stated position; he notes some good men disagree.
- Apostasy-as-near-blotting-out of “the faith” and rejection of social-gospel optimism are strong single-author outline claims; record without forcing vault-wide consensus.
- Corpus is single-author outline notes for Thru the Bible radio listeners, not full critical commentary. PDF and extract live under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/`; optional chapter-level split remains open.
