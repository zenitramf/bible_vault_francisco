# McGee Notes and Outlines on Pastoral Epistles and Philemon

## Summary

Intentional ingest of **J. Vernon McGee, Thru the Bible Notes & Outlines** on 1 Timothy, 2 Timothy, Titus, and Philemon (PDF from Bible_LLM_Raw). McGee frames the Pastorals as instructions for orderly local-church life and young pastors, contrasts the local assembly with the invisible body (Ephesians), defines apostasy as deliberate departure from the faith, and reads Philemon as a living illustration of substitution and imputation. Cleaned full text is staged at [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon|McGee Notes & Outlines — Pastoral Epistles and Philemon]]; promotion into `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/` chapter files remains open.

## Core claims

- The letters to Timothy and Titus are Pastoral Epistles: written by Paul to young converts established as pastors, yet the message is for churches and for orderly procedure in local and visible assemblies. [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#PASTORAL EPISTLES|McGee, Pastoral Epistles intro]]
- 1 Timothy’s theme is government and order in the local church, in contrast to Ephesians’ focus on the church as Christ’s invisible body; key verses are 1 Timothy 1:3 and 3:15. [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#1 TIMOTHY|McGee on 1 Timothy]]
- McGee dates 1 Timothy about A.D. 64 (interval after release from Rome, possibly from Macedonia, Timothy left at Ephesus); 2 Timothy about A.D. 67 as Paul’s swan song before death in Rome. [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#1 TIMOTHY|McGee on 1 Timothy date]] [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#2 TIMOTHY|McGee on 2 Timothy date]]
- Elder and bishop are the same person: *presbuteros* names the person, *episkopos* the office; qualifications emphasize character, household management, and aptitude to teach (1 Timothy 3; Titus 1). [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#III. Officers in the churches — Chapter 3|McGee on 1 Timothy 3]] [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#B. An orderly church must have ordained elders who meet prescribed requirements, vv. 5–9|McGee on Titus 1]]
- Apostasy is total, deliberate desertion of the faith by those who once knew gospel truth—not ignorance; 2 Timothy forecasts end-time church apostasy while the invisible church goes on to glory. [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#REMARKS:|McGee on apostasy]] [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#IV. Apostasy in the churches — Chapter 4|McGee on 1 Timothy 4]]
- In days of apostasy the Scriptures are the believer’s only source of help: every passage is God-breathed and profitable for teaching, conviction, setting right, and discipline (2 Timothy 3:16–17). [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#B. Authority of Scriptures in the last days, Chapter 3:10–17|McGee on 2 Timothy 3]]
- Titus presents the ideal New Testament church: orderly organization, sound doctrine, pure life, and readiness for every good work; the blessed hope of Christ’s appearing still grips Paul late in life (Titus 2:13). [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#TITUS|McGee on Titus]]
- Philemon’s primary purpose is to reveal Christ’s love in substitution and imputation (“put that on mine account”; receive him as myself); its practical purpose is brotherly love across master–slave classes. [[raw/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2-timothy-titus-philemon#PHILEMON|McGee on Philemon]]

## Agreements and tensions

- McGee agrees with Henry and the vault’s Pastoral Ministry concept that local-church order, elder character, and feeding under Christ are central; he adds a sharper local-vs-invisible church contrast and a dispensational reading of end-time apostasy and the Rapture (e.g. 2 Timothy 4:1, 8; Titus 2:13).
- On women in public prayer and teaching (1 Timothy 2), McGee’s restrictions (order, headship, no public tongues; context of mystery-religion excesses) should be held beside other sources rather than treated as the vault’s sole word.
- Elder/bishop identity is McGee’s stated position; he notes some good men disagree.
- Corpus is single-author outline notes for radio listeners, not full critical commentary. Cleaned text remains in `raw/` until chapter-level files are promoted under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/`.
