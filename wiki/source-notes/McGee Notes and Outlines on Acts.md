# McGee Notes and Outlines on Acts

## Summary

Intentional ingest of **J. Vernon McGee, Thru the Bible Notes & Outlines** on Acts (immutable PDF under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/`; original raw path `raw/Acts.pdf`). McGee treats Acts as the “5th Gospel,” continuation of Luke, and the indispensable bridge between Gospels and Epistles—the inspired record of the church’s beginnings (spiritual body vs Genesis’ physical universe). Writer is Dr. Luke (Acts 1:1); date about A.D. 63; span about 30 years. Key verse is Acts 1:8. The book’s proper sense is “the Lord Jesus Christ at work by the Holy Spirit through the apostles.” Structure follows 1:8 geography: Jerusalem (chs. 1–7), Judea and Samaria (8–12), uttermost part of the earth (13–28). High-quality pdf-inspector extract (40 pages) supports book-level synthesis; chapter files under a McGee commentary tree are not yet promoted. Comment detail through ch. 23 is full; chs. 24–28 are covered primarily from the outline and closing program (Felix, Festus, Agrippa, storm/shipwreck, Rome preaching to Gentiles).

## Core claims

- Acts is sometimes called the 5th Gospel, a continuation of Luke; it gathers the Gospels’ last notes (resurrection, ascension, promise of the Spirit, promise of the Second Coming), confirms the missionary commission, and furnishes a ladder for the Epistles—a bridge without which the NT has a “great, yawning gap” (Howson). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee Notes & Outlines on Acts]]
- Writer: Dr. Luke (Acts 1:1); Sir William Ramsay ranks Luke among the greatest historians. Date about A.D. 63; covers approximately 30 years; origin of the spiritual body as Genesis records the physical universe. Key verse: Acts 1:8. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee on writer, date, key verse]]
- Special features: prominence of the Lord Jesus Christ, the Holy Spirit (age of the Spirit; indwelling, 1 Cor 6:19), power of the church, church visible and invisible, places (Jerusalem to Rome), persons (110 named), resurrection as center of gospel preaching, Peter in the first section and Paul in the last. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee special features]]
- Title problem resolved by Acts 1:1–2: “The Lord Jesus Christ at work by the Holy Spirit through the apostles” (human instrumentalities)—not merely “Acts of the Apostles.” [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee on title]]
- Outline I (chs. 1–7, Jerusalem): preparation for the Spirit (ch. 1); Pentecost (ch. 2); first miracle and Peter’s second sermon (ch. 3); first persecution (ch. 4); Ananias and Sapphira and second persecution (ch. 5); deacons and Stephen (ch. 6); Stephen’s address and martyrdom (ch. 7). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee outline I]]
- Outline II (chs. 8–12, Judea and Samaria): Ethiopian eunuch (Ham), Saul of Tarsus (Shem), Cornelius (Japheth); Peter defends Gentile ministry and Antioch; death of James and arrest of Peter. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee outline II]]
- Outline III (chs. 13–28, uttermost part): three missionary journeys; Jerusalem council; defenses before mob, Sanhedrin, Felix, Festus, Agrippa; storm and shipwreck; arrival in Rome, last seen preaching to Gentiles. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee outline III]]
- Chapter 1: Jesus continues ministry after resurrection from a different position (cf. 2:33); ten recorded appearances; kingdom of God includes church purpose and re-establishment of David’s house (15:14–17); water baptism is ritual, Spirit baptism real; kingdom will be restored to Israel—times and seasons not given to man; 1:8 is personal command to each believer, not only corporate; ascension cloud is Shekinah glory; “this same Jesus” returns bodily to the same place (Zech 14:4); Matthias elected without Spirit guidance—Holy Spirit later ignores him; McGee holds Saul of Tarsus as Judas’s true successor (Gal 1:1). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee on Acts 1]]
- Chapter 2 (Pentecost as “Bethlehem of the Holy Spirit”): feast depicts beginning of the church (none five minutes before; church five minutes after); Spirit baptizes into Christ’s body (1 Cor 12:12–13); phenomena “like” wind and “as of” fire (not baptism of fire/judgment); filling follows regenerating, indwelling, sealing, baptizing; tongues were real polyglot languages of the Empire, not unknown tongues; Joel’s “this is that” is similarity, not full fulfillment (all flesh, heavenly signs, day of the Lord not yet); emphasis is Christ and resurrection, not tongues; local-church marks at 2:42; early church little organization, great power, love, and joy. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee on Acts 2]]
- Chapters 3–5: first miracle and last national call; persecution for preaching resurrection; Jesus the only way and name of power; church prays for courage, not cessation of persecution (Ps 2); Ananias and Sapphira lie to the Spirit—sin unto death (1 John 5:16); holiness of life in the early church. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee on Acts 3–5]]
- Chapters 6–7: deacon qualifications (honest report, full of the Spirit, wisdom, conviction, faith); Stephen’s Israel-history defense and martyrdom; Savior standing to receive first martyr; Saul prepared by Stephen’s witness; “fell asleep” = body sleep awaiting Rapture. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee on Acts 6–7]]
- Chapter 8: Simon the sorcerer as first religious racketeer—outward profession without saving faith or Spirit baptism; Ethiopian eunuch contrasts—Spirit-led Philip, Isaiah 53, heart belief and baptism. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee on Acts 8]]
- Chapter 9: Saul’s conversion the greatest event from Pentecost to the Reformation; “Who art thou, Lord?” and “Lord, what wilt thou have me to do?”; called to Gentile witness and suffering; Barnabas sponsors him at Jerusalem; Peter heals Aeneas and raises Tabitha. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee on Acts 9]]
- Chapter 10–11: Cornelius devout yet unsaved—works do not make a Christian; Gentile Pentecost; tongues as evidence to Jewish apostles that God saves Gentiles; three representative conversions (Ham/Shem/Japheth) each requiring Holy Spirit, Word of God, and man of God; Antioch Gentile center; name “Christian” at Antioch. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee on Acts 10–11]]
- Chapter 12: sovereign contrast—James executed, Peter delivered; “Easter” should be Passover; church prays with small faith; Herod’s pride judged. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee on Acts 12]]
- Chapters 13–14: Antioch (not Jerusalem) is the missionary church; Spirit chooses Barnabas and Saul; Saul becomes Paul; Pisidian Antioch sermon centers death and resurrection; Lystra healing then stoning—McGee judges Paul dead and raised, linking 2 Cor 12:1–9. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee on Acts 13–14]]
- Chapter 15: Jerusalem council—must Gentiles keep Mosaic Law? Peter: Gentiles saved as at Pentecost without Law; Israel never kept the Law (v. 10); James: God takes out of the Gentiles a people for His name now; “after this” and rebuilding David’s house = after the church, Second Coming (Rev 19); Gentiles not under Mosaic demands but exercise courtesy (idols, fornication); sharp Paul–Barnabas split over Mark. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee on Acts 15]]
- Chapters 16–18: Spirit forbids Asia and Bithynia; Macedonian vision; Luke joins (“we”); Lydia opens Europe; Philippian jailer—“Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ”; household believes separately; Athens Mars’ Hill: God as Creator, Redeemer, Judge—not a failure (converts, v. 34); Corinth sin-city; Gallio refuses religious case; Apollos knows only John’s baptism until Aquila and Priscilla. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee on Acts 16–18]]
- Chapters 19–20: Ephesus—“Did ye receive the Holy Spirit when ye believed?”; Tyrannus school two years; Demetrius silversmiths and Diana; Miletus elders farewell; false teachers will enter Ephesus (v. 29). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee on Acts 19–20]]
- Chapter 21: McGee holds Paul in the will of God going to Jerusalem and taking a Jewish vow—grace frees Gentiles from Law yet permits Jews to keep precepts when not trusted for salvation (1 Cor 7:17–18; 9:19–23); arrest saves Paul from death. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee on Acts 21]]
- Chapters 22–23: defense in Hebrew; Roman citizenship; Sanhedrin split on resurrection; Lord encourages Paul at night (23:11)—confirms he is not out of God’s will; plot of forty; sent to Caesarea. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee on Acts 22–23]]
- Chapters 24–28 (outline program): Paul before Felix, Festus, and Agrippa; voyage to Rome via storm and shipwreck; arrives in Rome, last seen preaching to Gentiles—gospel reaches the empire’s center. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Acts.pdf|McGee outline on Acts 24–28]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Agrees with [[wiki/source-notes/Matthew Henry Concise on Acts|Matthew Henry Concise on Acts]] atlas that Acts traces Spirit-empowered witness from Jerusalem outward, centers resurrection preaching, records Saul’s conversion, and settles Gentile liberty at the Jerusalem council.
- Adds sharper frames useful to [[wiki/concepts/Holy Spirit|Holy Spirit]], [[wiki/concepts/Church|Church]], [[wiki/concepts/Salvation|Salvation]], [[wiki/concepts/Grace|Grace]], and [[wiki/concepts/Resurrection|Resurrection]]: Acts 1:8 personal commission; Pentecost as church origin and Spirit baptism into the body; Joel partial-similarity reading; three-factor conversion pattern; law vs grace council with dispensational “after this” church/kingdom sequence; Paul-in-will-of-God on the Jerusalem vow.
- Dispensational notes (Matthias vs Paul as twelfth apostle; church not in prophecy; Davidic house rebuilt after church removal; Paul raised at Lystra; body-sleep/Rapture at Stephen’s death) are single-author outline readings—hold beside Henry and other sources, not as the vault’s sole word.
- Tongues as known languages and anti-unknown-tongue lean matches McGee’s 1 Corinthians outline; charismatic continuity questions remain open.
- Comment extract is richest through ch. 23; chs. 24–28 should be thickened if a fuller extract slice or chapter split becomes available.
- Corpus is a book-level PDF under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/`; optional split into chapter files remains open.
