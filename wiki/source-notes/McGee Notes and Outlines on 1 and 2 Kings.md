# McGee Notes and Outlines on 1 and 2 Kings

## Summary

Intentional ingest of **J. Vernon McGee, Thru the Bible Notes & Outlines** on 1 & 2 Kings (immutable PDF under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/`; original raw path `raw/1-2_Kings.pdf`). McGee treats Kings as the second of three double books (originally one book, divided by the Septuagint), continuing Samuel’s narrative from David and Solomon’s height through division to the captivities of Israel and Judah. Theme: the kingdom standard “as David his father” (repeated nine times in 1 Kings)—a human standard man failed to attain. First Kings records the *division* of the kingdom; Second Kings the *collapse*. Key verses: 2 Kings 17:22–23 and 25:21. Moral teaching: man’s inability to rule himself and the world; the throne on earth must be in tune with the throne in heaven. High-quality firecrawl-parse extract (32 pages) supports book-level synthesis; chapter files under a McGee commentary tree are not yet promoted.

## Core claims

- Writer unknown; written while the first temple still stood (1 Kings 8:8); Jeremiah is the traditional writer; modern scholarship assigns authorship to “the prophets.” [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf|McGee Notes & Outlines on 1–2 Kings]]
- Features: practically all rulers evil; God’s patience; mothers’ names of good and bad kings; grace in revival when king and people turn; prominence of the prophet and insignificance of the priest; long delay before captivity; man’s total inability to rule for God; wicked kings with godly sons and good kings with wicked sons. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf|McGee on Kings features]]
- Outline: I. Death of David (1 Kings 1–2); II. Glory of Solomon’s reign (3–11)—wisdom, temple, fame, shame and death; III. Division of the kingdom (1 Kings 12 – 2 Kings 16); IV. Captivity of Israel by Assyria (2 Kings 17); V. Decline and captivity of Judah by Babylon (2 Kings 18–25). Chronological table of divided-kingdom kings, characters, and prophets supplied for study. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf|McGee Kings outline]]
- David’s legacy to Solomon includes transfer of leadership to Judah and the house of David; Jerusalem as capital and religious center; stamp-out of idolatry and universal Jehovah worship; conquests and expanded borders; relatively political harem; psalmist ministry; temple planning; uniting the tribes; national power and peace. David’s charge (2:2–3) reveals little confidence in a palace-reared successor and something of David’s own rugged character. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf|McGee on David’s death and legacy]]
- Solomon prayed for wisdom—evidently political wisdom more than spiritual discernment—granted and shown in the two harlots’ case. Peace and prosperity mark his reign as prince of peace vs David man of war. Marriage to Pharaoh’s daughter and failure to remove idolatry show early compromise (3:3). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf|McGee on Solomon’s wisdom]]
- Temple patterned after the tabernacle but about twice as large, more ornate; McGee reads spiritual deterioration (windows substitute natural light for lampstand light speaking of Christ; measured cherubim vs unmeasured tabernacle cherubim speaking of Christ’s deity). Built without hammer/tool sound on site (6:7); seven years; estimated cost enormous. Glory fills the house after the ark is installed (8:10–11). Dedication prayer shows no primitive view that God dwells in a house (8:27). Temple is properly David’s—he gathered materials (1 Chronicles 22). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf|McGee on temple building and dedication]]
- Queen of Sheba visit: Solomon succeeded for a time as witness; Israel’s commission was nations coming to Jerusalem, not going out (contrast church’s Great Commission). “Ascent” (10:5) should be “burnt offering”—the offering that speaks of Christ; without shedding of blood no remission. Luxury items (apes, peacocks, gold, ivory) signal frivolous waste of witness energy. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf|McGee on Sheba and fame]]
- Solomon is “the most colossal failure on the pages of Scripture”; much given, much required. Harem of 1000 is licentious vs David’s largely political marriages. Lord angry; kingdom to be divided after his death for David’s sake. Jeroboam promoted then plots per Ahijah; flees to Egypt. Solomon reigns 40 years. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf|McGee on Solomon’s shame]]
- Division under Rehoboam’s harsh tax answer; Jeroboam sets golden calves at Bethel and Dan—political and religious split. No good king among Israel’s 19; Judah 20 kings with only 8 good (5 outstanding). David remains the standard of excellence; Jeroboam the standard of evil for the north. Asa leads first southern revival (half chapter here; three in Chronicles). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf|McGee on division and kings]]
- Omri builds Samaria; Ahab worse than father, marries Jezebel (Baal high priest’s daughter). Elijah announces drought, learns dependence at Cherith, Zarephath barrel, and widow’s dead son. Carmel contest: “One with God is a majority”; twelve-stone altar; brief effective prayer; Baal prophets slain; rain announced. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf|McGee on Ahab and Elijah]]
- Elijah under juniper tree: man of like passions; eyes off the Lord; nervous exhaustion; God gives food, sleep, then wind/earthquake/fire (God not in them) and still small voice (God in it); returns and calls Elisha. Naboth’s vineyard: Jezebel’s plot; Elijah’s judgment; Galatians 6:7 cited; delayed but not revoked sentence after Ahab’s partial repentance. Micaiah vs false prophets; Ahab dies by random arrow; dogs lick blood per Elijah. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf|McGee on Elijah’s weakness and Ahab’s end]]
- 2 Kings: king and prophet replace priest as chief instruments of communication. Ahaziah inquires of Baal-zebub; Elijah’s last public act and fire on captains. Elijah translated; Elisha double portion if he witnesses; mantle at Jordan. “Little children” who mock (2:23) are not nursery toddlers—*nahar/naar* used of adults; ridicule of translation is blasphemy against a great doctrine (cf. 2 Peter 3:3–4); God sends bears. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf|McGee on Elijah’s exit and Elisha]]
- Elisha’s miracles more extensive than Elijah’s: oil for widow; Shunammite son raised; poison stew; feeding 100; Naaman washed seven times in Jordan (pride broken); Gehazi leprous for greed; floating axe head—stick in waters of death as cross typology for recovering lost man; opened eyes to horses and chariots of fire; Syrian siege and four lepers. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf|McGee on Elisha’s ministry]]
- Jehu anointed; slays Joram, Ahaziah, Jezebel (Elijah’s word fulfilled); exterminates Ahab’s house and Baal worshippers but returns only to Jeroboam’s calf level—God still rewards limited obedience. Athaliah’s massacre of David’s line; Joash hidden and crowned; Jehoiada restores Jehovah worship. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf|McGee on Jehu and Athaliah]]
- Captivity of Israel (ch. 17): disobeyed God (v. 13), doubted God (v. 14), defied God (v. 15)—including refusal of sabbatic years (linked to 2 Chronicles 36:21). Story of the nation is the story of every individual (Romans 1; Acts 7:51; Hebrews 12). First mention of Samaritans via foreign colonists (v. 29). “Jews” first used in Bible at 16:6. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf|McGee on Israel’s captivity]]
- Hezekiah exceptionally good; revival; Assyrian threat; letter spread before the Lord; 185,000 destroyed. Recovery after appointed death leads to three foolish acts: showing treasures to Babylon, fathering Manasseh (worst king), pride (2 Chronicles 32:25). Manasseh surpasses Ahab; Amon evil; Josiah one of the best after Solomon—revival steps: temple repaired, return to Word, conviction of sin, put away idolatry and immorality, reinstituted Passover, further reformation. Judah falls under Nebuchadnezzar for Manasseh’s sins; temple burned; Gedaliah murdered; Jehoiachin later elevated in Babylon. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf|McGee on Judah’s decline and exile]]
- Jonah son of Amittai historically confirmed as prophet in Jeroboam II’s border restoration (14:25). Uzziah good but leprous for priestly intrusion (cross-ref 2 Chronicles 26). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/1-2_Kings.pdf|McGee on later kings]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Agrees with Henry Concise/Complete Kings atlas that temple dedication, Solomon’s fall, Carmel, captivity reasons, Elijah–Elisha succession, and Josiah’s reform are central pastoral hubs.
- Pairs with [[wiki/source-notes/McGee Notes and Outlines on 1 and 2 Chronicles|McGee on 1–2 Chronicles]]: Kings = throne/political/man’s viewpoint; Chronicles = altar/religious/God’s viewpoint—same ground, different emphasis. Temple “David’s not Solomon’s,” Sheba/burnt-offering witness, and reformation-as-return-to-Word themes recur across both outlines.
- Adds sharp moral frame (inability to rule for God; David standard repeated nine times), prophet-over-priest prominence, Elijah’s still-small-voice psychology, Elisha axe-head cross typology, and three-part captivity etiology (disobey/doubt/defy).
- Dispensational and typology notes (tabernacle light/cherubim vs temple windows; axe-head stick = cross; Israel witness vs church go; Hezekiah should have died at appointed time) are McGee radio-outline readings—hold beside Henry and other sources, not as the vault’s sole word.
- Chronological kings table and recommended-books list are Thru the Bible study aids, not independent critical apparatus.
- Corpus is a book-level PDF under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/`; optional chapter split remains open.
