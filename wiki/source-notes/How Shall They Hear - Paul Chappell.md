# How Shall They Hear — Paul Chappell

## Summary

Pastor Paul Chappell (Lancaster Baptist Church) continues a verse-by-verse Romans series in the “No Condemnation” set with an evening message on Romans 10:14–17 titled *How Shall They Hear*. From the “whosoever shall call” of verse 13 he argues that a universal invitation requires universal proclamation: salvation has been *provided* in Christ’s blood, but that is not the same as salvation having been *offered* wherever people have never heard. Three questions structure the sermon—(1) how shall they hear without a preacher, (2) how shall they preach except they be sent, (3) how shall they be saved—answering that hearers need gospel messengers, churches must send laborers (Acts 13 pattern), and faith comes by hearing the word of God, though not all obey. Local applications include soulwinning, bus ministry, Bible college, prayer for God to raise preachers, and challenging young people to consider ministry rather than treating a “fallback” career as primary. The sermon explicitly frames God as knowing who will be saved while denying a God who “selects some for heaven and damns others to hell,” and presses “whosoever will” language throughout.

## Core claims

- “Whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved” (Rom 10:13; Joel 2:32) is a universal invitation: race, culture, and past sin do not bar those who will call on Christ. [[sources/transcripts/How Shall They Hear - Paul Chappell|Source]]
- Salvation provided in Christ’s death is not identical to salvation offered: provision can be sufficient while many still never hear the gospel; the problem is then proclamation, not an insufficient cross. [[sources/transcripts/How Shall They Hear - Paul Chappell|Source]]
- The chain of Romans 10:14 requires hearing and a preacher (soulwinner, missionary, or other gospel messenger): people must hear in order to believe, and believe in order to call. [[sources/transcripts/How Shall They Hear - Paul Chappell|Source]]
- “How shall they preach except they be sent?” calls churches to send laborers (illustrated from Acts 13:2–3), not only to affirm that the lost may come. [[sources/transcripts/How Shall They Hear - Paul Chappell|Source]]
- Faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God (Rom 10:17); not all obey the gospel, so salvation is by a believing heart hearing the word, not by universal response. [[sources/transcripts/How Shall They Hear - Paul Chappell|Source]]
- Believers are “sent ones” under Christ’s commission (including John 20:21); the local church’s ministries (soulwinning, buses, school/college, missionary support) exist so that “whosoever” may hear. [[sources/transcripts/How Shall They Hear - Paul Chappell|Source]]
- The church should pray for and challenge young people toward preaching and missions; a thick “churches needing pastors” need and declining vocational surrender are pressed as pastoral urgency, with Christ himself presented as the true “fallback” for those called. [[sources/transcripts/How Shall They Hear - Paul Chappell|Source]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Agrees with existing [[wiki/passages/Romans 10|Romans 10]], [[wiki/concepts/Faith|Faith]], [[wiki/concepts/Word of God|Word of God]], and [[wiki/concepts/Salvation|Salvation]] synthesis that faith comes by hearing the word and that the same Lord is rich to all who call.
- Agrees with [[wiki/concepts/Homiletics|Homiletics]] / [[wiki/concepts/Christ-Centered Ministry|Christ-Centered Ministry]] that the world needs the preached word and that call to ministry is a serious favor to pursue—not institutional self-promotion alone.
- **Tension (election / “whosoever” framing):** Chappell denies serving “a God who selects some for heaven and damns others to hell,” while affirming God’s exhaustive knowledge of destinies. That Independent Baptist free-will framing sits in tension with stronger particular-election readings elsewhere in the vault’s Reformed-leaning commentary spine; keep it attributed to this source, not as vault consensus.
- **Tension (provision vs offer):** The provision/proclamation distinction is pastoral and missional; it should not be flattened into a claim that the atonement’s nature is settled only by this sermon’s language.
- Cultural setting: large Independent Baptist church with Bible college, soulwinning program, and global missions emphasis; illustrations (Iran underground church, Indonesia missionary, retiring pastors, Maine pulpit need) are local and contemporary, not timeless dogma.
