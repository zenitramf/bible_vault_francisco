# McGee Notes and Outlines on Amos and Obadiah

## Summary

Intentional ingest of **J. Vernon McGee, Thru the Bible Notes & Outlines** on Amos and Obadiah (immutable PDF under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/`; original raw path `raw/Amos-Obadiah.pdf`, briefly mis-staged under `sources/personal-notes/ingest/` then promoted). High-quality pdf-inspector extract (15 pages, text-based) supports book-level synthesis. McGee presents Amos as a lay herdsman and sycamore-fruit gatherer from Tekoa who prophesied primarily to the northern kingdom under Jeroboam II (contemporary of Hosea, Isaiah, Micah), “two years before the earthquake” (Amos 1:1; cf. Zechariah 14:5). Theme: God rules the world; nations are responsible according to the light they have; the final test is Amos 3:3—“Can two walk together, except they be agreed?” In prosperity he announces judgment on luxury and immorality. Outline: judgment on surrounding nations (1:1–2:3), on Judah and Israel (2:4–6:14), and visions of the future including dispersion and millennial restoration (chs. 7–9). Obadiah (“Servant of Jehovah”) is the shortest OT book (21 verses), a prophecy of judgment against Edom; McGee keys the book to pride (v. 3) as the magnified flaw of Esau, with poetic justice (*lex talionis*) and Israel’s restoration climaxing “And the kingdom shall be the Lord’s” (v. 21).

## Core claims

- Amos was not a school-of-the-prophets graduate but a layman—herdsman and gatherer of sycamore fruit (Amos 1:1; 7:14–15), native of Tekoa about twelve miles south of Jerusalem; though born in Judea, his messages were primarily to northern Israel and to the world in general. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Amos-Obadiah.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on Amos]]
- Ministry during Jeroboam II (Israel) and Uzziah (Judah); contemporary of Hosea in Israel and Isaiah and Micah in Judah; dated “two years before the earthquake” (Amos 1:1), later recalled by Zechariah (Zechariah 14:5). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Amos-Obadiah.pdf.extract.md|McGee on Amos time]]
- Theme: God as ruler of the world; all nations responsible to Him according to the light they have; final test Amos 3:3 agreement-with-God; in a day of prosperity he pronounces punishment—judgment awaits nations living in luxury and immorality. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Amos-Obadiah.pdf.extract.md|McGee on Amos theme]]
- Outline I (1:1–2:3): judgments on Syria (cruelty), Philistia (making slaves), Phoenicia/Tyre (breaking treaty, selling slaves), Edom (revengeful spirit), Ammon (violent crimes), Moab (injustice beyond death); “three transgressions” = ungodliness in worst form (Luther); fulfillments noted via 2 Kings and Nebuchadnezzar/Alexander. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Amos-Obadiah.pdf.extract.md|McGee Amos outline I]]
- Outline II (2:4–6:14): Judah judged for despising the Law (had the Law; nations without it not judged by it); Israel for immorality and blasphemy like the Amorites; ch. 3—privilege creates responsibility, the higher the blessing the greater the punishment (3:1–2 chosen purpose; 3:3 principle of judgment); ch. 4 past punishments ignored; ch. 5 future punishment and plea to seek the Lord; day of the LORD opens with Great Tribulation (darkness, not light—5:18, 20); empty religious forms plus idolatry; Assyrian captivity (5:27); ch. 6 present admonition—affluence sins of gluttony, heathen music, drunkenness; God hates a godless nation. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Amos-Obadiah.pdf.extract.md|McGee Amos outline II]]
- Outline III visions (chs. 7–9): grasshoppers and fire withdrawn in mercy; plumbline means imminent action (cf. Isaiah 28:17; Jeremiah 31:38–39; Zechariah 2:1–2); historic interlude—Amaziah priest of Baal at Bethel charges Amos before Jeroboam; Amos answers he is herdsman called of God and answers with fearful prophecy; summer fruit = perishable goodness ripe for judgment; famine of the word (8:11) and “wandering Jew” (8:12); worldwide dispersion without utter destruction (9:8–10); worldwide regathering and kingdom restoration (9:11–15), quoted by James at the Jerusalem council (Acts 15:16–17), yet to be fulfilled as millennial kingdom. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Amos-Obadiah.pdf.extract.md|McGee Amos visions]]
- Obadiah means Servant of Jehovah; one of four prophets of whom almost nothing is known (with Habakkuk, Haggai, Malachi); name alone and brief prophecy known (Pusey). Date disputed (887 B.C. Jehoram/Athaliah; Pusey under Jehoshaphat; Farrar/Moorehead ~587 B.C. contemporary of Jeremiah); hinges on whether v. 11 is historical or prophetical—natural reading historical yields late date subsequent to Babylonian captivity. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Amos-Obadiah.pdf.extract.md|McGee on Obadiah writer/date]]
- Key: Edom—“How are the things of Esau searched out!” (Obadiah 1:6); Ginsburg: “How are the things of Esau stripped bare!” Feature: shortest OT book, blunt “Thus saith the Lord GOD concerning Edom”—judgment prophecy still primary, pertinent, practical. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Amos-Obadiah.pdf.extract.md|McGee Obadiah key]]
- Background: Edomites from Esau, Israelites from Jacob (Genesis 25:24–34); Esau despised birthright (relationship with God for a bowl of soup); Malachi 1:2–3 “I loved Jacob, and I hated Esau” explained in Obadiah—God did not hate Esau at the beginning, but when the nation magnified pride: “The pride of thine heart hath deceived thee” (v. 3); Petra’s impregnable rose-rock fortress bred false security and bowing God out of civilization—what God hates is man saying “I don’t need God.” [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Amos-Obadiah.pdf.extract.md|McGee on Edom pride]]
- Obadiah outline: I. Edom—destruction (vv. 1–16): charge (1–9), crime against brother Jacob (10–14), catastrophe with *lex talionis* (15–16); II. Israel—restoration (vv. 17–21): condition (17), calling (18), consummation—“And the kingdom shall be the Lord’s” (19–21). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/Amos-Obadiah.pdf.extract.md|McGee Obadiah outline]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Agrees with Henry Concise/Complete Amos and Obadiah atlas on nations’ judgment, Israel’s privilege-and-guilt, day of the LORD darkness, summer-fruit ripeness, famine of the word, Edom’s enmity and Jacob’s restoration ([[wiki/source-notes/Matthew Henry Concise on Amos|Matthew Henry Concise on Amos]]; [[wiki/source-notes/Matthew Henry Concise on Obadiah|Matthew Henry Concise on Obadiah]]).
- Adds radio-outline frames useful to [[wiki/concepts/Justice|Justice]] (nations measured by light; privilege creates responsibility), [[wiki/concepts/Prophecy|Prophecy]] (layman called of God vs Amaziah/Baal establishment), [[wiki/concepts/Worship|Worship]] (forms without agreement with God; golden-calf sarcasm), [[wiki/concepts/Word of God|Word of God]] (Judah despised the Law; famine of hearing the words of the LORD), and [[wiki/concepts/Hope|Hope]] (Amos 9:11–15 millennial restoration; Obadiah kingdom the Lord’s).
- Dispensational readings—day of the LORD opening with Great Tribulation (Amos 5), “wandering Jew,” and Acts 15:16–17 as yet-future millennial fulfillment rather than (only) church-age application—are McGee’s outline emphases; hold beside Henry’s gospel-blessing reading of Amos 9:11–15, not as the vault’s sole eschatology.
- Obadiah date remains disputed in the notes themselves; pride-as-key and *lex talionis* structure are the durable theological claims.
- Dual-book PDF promoted beside other McGee PDFs under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/` (evidence wikilinks target the extract sidecar).
