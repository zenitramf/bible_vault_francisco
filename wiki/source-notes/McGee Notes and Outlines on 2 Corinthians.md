# McGee Notes and Outlines on 2 Corinthians

## Summary

Intentional ingest of **J. Vernon McGee, Thru the Bible Notes & Outlines** on 2 Corinthians (immutable PDF under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/`; original raw path `raw/2_Corinthians.pdf`). McGee frames the letter as written from Philippi shortly after 1 Corinthians, after Titus brought good news that the breach with Corinth was healed. He contrasts 1 Corinthians (conditions and *corrections* in the church) with 2 Corinthians (conditions of the *ministry* within the church). The book is hard to outline and full of personal detail; each chapter carries a minor theme, often in a striking verse. High-level outline: **I. Comfort of God** (chs. 1–7, Christian living); **II. Collection** for poor saints at Jerusalem (chs. 8–9, Christian giving); **III. Calling** of the apostle Paul (chs. 10–13, Christian guarding). High-quality firecrawl-parse extract (8 pages) supports book-level synthesis.

## Core claims

- Occasion: after 1 Corinthians from Ephesus (grave danger, 2 Cor 1:8), Paul wrote 2 Corinthians from Philippi; Titus had been sent to Corinth; Paul and Timothy waited at Troas then went to Philippi where Titus brought good news (2:12–13; 7:5–11); the breach was healed. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee Notes & Outlines on 2 Corinthians]]
- 1 Corinthians deals with conditions and corrections in the church; 2 Corinthians deals with conditions of the ministry within the church. The epistle is less organized than Paul’s other letters but contains more personal details. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on character of 2 Corinthians]]
- Outline triad: Comfort of God (chs. 1–7, Christian living); Collection for poor saints (chs. 8–9, Christian giving); Calling of the apostle Paul (chs. 10–13, Christian guarding). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee outline]]
- Ch. 1: “Comfort”/“consolation” nine times in five verses—not sentimental but sustaining help, the same word used for the Holy Spirit the Comforter. God comforted Paul through a crisis in Ephesus (probably sickness unto death, v. 9) so he could comfort others. Holy Spirit confirms, anoints, seals, and is the earnest/pledge (vv. 21–22; cf. Eph 4:30). Father true, Son absolute/positive, Spirit dwelling within. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 2 Corinthians 1]]
- Ch. 2: The sinning saint of 1 Corinthians 5 had repented; Paul urges restoration; refusal gives Satan an advantage (v. 11). Secondary theme: God always causes triumph (v. 14); the believer is a sweet savor to lost and saved; business is to declare the gospel accurately—responsibility for results rests on the hearer. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 2 Corinthians 2]]
- Ch. 3: Converts become the gospel to the unsaved. Ministry of the gospel is more glorious than Moses’; veil is over hearts that follow the Law; those led by the Spirit are not under the Law (v. 17). V. 18 great theme: only the Spirit develops Christian character; “changed” is *metamorphosis* (same word as Christ’s transfiguration)—ultimate goal of humanity seen in the Transfiguration. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 2 Corinthians 3]]
- Ch. 4: Suffering tests genuineness of ministry; contrasts show suffering is not defeat. Satan as god of this world blinds men at one point—the gospel; lost world is a prison with only one way out (Christ, John 14:6). “Earthen vessels” (*ostrakinos*, clay pitchers) like Gideon’s 300—vessels must be broken for light to shine. Greek word-studies on pressed/perplexed/persecuted. Present suffering is light versus eternal weight of glory; unseen things are eternal. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 2 Corinthians 4]]
- Ch. 5: Body is a tent; death is departure home to be with the Lord. Believers appear at the *bema* of Christ for reward or not—works judged. Secondary (major) theme is reconciliation: God is already reconciled in Christ; the gospel calls us to accept God’s message and method, not to reconcile God by our works (vv. 14–21). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 2 Corinthians 5]]
- Ch. 6: Nineteen trying experiences of ministry; nine life-covering contrasts; personal appeal to break cleanly with idolatry. [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 2 Corinthians 6]]
- Ch. 7: Comfort of God again (vv. 4, 6, 7, 13). V. 10 is God’s definition of repentance—a change of mind; in turning to Christ by faith, sinners turn from sin (repentance for salvation). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 2 Corinthians 7]]
- Chs. 8–9 (Christian giving): Giving is a grace; God wants the person before the gift—Macedonians first gave themselves (8:5). Principles not rules; tithe not demanded; Christ gave all, not a tenth (8:9). Give to a specific cause (poor saints in Jerusalem), reputable messengers (Titus and company), and in reality not mere promise. Give generously, not grudgingly but hilariously (9:7); God gives grace to give; give bountifully (probably more than a tithe); never out-give God (9:15). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 2 Corinthians 8–9]]
- Ch. 10: Warfare is spiritual; success not measured by numbers, money, or outward growth. Weapons are secret and mighty—Word of God the hush-hush weapon, Holy Spirit the General, prayer the ammunition. Warriors are successful, not victorious; victory is Christ’s and we enter into it (cf. 2:14). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 2 Corinthians 10]]
- Ch. 11: Very personal vindication; Paul pays his own way. Secondary theme: ministers of Satan are attractive and winsome and teach false doctrine for material benefit. Paul’s life vindicates his ministry (vv. 16–33). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 2 Corinthians 11]]
- Ch. 12: Paul’s third-heaven experience tied to being stoned at Lystra (Acts 14:19). Three heavens: birds, stars, abode of God. God “put a zipper on” Paul’s mouth; thorn in the flesh keeps him humble. The man who went to heaven returns to Corinth in weakness (v. 10). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 2 Corinthians 12]]
- Ch. 13: Third visit to execute apostleship; proof through power of Christ in Paul’s weakness. Believers should take regular inventory whether they are in the faith (v. 5). Declare the Word of God, not defend it (v. 8). Conclusion returns to the comfort of God (vv. 11–14). [[sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/2_Corinthians.pdf.extract.md|McGee on 2 Corinthians 13]]

## Agreements and tensions

- Agrees with Henry Concise/Complete atlas that 2 Corinthians treats comfort in affliction, restoration of the penitent, gospel glory over law, ministry under suffering, reconciliation, generous giving, and defense of Paul’s apostleship ([[wiki/source-notes/Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Corinthians|Matthew Henry Concise on 2 Corinthians]]).
- Adds sharper three-part frame (comfort / collection / calling), Spirit seal-and-earnest tetrad (1:21–22), gospel-as-savor and results-not-our-business emphasis (ch. 2), *metamorphosis*/Transfiguration goal (3:18), clay-vessel and Satan-blinding motifs (ch. 4), God-already-reconciled gospel (ch. 5), repentance-as-change-of-mind (7:10), grace-not-tithe giving principles (chs. 8–9), and spiritual-weapons triad Word/Spirit/prayer (ch. 10).
- Third-heaven identification with Lystra stoning, three-heavens cosmology, and “tithe not demanded / give more than a tithe” pastoral applications are McGee’s radio-outline readings—hold beside Henry and other sources, not as the vault’s sole word.
- Corpus is a book-level PDF under `sources/commentaries_english/mcgee-thru-the-bible/`; optional split into chapter files remains open (same pattern as other McGee notes).
